Part 22 finished the squad with [Park Jae-hwan](/articles/2026-season-review-park-jae-hwan), a center back who fell out of the side in July and has watched since. Somebody made that call, and every other one. This is the review of the man who picks the team, on the same terms as his players: what he has done well, where the same mistakes keep showing up, and what he brings to the last six matches.

## The season in one paragraph

Kim Do-gyun is in his third season at Seoul E-Land and his seventh as a manager, and he has spent all of them chasing the thing this club has never done. He took Suwon FC up in his first season in charge in 2020 and finished fourth in the top flight with them in 2021. He arrived at Mokdong in December 2023, took a club that had never been promoted to third and the promotion playoff in 2024, then to fourth and a league record for goals in 2025, and lost a home semifinal to Seongnam in November. In January, with Ulsan HD wanting him as their first choice and the club refusing to release him, he said there was work he still had to do here. At the league's media day in February he made the target explicit: this year the club must go up, no conditions. On September 4 at Cheongju he became the 23rd manager in K League history to reach 100 wins, in his 247th match, and he spent the press conference talking about the three points. Seoul sit third with six matches to play, one place and one tiebreaker outside the automatic promotion places, on course, by this site's model, for about 60 points, which would be the best return of his three years and still not enough unless Suwon FC slip.

## What he has done well

**He changed the shape, and it worked.** After the 1-2 home defeat to a winless Chungbuk Cheongju in June, the worst result of the season, he said in public that the idea Seoul were preparing for promotion was nonsense on the evidence in front of him, and that he was wondering whether he should carry on as manager. What he did next was not a speech. Before Gimhae in July he told Best Eleven that the first fifteen matches had been played almost entirely off a back three and that the team would be better off simply going to a four. Since that declaration Seoul have taken two points a match against 1.73 before it. The change is defensive: 1.42 goals conceded a match with a three, 0.93 with a four, while the scoring barely moved. He is also not dogmatic about it. He went back to a three at Suwon FC in July when the four had not survived a promotion rival, and he used one again against Daegu in September with Park Chang-hwan suspended, which produced the best win of the season.

**His bench scores.** Sixty-four percent of Seoul's goals have come after halftime. In the nine rounds from Gimhae in July to Seongnam in August, a substitute scored or set up a goal in seven of them. At home to Cheonan in July he sent on Ahn Joo-wan and Lee Ju-hyeok together on 53 minutes and Ahn scored eleven minutes later. At Busan in August he made an attacking change after half an hour, which is rare for any manager, and that substitute crossed for the winner on 84 minutes. At Gimhae a double change on the hour turned 0-0 into 3-1. Korean reports started using a phrase for it, that the manager's substitutions hit the mark, and the site's own match reports counted four goals inside minutes of a change in five rounds.

**He plays the kids, and coaches them.** Ahn Joo-wan joined from a high school in March and made his debut five days later aged 16 years and 11 months, the youngest appearance in the history of the division. He is now also the youngest scorer in K League history and the youngest to score twice in a match. None of that is a token: his first start came in August precisely because Seoul had failed to break down three low blocks in a row and he was the one player who attacked the space behind the line. Ahn's own account of the coaching is the best evidence of intent. He says he barely defended at school because he scored so often, and that here the manager tells him that wherever he plays, if he cannot defend he will not survive as a professional. Baek Ji-woong at 22 has played almost every minute, Cho Jun-hyun and Son Hyuk-chan are in rotation, and the division's rule that ties the number of substitutions to how many under-22 players you use has never once cost Seoul a change.

**He rebuilds players.** One Korean piece in August called him the boss of the rehab factory for what he has done with Park Jae-yong, a striker who had managed one goal in each of the previous two seasons behind foreign forwards and has ten now. The same article has his version of the job, that the first thing is to understand what developing a player actually means, and that a coach cannot pick favorites. Euller turned down the top flight to stay at this club. Carius came back from a torn Achilles and won a match at Jeonnam within three appearances. Min Sung-jun arrived to replace a goalkeeper the fans loved and has played every minute.

**He stayed.** Ulsan HD, one of the biggest clubs in the country, wanted him after the 2025 season and made him their first choice. Seoul E-Land refused to let him go because his contract had time on it, and he chose to recommit rather than push. His line in January was that there was work to do here and he wanted the result called promotion. A manager in the third year of a promotion project with two failures behind him is the most replaceable man at any club. He is the one who stayed by choice.

## Where the same mistakes keep showing up

**He cannot protect a lead.** Seoul have led in four matches they did not win, at Suwon Samsung on the opening day, at home to Busan in March, at home to Cheongju in June, and at Suwon FC in July. That is eleven points dropped from winning positions, and eleven points would have this team clear at the top of the table rather than third on a tiebreaker. Ten of the thirty goals they have conceded arrived in the 75th minute or later, eight of those in the 85th or later, and nine of the ten came when Seoul were level or ahead. His own stated standard, given in July, is that a strong side has the power to score and turn matches around after 80 minutes. Going forward his team meets it: nine goals in the 84th minute or later. Going the other way it does not.

**The low block still beats him.** Against Paju at Mokdong in August, Seoul had 21 shots, 15 on target, 12 corners, hit the woodwork twice and scored once. Against Suwon Samsung in September the same shape produced 18 shots and no goal. August, the month of three draws in four rounds against sides that sat in, is his second worst month of the season. He has named the problem himself, saying before the run-in that Seoul were caught by bottom-half teams too often last season and cannot relax now. Every one of the six remaining opponents is a bottom-half team that will defend deep.

**Set pieces, at both ends.** Seven of the first 25 goals Seoul scored this season came from dead balls, which is a coached strength. The other end is the one thing that has not improved since July. Seoul have conceded from corners at Chungnam Asan, at Seongnam, against Suwon Samsung and against Daegu. He leads on it himself in press conferences, saying they are conceding goals they did not need to concede, and he went into the Daegu match asking four different players to score from set pieces. Instead Osmar nearly put one into his own net.

**Mokdong is the harder ground.** Seoul are better away from home than at their own stadium, 1.92 points a match against 1.77, and four of their six defeats have come in front of their own supporters, including both matches against Suwon Samsung and the Cheongju night in June. Three of the last six are at Mokdong.

**Two rotations went wrong.** Resting players before the summer break against Cheongju produced the defeat that made him question himself in public. Five changes and two debutants at Seongnam in August produced a draw that cost two points in a race decided by two points. The opposing manager said six of the names had surprised his staff. In between, the all-domestic eleven he picked at Jeonnam in May won 1-0 with the players he had rested coming on to decide it, so he can do this well. He has done it badly at least as often.

## His strengths, and what they mean for the last six

**The bench is the plan.** Six matches against deep defenses will be decided in the last half hour, which is exactly where this team scores. He has five substitutions available in almost every match because he starts enough young players to unlock them, and he has Park Jae-yong, Kim Hyun, Gabriel, Kang Hyeon-je and Ahn Joo-wan as attacking changes. No manager in the division has got more out of a bench this season.

**Scoring first is worth more than any tactical point.** Seoul take 2.31 points a match when they score first and 1.45 when they concede first. Five of the six remaining opponents are sides Seoul should lead. The instruction that follows is the one he has already given, that the team has to create more goals, because Suwon FC are five clear on the first tiebreaker and a one-goal win does nothing about that.

**He knows what the number is.** Before the ceremony for his hundredth win he said he had wanted 108 this year, that it had not happened, and that he now wanted at least 106. That is six more wins from six matches, the win-out scenario, and he said it out loud rather than managing expectations. Whatever else is true of him, he is not hiding from the target he set in February.

## The verdict at Round 27

The best three-year run in the club's history belongs to him, and so does the fact that it has produced nothing yet. His squad is the youngest in the division and plays the most attacking football in it. He found the shape that fixed his defense halfway through a promotion race, which is harder than finding it in preseason. He gave a 16-year-old a debut and then the ball in a promotion race, and got three league records back. He also threw away eleven points from winning positions, has no answer yet to a packed penalty area, and concedes from corners in a season where the second tiebreaker may decide who goes up. Nothing about the first 26 matches disqualifies him from being the manager who finally takes Seoul E-Land up. Nothing about them guarantees it either.

**Grade so far: B.** The B is for the mid-season fix, the bench, the teenagers and the 100th win. The grade moves in either direction over six matches, and he would be the first to say that is the only part that counts.
