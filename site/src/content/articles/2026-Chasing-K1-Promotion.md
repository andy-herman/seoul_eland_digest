Two rounds have gone since the last refresh, and the race for second has moved away from Seoul. In Round 31 Seoul lost 4-2 at Yongin while Suwon FC beat Ansan 1-0, which turned a level race into a three-point gap. In Round 32 both won: Seoul came from behind to beat Jeonnam 3-2 at Mokdong with two Euller penalties, and Suwon FC beat Gyeongnam 3-0. Daegu, who drew 2-2 with Suwon Samsung and then won 1-0 at Seongnam, are one point behind Seoul. Suwon Samsung clinch promotion with a draw at home to Yongin on Sunday.

Two clubs go up automatically. Suwon Samsung are 96 percent for the title and above 99 for the top two. Second place is Suwon FC's to lose: the model has them at 86 percent, Seoul at 8 and Daegu at 6. The fight that is genuinely open is for third. Seoul and Daegu are separated by a point, Daegu have scored one more goal, and the model has Seoul finishing third in 43 percent of runs and fourth in 49. Seoul are certain of a playoff place either way. The semi-finals are on December 2 and the final on December 5, and the final's loser then plays K League 1's bottom club, so up to four K League 2 clubs can go up this year. How the playoffs work, and why third is a better place to finish than fourth, is in [The Long Way Up](/articles/2026-the-playoff-route).

## What Changed in Rounds 31 and 32

**Round 31.**

- **Seoul E-Land lost 4-2 at Yongin** on Saturday October 31. Captain Park Chang-hwan went off injured on 21 minutes, Caio's back pass led to Yongin's second goal, and Gabriel scored twice and set up the first. The full story is in the [Round 31 digest](/rounds/2026-r31_seoul_e-land_digest).
- **Suwon FC beat Ansan 1-0** at home on Sunday November 1 and went back into second, three points clear.
- **Daegu drew 2-2 with Suwon Samsung** in front of 12,230 in Daegu on the Saturday, which ended the leaders' run of seven straight wins.
- **Elsewhere:** Chungnam Asan beat Gimpo 5-1 and Gyeongnam beat Hwaseong 1-0.

**Round 32.**

- **Seoul E-Land beat Jeonnam 3-2** at Mokdong on Saturday November 7, after going a goal down: Euller scored two penalties, both conceded by Kim Ju-yeop, and Park Jae-yong scored the third. The [Round 32 digest](/rounds/2026-r32_seoul_e-land_digest) has it.
- **Suwon FC beat Gyeongnam 3-0** at home earlier the same afternoon.
- **Daegu won 1-0 at Seongnam**, also on Saturday afternoon.
- **Still to play on Sunday:** Suwon Samsung v Yongin, Cheonan v Busan and Paju v Chungnam Asan.

Net effect, on the same model rerun on the table each week: Seoul's top-two chance went from 34 percent after Round 30 to 13 after Round 31 and 8 now. Suwon FC went from 51 to 77 to 86, and Daegu from 16 to 11 to 6. Almost all of Seoul's fall came in Round 31. The model had made Seoul 45 percent to win at Yongin, and a win there would have taken them to about half; the defeat, followed by Suwon FC's win the next day, took them to 13. In Round 32 all three won, so the gaps did not move, but with one match fewer left the same three-point gap is worth more to Suwon FC than it was a week ago.

## What Seoul Actually Need

Seoul are on 1.90 points per game. Two matches remain, so the possible totals are 57 to 63. In practice the main route to second is to win both, which happens in 19 percent of the model's runs, and then it is enough a third of the time.

What it depends on is Suwon FC's last two results:

| If Seoul win both (63 points) and Suwon FC... | Suwon FC finish on | Seoul go up |
|---|---|---|
| lose both, draw both, or draw and lose | 60 to 62 | always |
| win one and lose one | 63 | about 7 times in 10, on goals scored |
| win one and draw one, or win both | 64 or 66 | almost never |

If the two finish level on points, goals scored decides, and the two clubs are level on 57 now. If both finish on 63, Seoul come out above about seven times in ten, because a Seoul side that has won twice has usually scored more than a Suwon FC side that has won once and lost once. If Seoul take four points from six, they need Suwon FC to lose both, and then they go up about two times in three, or to draw one and lose one, and then it is about even.

The realistic target is third. Daegu are one point behind with one more goal scored, and their last two are Gimhae at home, which the model makes 73 percent Daegu, and Cheonan away. Kim Do-gyun said before the Jeonnam match that if Suwon FC win out, Seoul must at least hold third; afterward he said the win had at least kept Seoul in a position to keep chasing.

## Higher Numbers You May Have Seen

Earlier this autumn a prediction circulated online that put Seoul above 50 percent for the top two and Suwon FC in the mid-40s; we could not find its source, so we cannot say how it was built. Our model came closest to it after Round 30, when Suwon FC lost at home to Chungnam Asan and Seoul reached 34 percent. Since then Seoul have lost at Yongin and Suwon FC have won twice, and the model is at 8 percent. Every model, ours included, is an estimate built on assumptions, and different assumptions give different numbers. If you know where the higher figure came from, we would still like to see it.

## The Case For and Against Each Rival

**Suwon Samsung Bluewings (63).** A draw at Daegu ended a run of seven straight wins, but they are three points clear with a game in hand on the clubs below them and need one point against Yongin on Sunday to go up. The model has them at 96 percent for the title.

**Suwon FC (60).** Two straight wins since the home defeat to Chungnam Asan, four goals scored and none conceded in them, and two matches left against sides with nothing to play for. The case against is Gimpo away, where the model gives them a 47 percent chance of winning, a tiebreak in which they are level with Seoul on goals scored, and the loss of centre-back Delan to a calf injury. They are 86 percent for second.

**Daegu FC (56).** Unbeaten in their last four, with a draw against the leaders and three wins, and one point behind Seoul with more goals scored. The model gives them a 6 percent chance of the top two, and has them finishing third 46 percent of the time and fourth 48.

**Busan IPark (48) and Hwaseong FC (48).** The playoff pair, at 99 and 88 percent for the top six; the model has Busan fifth 92 percent of the time and Hwaseong sixth 82. Chungnam Asan, seventh on 42 with two games in hand on Hwaseong, are 13 percent for the top six.
