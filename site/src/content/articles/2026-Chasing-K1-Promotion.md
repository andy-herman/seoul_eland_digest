## What Changed in Round 29

All three of the top three won again. This time the club in third gained ground anyway.

- **Seoul E-Land beat Gimhae 6-2** at Mokdong on Sunday, in heavy rain on a waterlogged pitch, after falling behind to Woo Je-uk's header on 20. Park Jae-yong equalized on 24 and Alan Carius scored the club's 600th goal on 28. After the break Euller scored twice (on 52, and from a direct free kick on 85), academy product Son Hyuk-chan scored his first career goal on 60, and Kim Hyun made it six on 90. Six goals equals the club's single-match scoring record. It was Seoul's third straight win and Kim Do-gyun's 50th win as Seoul E-Land manager. The one cost: Son Hyuk-chan's booking on 55 was his fifth of the season, so he is suspended for Yongin.
- **Suwon FC won 2-1 at Seongnam** on Saturday. Han Chan-hee scored on 37, Yoo Ju-an equalized on 84, and Gu Bon-cheol won it in the sixth minute of stoppage time. Fifteen unbeaten.
- **Suwon Samsung beat Hwaseong 3-2** on Sunday, their sixth win in a row, to stay five clear of the two clubs below them.
- **Daegu beat Chungbuk Cheongju 3-1** on Saturday, back from the bye, and stay five behind second with five to play.
- **Busan** beat Jeonnam 2-1 and move up to fifth; **Hwaseong** drop to sixth. **Gimpo** beat Paju 4-1 and **Chungnam Asan** drew 0-0 at Gyeongnam, which leaves both too far back to catch Seoul.

Net effect: Seoul's top-two chance rose from 18 percent to 26 after the complete round, and Suwon FC's fell from 82 to 74. Before the round, the model said a Suwon FC win at Seongnam would push Seoul's number down to about 11. Suwon FC did win, and Seoul's number went up anyway. The model does not split the change into parts, but three of its inputs moved Seoul's way. The first is the tiebreak: a week ago, in the runs where the two finished level on points, Seoul came out ahead less than 5 percent of the time; now it is 39 percent. The second is Seoul's own form, three wins in a row. The third is the form of the clubs still to play. Yongin lost 4-0 at home to Ansan on Saturday, and the model now makes Seoul 70 percent to win there, up from 49 a week ago. After Gimpo's 4-1 win, Suwon FC's November trip there is a coin flip in the model (50 percent, down from 62).

## What Seoul Actually Need

Seoul are on 1.93 points per game. Four matches remain, so the possible totals are 54 to 66. The model projects 62.6, and at 63, three wins and a defeat, Seoul finish in the top two 24 percent of the time. The honest targets: three wins and a draw (64 points) gets Seoul up 38 percent of the time; four wins from four (66) gets them up 70 percent of the time. There is no total that makes it a coin flip, because 65 cannot be reached from four matches: the step from 64 to 66 is the step from 38 percent to 70. At 62 it is 14 percent, and at 61 it is 7.

The other half is Suwon FC, and here the six goals changed the arithmetic. A tie on points still goes to goals scored, but Suwon FC now lead by one rather than five, so a tie is no longer close to a certain loss for Seoul: the two finish level on points in about 9 percent of the model's runs, and Seoul come out above in 39 percent of those. Even so, the realistic route is to finish clear. If Suwon FC reach 64 or more, Seoul go up only 11 percent of the time; if Suwon FC finish on 61 to 63, it is 57 percent; on 58 to 60, 81. Suwon FC finishing on 63 or fewer means dropping at least six of their remaining 15 points, which the model gives a 31 percent chance. Finishing on 60 or fewer, nine points dropped, happens 6 percent of the time.

Kim Do-gyun's own framing after Gimhae: it was a match that needed three points and goals, narrowing the gap with Suwon FC on goals scored is a positive sign that could also weigh on them, and Seoul have to win every remaining match for there to be any hope. The model agrees with him, with the footnote that four wins from four happens in about 15 percent of its runs, and that even then it is not enough three times in ten.

We looked for its source in Korean and English, across news sites, fan communities, K League United and stats sites, and could not find it, so we cannot say how it was built. Our model has Seoul at 26 percent and Suwon FC at 74, and three things keep Seoul's number well short of a half. Suwon FC are level on points with a game in hand, five matches to Seoul's four. They lead on goals scored, the first tiebreaker, even if only by one now. And even if Seoul win all four and finish on 66, the model sends them up 70 percent of the time, not every time, because Suwon FC can still match or pass that total on their own. Every model, ours included, is an estimate built on assumptions, and different assumptions give different numbers. If you know where the higher figure came from, we would like to see it.

## The Case For and Against Each Rival

**Suwon Samsung Bluewings (59).** Six straight wins, five clear of Seoul and Suwon FC, and 23 conceded in 27. The case against is the calendar: three of their five remaining matches are away, including the trip to Daegu on October 31. Even a slip there would most likely cost them the title rather than a top-two place, where the model has them at 98.5 percent.

**Suwon FC (54, one game in hand on Seoul).** Fifteen unbeaten, four of five remaining at home, and no top-six opponent left. The case against is thicker than a week ago: the cushion on the tiebreak is down from five goals to one, the draw habit (nine in 27) has not gone away, they needed a 96th-minute winner at Seongnam, and the trip to Gimpo on November 21 is now a coin flip in the model. They are still the clear favorite for second.

**Daegu FC (49).** Back from the bye with a 3-1 win, but five behind second with five to play. The model gives them a 2 percent chance of the top two and has them finishing fourth 82 percent of the time; the October 31 home match against the leaders is their last chance to change that.

**Busan IPark (45) and Hwaseong FC (44).** The playoff pair, both above 90 percent for the top six. Busan beat Jeonnam to move above Hwaseong, who finish with four away matches. Gimpo, six behind Hwaseong in sixth after their 4-1 win over Paju, are 13 percent for the top six; Chungnam Asan are under 1.
