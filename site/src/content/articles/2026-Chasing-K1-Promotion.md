## What Changed in Round 30

Seoul did not play, and the round still went their way.

- **Seoul E-Land had the bye**, their second of the season after Round 17, and next play at Yongin on Saturday October 31 without the suspended Son Hyuk-chan.
- **Suwon FC lost 1-0 at home to Chungnam Asan** on Sunday. The only goal was a Suwon FC own goal on 26 minutes, and it ended a 15-match unbeaten run. Suwon FC had won 3-1 at Chungnam Asan earlier in the season.
- **Suwon Samsung won 2-0 at Seongnam** on Sunday, their seventh win in a row. Kang Hyeon-muk scored on 26 minutes and a Seongnam own goal made it two late on.
- **Daegu won 4-2 at Jeonnam** on Saturday and are two points behind Suwon FC and Seoul with four to play.
- **Busan** beat Gyeongnam 3-1 and **Hwaseong** drew 1-1 at Paju, so the playoff places are close to settled. Chungnam Asan's win lifts them to seventh, and **Gimpo** lost 2-1 at home to Gimhae.

Net effect, on the same model rerun on the table before the round: Seoul's top-two chance went from 23 percent to 34, Suwon FC's from 74 to 51 and Daegu's from 5 to 16, while Suwon Samsung's title chance rose from 83 percent to 97. Almost all of Seoul's gain is Suwon FC's defeat. The model had made Suwon FC 52 percent to beat Chungnam Asan, and in the runs where they did not win, Seoul were at 32 percent. Daegu's win took a little of that back. The two now finish level on points in 12 percent of runs, up from 9, and Seoul come out above in 32 percent of those, up from 27.

## What Seoul Actually Need

Seoul are on 1.93 points per game. Four matches remain, so the possible totals are 54 to 66. The model projects 61.0, about seven points from the last twelve. The honest targets: three wins and a defeat (63 points) is now the coin flip, enough 63 percent of the time; three wins and a draw (64) gets Seoul up 77 percent of the time, and four wins from four (66) 95 percent. At 62, two wins and two draws, it is 44 percent; at 61, 25.

The other half is Suwon FC. A tie on points still goes to goals scored, where Suwon FC lead by one: the two finish level on points in 12 percent of the model's runs, and Seoul come out above in 32 percent of those. The realistic route is still to finish clear. If Suwon FC reach 64 or more, Seoul go up only 8 percent of the time; if Suwon FC finish on 61 to 63, it is 33 percent; on 60 or fewer, 59. Suwon FC finishing on 63 or fewer means dropping at least three of their remaining 12 points, a defeat or two draws, which the model gives a 74 percent chance. Finishing on 60 or fewer, six points dropped, happens 30 percent of the time.

Kim Do-gyun's framing after Gimhae was that Seoul have to win every remaining match for there to be any hope. A week later the model is a little kinder: four wins from four happens in about 5 percent of its runs and is enough 95 percent of the time, but three wins and a defeat is now enough more often than not.

After Round 30 our model is much closer to it than it was, with Seoul at 34 percent and Suwon FC at 51, and two things still keep Seoul's number short of a half. Suwon FC lead on goals scored, the first tiebreaker, even if only by one. And Daegu, two points back, take second place in 16 percent of runs. Even if Seoul win all four and finish on 66, the model sends them up 95 percent of the time, not every time, because Suwon FC can match that total too. Every model, ours included, is an estimate built on assumptions, and different assumptions give different numbers. If you know where the higher figure came from, we would still like to see it.

**Suwon Samsung Bluewings (62).** Seven straight wins, eight clear of Suwon FC and Seoul, and 23 conceded in 28. The case against is only the calendar: two of their four remaining matches are away, including the trip to Daegu on October 31. The model has them at 97 percent for the title and above 99 for the top two.

**Suwon FC (54).** Three of four remaining at home, and no top-six opponent left. The case against has grown: the unbeaten run ended at home to Chungnam Asan, the game in hand is gone, the cushion on the tiebreak is one goal, the draw habit (nine in 28) has not gone away, and the trip to Gimpo on November 21 is the one match of their four the model gives them less than an even chance of winning (40 percent). They are still slight favorites for second, at 51 percent.

**Daegu FC (52).** A 4-2 win at Jeonnam leaves them two behind second with four to play. The model gives them a 16 percent chance of the top two and has them finishing fourth 53 percent of the time; the October 31 home match against the leaders, which it rates 34 percent Daegu, 30 draw and 36 Suwon Samsung, is the one that could change that.

**Busan IPark (48) and Hwaseong FC (45).** The playoff pair, at above 99 and 93 percent for the top six. Busan beat Gyeongnam 3-1; Hwaseong drew 1-1 at Paju and finish with three away matches. Chungnam Asan, seventh on 39 after their win at Suwon FC, and Gimpo, eighth on 38, are each about 4 percent for the top six.
