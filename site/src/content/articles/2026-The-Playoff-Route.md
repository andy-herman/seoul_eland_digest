There are two ways for Seoul E-Land to reach K League 1 this season. The first is to finish in the top two, which the model now gives roughly a one-in-five chance and which depends heavily on Suwon FC dropping points (that race is covered in [The Run-In](/articles/2026-the-run-in)). The second is the playoffs, and this season they work differently from last year. For a club in third place, they are unusually friendly.

All probabilities here come from the same model behind [Chasing K1](/articles/2026-chasing-k1-promotion), rerun after the complete Round 28, extended to simulate the playoffs under the 2026 rules. They are estimates, and single knockout matches are where estimates are least reliable.

## How the 2026 Playoffs Work

K League 1 grows to 14 clubs in 2027 and Gimcheon Sangmu's agreement with their home city ends after this season, so the league changed the promotion system for 2026 alone. The rules, from the official K League 2 competition regulations:

- **First and second** go up automatically. No playoff, no K League 1 opponent.
- **Third to sixth** go into the playoffs. The semi-finals are on **Thursday December 3**: third hosts sixth, fourth hosts fifth. Each is a single 90-minute match. If it is level after 90 minutes, there is no extra time and no penalty shootout: the higher-placed club goes through.
- **The final** is on **Sunday December 6**, at the ground of the higher-placed of the two semi-final winners. Again a single match, again no extra time: a draw sends the higher-placed club up. **The winner is promoted.**
- **The loser of the final** gets one more chance, a promotion-relegation playoff against the club that finishes **12th in K League 1**, but only if Gimcheon Sangmu are not that club. Gimcheon are relegated whatever happens. The format and dates of that last tie have not been published.
- **If Gimcheon do finish 12th**, there is no promotion-relegation playoff at all, and the K League 2 final is played over two legs instead.

Compare that with last season, when only the champion went up automatically, the runner-up had to play K League 1's 11th-placed club over two legs, and the playoff winner had to beat K League 1's 10th-placed club. In 2026 the K League 2 playoff winner does not have to play a top-flight side at all.

## Why Third Place Is Worth So Much

Put the rules together and the club that finishes third plays two matches, both at home, and goes up if it avoids defeat in both. A draw in the semi-final is enough. A draw in the final is enough. The club that finishes fourth also hosts its semi-final with a draw in hand, but then usually has to go to third's ground for the final and win there outright.

The model puts a number on the difference. If Seoul finish third, they win the playoffs and go up 62 percent of the time. If they finish fourth, 35 percent. Third is worth almost twice as much as fourth.

## Where Seoul Are Likely to Finish

| Final position | Chance | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | 1% | Champions, promoted |
| 2nd | 17% | Promoted automatically |
| 3rd | 67% | Two home playoff matches; a draw is enough in both |
| 4th | 14% | Home semi-final with a draw in hand, then usually away |
| 5th or 6th | under 1% | Away semi-final, and a draw is not enough |

Seoul are third on 51 points. Daegu are fourth on 46 with a game in hand and six to play, the only club behind Seoul with a realistic chance of catching them. Hwaseong (44) and Busan (42) are fifth and sixth, and Chungnam Asan and Gimpo are seven points behind Busan. Seoul are not yet mathematically sure of a top-six place, but the model has them in it in every run. On average, third place in this division ends on about 61 points and fourth on about 56.

The fight that matters for Seoul's playoff route is therefore with Daegu, not with Suwon FC. Holding third is the thing Seoul most control.

Hwaseong are the opponent to worry about. They are the only one of the three Seoul have not beaten this season, and the model gives Seoul their lowest chance of winning against them.

## The Whole Route, End to End

Combining the rest of the regular season with the playoffs:

| How Seoul's season ends | Chance |
|---|---|
| Promoted automatically (top two) | 19% |
| Promoted by winning the playoffs | 47% |
| Lose the playoff final, then face K League 1's 12th-placed club | 18% |
| Knocked out in the semi-final | 16% |

That is roughly a two-in-three chance of promotion without needing the final tie against a K League 1 club, and a further chance of nearly one in five of reaching that tie. These are the model's numbers. They do not know about injuries, suspensions, or which team arrives in December in form. Over a single knockout match the gap between favorite and underdog is narrower than any season table suggests, which is exactly why the draw rule matters so much.

## The Last Chance: K League 1's 12th Place

If Seoul lose the final on December 6, their season is not necessarily over. The K League 1 club that finishes 12th plays the losing finalist, unless that 12th-placed club is Gimcheon. Right now 12th is Gwangju FC, with 18 points from 31 matches, 2 wins all season and a goal difference of minus 41. Gimcheon are 10th, 15 points clear of Gwangju with seven matches left, so the extra tie is likely to happen. Its format has not been announced.

History is a warning against assuming anything. Between 2022 and 2025, K League 2 clubs won only 2 of 8 promotion-relegation ties against K League 1 sides, although those opponents finished 10th or 11th rather than bottom. This exact matchup has no precedent. We have not tried to model it.

## Seoul Have Been Here Before

Kim Do-gyun's Seoul have reached the playoffs in both of his first two seasons, and the draw rule has been on both sides of the story.

- **2024:** third place. In the K League 2 playoff at Mokdong, Seoul came back from 2-0 down to draw 2-2 with Jeonnam, Baek Ji-woong scoring the equalizer on 80, and went through as the higher-placed club. Then they lost the promotion-relegation playoff to Jeonbuk, 1-2 at Mokdong in front of 13,205 and 1-2 in Jeonju.
- **2025:** fourth place. The semi-playoff at Mokdong needed only a draw. Seoul lost 0-1 to Seongnam.
- **2020, with Suwon FC:** Kim's team drew 1-1 at home against Gyeongnam in the playoff and went up as the higher-placed club.

In February he said the goal was a top-two finish. After the home defeat to Suwon Samsung in September, he said Seoul had to play out the season properly whether they finished second or third, and then wait for what comes next.

## What Needs to Happen

**Route one, the top two (19 percent).** Win almost all of the last five and hope Suwon FC fail to win at least three of their last six. Mostly out of Seoul's hands.

**Route two, third place and two home matches (the most likely way up).** Stay ahead of Daegu, who are five points back with a game in hand. Then, at Mokdong on December 3 and December 6, do not lose. A draw in either match is enough.

**Route three, fourth place.** Win or draw at home against fifth on December 3, then almost certainly win, not draw, at third's ground on December 6.

**Route four, the losing finalist.** Beat K League 1's 12th-placed club, if that club is not Gimcheon, in a tie whose format is not yet known.

## Bottom Line

The new rules have made third place one of the best positions in Korean football's second tier. A club that finishes third needs two home results that are not defeats, against sides it has finished above over 32 matches. Seoul are third now, five points clear of fourth. The model's single most likely version of the season is Seoul finishing third, beating Busan or drawing with them at Mokdong on December 3, and avoiding defeat against Daegu or Hwaseong three days later. Two years ago they were one tie from the top flight. This year the last match may not even need to be won.
