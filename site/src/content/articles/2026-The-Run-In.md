Six rounds of K League 2 are left, Rounds 29 to 34, and Seoul E-Land play in five of them because they have the bye in Round 30. Suwon Samsung and Suwon FC play in all six. Two clubs go up automatically, and after Round 28 the race for those two places looks like this: Suwon Samsung clear at the top, Suwon FC and Seoul level on points for second, and Suwon FC ahead on the tiebreaker.

This piece goes through every remaining match for all three clubs and asks the question a Seoul supporter actually needs answered: which of Suwon FC's results should we be watching? All probabilities come from the same 20,000-run model behind [Chasing K1](/articles/2026-chasing-k1-promotion), rerun after the complete Round 28. They are the model's numbers, not certainties, and they will move every week.

## The Arithmetic Seoul Cannot Escape

The first tiebreaker is goals scored, and Suwon FC lead Seoul by five. In the model's runs where the two finish level on points, Seoul come out ahead less than 5 percent of the time. For practical purposes, Seoul need to finish above Suwon FC on points.

What that asks of Seoul's own five matches:

| Seoul's final total | How Seoul get there | Chance Seoul finish top two |
|---|---|---|
| 66 | Five wins from five | 74% |
| 64 | Four wins and a draw | 44% |
| 63 | Four wins and a defeat | 32% |
| 62 | Three wins and two draws | 19% |
| 61 | The model's projection | 10% |

Even the perfect finish is not enough a quarter of the time, because Suwon FC can still reach 67 or 69 without help. Below 62 it falls to one in ten or worse. Seoul's task is to win, and win almost every time.

Two things stand out. In every one of these matches, the difference for Seoul between a Suwon FC win and a Suwon FC defeat is about 20 points of probability, so there is no single fixture that decides it. And a Suwon FC defeat is worth noticeably more to Seoul than a draw, because a draw still gives Suwon FC a point.

Suwon FC are fourteen matches unbeaten and have not lost at home all season: 7 wins and 5 draws in 12. They are still without Frizzo, their 13-goal top scorer, who has missed at least their last two matches; they won both. Four of their regulars, including captain Han Chan-hee and second-top scorer Ha Jung-woo, are one caution from a one-match ban, which could matter in a tight run-in.

### The four to watch

These are the matches where a Suwon FC slip is most plausible, in order of how likely the model thinks it is.

**1. Seongnam v Suwon FC, Saturday October 17, 14:00 (Suwon FC fail to win 47 percent of the time).** Seongnam are eleventh, but Tancheon is a hard place to win: Seongnam have lost only twice there all season and drawn 7 of their 12 home matches, including 1-1 with Seoul and 2-2 with Hwaseong in their last two. The first meeting between these sides ended 0-0. It is also the first of the six, played the day before Seoul host Gimhae. If Suwon FC win, Seoul's chance drops to about 11 percent. If they lose, it rises to about 31.

**2. Suwon FC v Gyeongnam, Saturday November 7, 14:00 (44 percent).** Gyeongnam are the only side among the six to have beaten Suwon FC this season, 3-2 in Changwon in May, and away from home they rarely lose by much and rarely win: 1 win, 8 draws and 4 defeats on the road. That makes them exactly the kind of opponent that takes a point off a team that does not lose at home. It kicks off two and a half hours before Seoul host Jeonnam the same afternoon.

**3. Gimpo v Suwon FC, Saturday November 21, 16:30 (38 percent).** Gimpo held Suwon FC 1-1 in Suwon in April. Their home record is poor, 2 wins in 11 at Solteo, but they are a side that draws, 11 of 26 overall, and they may still be chasing a playoff place on November 21. Seoul play Chungnam Asan the next day.

**4. Suwon FC v Chungnam Asan, Sunday October 25, 14:00 (36 percent).** Asan are level on 35 points with Gimpo, seven behind sixth, and still chasing the playoff places. The catch is that they are poor travelers, 2 wins in 12 away from home, and Suwon FC won 3-1 at their ground in May. This is the weekend of Seoul's bye.

### The two that probably will not help

Suwon FC against **Ansan** on November 1 (84 percent to win) and against **Chungbuk Cheongju** on the final day (73 percent) are the matches the model is most confident about. Ansan are sixteenth with the worst defense in the division. Cheongju have won their last three, which is worth remembering for November 29, but Suwon FC beat them 4-1 in the first meeting.

Draws and defeats count the same in that table, but not in the standings: in points terms, two Suwon FC defeats are worth exactly as much to Seoul as three draws.

## The Shared Opponents

Three clubs will have a say in more than one of these races. **Gyeongnam** play Suwon FC on November 7, Suwon Samsung on November 21 and Seoul on the final day, and they have already beaten Suwon FC once. **Chungnam Asan** play Suwon FC on October 25 and Seoul on November 22, and they beat Seoul 3-0 in May. **Seongnam** host both Suwon clubs on consecutive weekends in October. If Seoul's season ends in the top two, at least one of those three will probably have helped.

## Bottom Line

Seoul cannot do this on their own anymore. They need to win almost every remaining match, ideally by more than one goal, and they need Suwon FC to drop points at least three times in six. The model gives that combination roughly one chance in five. The weekends to circle are October 17 at Tancheon and November 7 when Gyeongnam come to Suwon, the two Suwon FC matches the model rates closest to even. If Suwon FC win both, which happens in about 30 percent of the model's runs, Seoul's chance falls to 7 percent and their season is almost certainly heading for the playoffs. If Suwon FC drop points in both, about one run in five, Seoul's chance roughly doubles to 38 percent, and it is a race again.
