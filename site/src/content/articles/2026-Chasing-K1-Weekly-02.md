The second edition of the weekly letter. Same four things as last time: the one number that matters (Seoul's chance of an automatic promotion place), how it has moved, what Suwon Samsung, Suwon FC, Daegu and Hwaseong play in the coming round and what the model expects of them, and one week further ahead. The [Chasing K1](/articles/2026-chasing-k1-promotion) page stays as the running summary; this is the diary.

## The number this week

**18 percent.** That is the model's chance, after 20,000 simulated finishes from the complete Round 28 table, that Seoul E-Land finish first or second and go up without a playoff. Suwon FC are at 82 percent, Daegu at 2, Hwaseong at zero. Suwon Samsung win the league in 83 percent of runs.

Seoul won, Suwon FC won, Suwon Samsung won by three, and the number went down. That is what a race looks like when you are third and everyone above you keeps winning: Seoul's projected total has gone up by more than a point since No. 1, and their chance has gone down by four. Two things moved it this weekend, in roughly equal parts. One is Sunday: Suwon Samsung's 4-1 win over Ansan, their fifth in a row, put them five clear with a game in hand. The other is the model's inputs. This rerun replaces every club's hand-entered recent form with its actual last five results from the official record; two of the old figures, Seoul's and Daegu's, did not match the results they were meant to summarize, and fixing them, along with refreshing the form of Seoul's remaining opponents, accounts for the rest.

Round 29 is Gimhae at Mokdong on Sunday October 18 at 14:00. The model gives Seoul 82 percent to win, 14 to draw, 4 to lose, with 2-0 the likeliest score.

**The match to watch is Seongnam against Suwon FC.** It is Suwon FC's last away match until late November and the one in their run-in the model rates closest to a coin flip, 53 percent. Seongnam are eleventh and lost 3-1 at Cheongju on Friday, but their last two home matches at Tancheon were a 1-1 draw with Seoul and a 2-2 draw with Hwaseong. If Suwon FC fail to win there, Seoul's chance rises to 27 percent. If Suwon FC win, it falls to about 11.

**Suwon Samsung against Hwaseong** is the title fixture: the leaders on five straight wins against the fifth-placed side, who play every one of their remaining matches away from home. It decides how big Suwon Samsung's cushion at the top is, not who finishes second.

**Daegu** come back from the bye against a Chungbuk Cheongju side that has won its last three. At five points behind Seoul and Suwon FC with six to play, Daegu need wins and help.

## What Seoul need this week

A win against Gimhae, and goals. The arithmetic from here is simple to state. Five wins from five, 66 points, puts Seoul in the top two 75 percent of the time. Four wins and a draw, 64, is 43 percent. Anything below 63 is a long shot whatever Suwon FC do. And because a tie on points goes to goals scored, every goal Seoul score against the bottom half is worth something that a 1-0 is not. The model's likeliest score against Gimhae is 2-0. Seoul need better than likely.
