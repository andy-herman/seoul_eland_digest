Part 9 was [Lee Ju-hyeok](/articles/2026-season-review-lee-ju-hyeok), who started on the left against Daegu. Part 10 is the midfielder who started in the middle that night because Park Chang-hwan was suspended, and scored the goal that opened the most important home win of the season. For most of the summer he was not playing at all, which is the other half of the story.

## The season in one paragraph

Cho Jun-hyun arrived from Suwon FC in December, a 21-year-old who had left Soongsil University after two years, played eight K League 1 matches in two seasons, and been called to the domestic camp before the Under-23 Asian Cup. The club presented him as a forward-carrying midfielder, and the press as the replacement for Seo Jae-min, who had left for Incheon. He said he wanted to be remembered as a core member of a promotion team. He started six of the first nine matches, had a rehearsed set-piece goal against Seongnam ruled out in May, and then lost his place: between Round 14 and Round 26 he started once and sat unused on the bench six times. With Park Chang-hwan banned for Daegu, Kim Do-gyun gave him the start, and on 29 minutes he followed up his own saved shot to score his first goal as a professional, in his 24th league match. He was booked nine minutes later and replaced on the hour.

## What he has done well

**He carries the ball.** This is what Seoul signed him for, and the numbers say he does it: fifteen successful dribbles at nearly 79 percent, with a duel win rate of two in three and 22 fouls won in 902 minutes. In Round 2 against Gyeongnam, the first home win of the season, he drove down the left six minutes in and put in a cross that caused a scramble in front of goal, and he lasted until the 75th minute in a midfield two with Park Chang-hwan. His own description of his game, from May, is that the attacking side is his strength and that driving forward is what he wants national-team coaches to see.

**He arrives.** The two goals he has put in the net this season came from the same instinct. Against Seongnam in Round 13, playing on the right of the front three, he met Euller's low corner first time in the 56th minute and scored, only for the referee to rule it out for a foul earlier in the move. Kim Do-gyun, who spent most of that press conference angry about the second half, stopped to say that "Cho Jun-hyun's goal was a pity too, but it was something we prepared." Against Daegu, Oh In-pyo ran in behind, Baek Ji-woong crossed low and first time, Han Tae-hee saved Cho's first shot, and Cho was the one who kept running and put the rebound in. A midfielder who follows his own shot is a midfielder who will score a few of these.

**He took the big start when it came.** He had played 15 minutes across the four matches before Daegu. He was asked to fill the role of the club's most-used midfielder in a match the Korean press called a six-point game, and he scored the opener and held the middle with Yoon Seok-ju until Carius replaced him on 61. The same thing happened on a smaller scale at Hwaseong in August, when he came in for Park Chang-hwan in the toughest away match of the summer and the defensive half of the job was done well.

**He is tidy.** For a player whose instinct is to go forward, 84.8 percent passing and a 71 percent long-ball rate are good numbers. He does not give the ball away often, which is part of why he gets the nod in the middle when Park Chang-hwan is missing rather than being used only as a wide option.

## Where the same mistakes keep showing up

**He has not held a place.** After starting the first four matches he lost ground to Carius's return, to Seo Jin-seok, and in the summer to Yoon Seok-ju. He was out of the squad three times and an unused substitute seven. The Hwaseong start in August ended on the hour and he was back on the bench for the next three matches. A 22-year-old who plays 902 minutes in a promotion season is not failing, but he has spent the summer as the fifth or sixth midfielder in a side that starts three.

**The forward passing is not there yet.** Six chances created and no assists is a thin return for a player described as a creator. When he replaced Park Chang-hwan at Hwaseong, the 0-0 stalled in exactly the place Park usually covers: Seoul could not move the ball forward through the middle. Cho was honest about the tension himself. "The forward play I like carries some risk," he said in May. "Our team prefers more stable play in midfield, so I get a lot of feedback on my game. I need to get better at telling where to use my strengths."

**The fouls.** Three yellow cards in 902 minutes, and the Daegu one came on 38 minutes for stopping a counterattack, nine minutes after he had scored. That kind of foul is part of playing the Park Chang-hwan role. It is also why Park carries eight bookings, and Cho is now two away from his first ban.

## His strengths, and what they mean for the last six

**Cover for Park Chang-hwan.** Park is back for Gimpo on October 9, but he has eight yellow cards and the next ban comes at eleven. If Park misses another match, Daegu showed that Cho can start in his place without the team losing its shape.

**A carrier against deep blocks.** Kim Do-gyun has pointed out that the matches against promotion rivals are all played, and most of the six sides left will sit in. Seoul's problem, by the manager's own account, is goals: he said after Daegu that the forwards coming off the bench have to do more. A midfielder who beats a man and draws fouls around the box is a useful late option against Gimpo, Yongin and Chungnam Asan, even if the bench goals he was talking about are expected from attackers.

**Set pieces.** The Seongnam routine that produced his disallowed goal was one Kim Do-gyun said the team had worked on all week. Seoul's remaining matches will be decided by the second ball in crowded boxes, and Cho has shown he will be the one arriving for it.

## The verdict at Round 27

A spring starter who became a summer spectator and then scored one of the season's most important goals. The Daegu night does not undo three months on the bench, and 902 minutes with no assists is not the core-member season he promised in December. But he has done the thing a squad midfielder is kept for, which is to step in when the first choice is missing and not let the team drop.

**Grade so far: C plus.** The plus is for Daegu and the ball-carrying numbers. The rest of the grade is for the stretch from Round 14 to Round 26, in which he played about two hours of football.
