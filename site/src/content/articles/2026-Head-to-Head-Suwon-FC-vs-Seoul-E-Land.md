Suwon FC lost 1-0 at home to Chungnam Asan on Sunday, to an own goal on 26 minutes, and with it went their 15-match unbeaten run and their game in hand. Seoul E-Land had the Round 30 bye. The two clubs are now level on 54 points from 28 matches, with four to play each.

Suwon Samsung, eight points clear on 62, are 97 percent for the title and above 99 percent for the top two, so the race that is left is for the second automatic promotion place, and it is between these two. Daegu, two points further back on 52, are the outsider. After the complete Round 30, the model gives Suwon FC a 51 percent chance of the top two, Seoul E-Land 34 and Daegu 16. A week ago it was Suwon FC 74, Seoul 23. Seoul finish above Suwon FC in 40 percent of the model's runs; their top-two chance is lower than that because in some of those runs Daegu pass them both.

Every figure here comes from the [Run-In Predictor](/articles/2026-run-in-predictor), 250,000 simulations of every remaining K League 2 fixture, rerun after Round 30. They are estimates, not certainties, and they will move every week.

## The Verdict

Suwon FC are still favorites for the second automatic place, but only just: 51 percent to Seoul's 34, a race that went from 74 against 23 to close to a coin flip in a weekend in which Seoul did not play. The likeliest promoted pair is Suwon Samsung and Suwon FC, in 50 percent of runs, then Suwon Samsung and Seoul E-Land in 34, then Suwon Samsung and Daegu in 16. Daegu are the third party: two points behind, they host Suwon Samsung on October 31 and finish with Seongnam away, Gimhae at home and Cheonan away.

Seoul control most of their own fate. Win all four and they go up 95 percent of the time. The catch is that four wins from four is rare, and three, the realistic ambition, still needs Suwon FC to drop points somewhere. Gimpo away on November 21 is where the model thinks that is likeliest.

Whoever misses out still has a second route. Third to sixth go into the playoff, and both clubs are already certain of a top-six place. How that works, and why third is not a bad place to finish this season, is in [The Long Way Up](/articles/2026-the-playoff-route).
