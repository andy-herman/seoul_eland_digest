# Round 25 Digest: Chungbuk Cheongju vs Seoul E-Land FC

> *A seventeen-year-old who lives on pocket money headed in one goal and curled in another inside four minutes of first-half stoppage time, breaking a record that had stood for twenty years. His manager, booked in the second half, collected the 100th win of his career. Seoul E-Land went back to second on a Friday night in Cheongju, nine unbeaten, with the leaders due at Mokdong in eight days.*

## The Round in One Paragraph

Seoul E-Land beat Chungbuk Cheongju 2-0 at Cheongju Stadium and the scorer of both goals was born in April 2009. [[Ahn Joo-wan]] headed Euller's cross in on 45 minutes, having already hit the crossbar with a header on the half hour, then in stoppage time took Osmar's pass on the left of the box, cut inside and bent a right-footed shot into the far corner. At 17 years, 4 months and 21 days he became the youngest player in K League history to score twice in a match, taking a record Lee Hyun-seung had held since a 2006 League Cup game, and doing it roughly ten months younger than the league-only mark Yang Min-hyuk set for Gangwon two summers ago. The second half was a formality that Seoul managed without much grace, Kim Do-gyun collecting a yellow card for his protests on 54, [[Min Sung-jun]] making one important stop from Ménder García, and a late flurry of substitutions running the clock down. It was Kim Do-gyun's 100th win as a K League manager, the 23rd coach to reach the number, and it took Seoul to 45 points and second place on Friday night. By Sunday, Suwon Samsung had beaten Chungnam Asan to reach 50 and restore a five-point lead. The match that decides whether that gap is real is on September 12, at Mokdong, against them.

## Match Report

The lineup was the strong one this time. After five changes and two debutants at Seongnam six days earlier, Kim Do-gyun restored Euller, [[Alan Carius]], [[Baek Ji-woong]], [[Bae Seo-jun]] and [[Ahn Joo-wan]] to a 4-3-3: [[Min Sung-jun]]; [[Oh In-pyo]], Osmar, [[Baek Ji-woong]] and [[Bae Seo-jun]] across the back, with the captain [[Kim Oh-kyu]] left out entirely after his unexplained early exit at Tancheon; [[Alan Carius]], [[Yoon Seok-ju]] and [[Park Chang-hwan]] in midfield; Ahn Joo-wan on the left, [[Park Jae-yong]] central, Euller on the right. Luiz Quinta's Cheongju, who had beaten Seoul at Mokdong in June for their first win of the season and have drawn fourteen times since, set up in a 4-3-3 built around García, Lee Jong-eon and Bandeira, with Kim Sun-min and Jeong Jin-woo in midfield. The broadcast's pre-match note was the one everyone was thinking: eight unbeaten, two straight draws, first-half goals conceded in both, and a team that badly needed to score first.

They nearly did inside three minutes. Euller's inswinging corner was cleared, Lee Jong-eon skied a half-chance at the other end, and then on 8 minutes Euller and Carius combined for what looked like the opener, Euller's left-footed pass and Carius's finish, only for the assistant to flag Carius offside. The broadcast's co-commentator, watching Seoul dominate the ball without converting for the third week running, called it the second big chance missed and said what the whole fanbase was thinking: it was getting painful to watch a team this good at everything except the last touch.

Ahn Joo-wan changed the mood. He hit the crossbar with a header on 27, a warning delivered with the kind of timing his manager has been trying to coax out of him all summer. [[Park Chang-hwan]] was booked on 31 for a late tackle, and the referee Park Jin-ho briefly conferred over the color of the card. Then, with the half about to end, the goal: [[Bae Seo-jun]] fed Euller on the right, Euller's cross was, in the commentator's words, delivered with the kind of quality a professional cannot miss, and Ahn Joo-wan met it with a header that this time went in rather than against the bar. Forty-five minutes on the clock. Cheongju, who had been the better side for stretches of possession, never got a chance to reset.

Because four minutes later it was two. A Seoul corner, Osmar stepping up into the attack and recycling the ball, and Ahn Joo-wan collecting it on the left edge of the box with a defender to beat. He shaped to go outside, cut in, and curled a right-footed shot across the goalkeeper into the far top corner, the sort of finish that gets replayed in slow motion before the players have finished celebrating. The broadcast called it a wonder goal. His teammates mobbed him. The record was his, and he did not know it until someone told him in the mixed zone.

The second half was a different match, and Quinta made it so with three halftime substitutions, one of them Francisco Geraldes, who had been a Seoul E-Land player until the deadline-day trade that brought Hong Seok-jun the other way. Cheongju held the ball, García headed a corner over Min Sung-jun's bar and later headed another that the goalkeeper claimed, and the visitors defended the lead in a way that was more tense than it needed to be. The manager felt it too: on 54 Kim Do-gyun was shown a yellow card for his running dialogue with the referee, an unusual sight for a coach known for measured pressers. [[Ahn Joo-wan]] nearly had his hat-trick early in the half, a left-footed strike that dropped to Euller for a follow-up. Park Chang-hwan drove one straight at Jo Seong-bin. Otherwise Seoul spent the last half hour keeping the ball and their heads.

The substitutions told the story of the week ahead. Euller and Park Jae-yong came off together on 79 for [[Lee Ju-hyeok]] and [[Kim Hyun]], both saved for Saturday against Suwon. Hong Seok-jun replaced the record-breaker on 86 against his former club. [[Cho Jun-hyun]] came on for Carius on 87 and [[Yoon Seok-ju]] was booked in the same minute. Caio Marcelo took [[Bae Seo-jun]]'s place in stoppage time. The whistle came in front of 2,610, and the first person to reach Kim Do-gyun was the seventeen-year-old who had just given him his hundredth win.

## Player Performances

**[[Ahn Joo-wan]] (안주완):**
Three goals in the season now, all of them from a 17-year-old, and the two here were entirely different kinds of finish: a striker's header attacking a cross, and a winger's curler from the corner of the box. The Sports Chosun interview afterwards was the best thing published about this club all week. He had no idea about the record until told; he had been telling himself for weeks that he had to start scoring; he had almost no headed goals in his career; he lives on an allowance and does not buy things because he cannot afford them; he wants the youngest-ever hat-trick next; and he would trade any of it for promotion. He also admitted on the broadcast that he barely defended in school because he scored too much, and that learning to defend in the pros has been hard. Every part of that is a footballer who is going to be very good and knows exactly what he does not yet do.

**Euller (에울레르):**
One assist, the disallowed near-assist on 8, the delivery on the corner that led to the second, and 79 minutes of running Cheongju's left side. His manager took him off with a title decider eight days away; the broadcast noted that every Seoul chance of note had come off his boot. In the R23 digest we called his left foot the most valuable weapon in the squad. Nothing since has changed that.

**Osmar (오스마르):**
Back at center-back after a full match as the holding midfielder at Seongnam, and involved in the second goal from an attacking corner. At 38 he has now played every minute of a three-match, thirteen-day stretch. The clean sheet, Seoul's first since Hwaseong in early August, was built on him and [[Baek Ji-woong]] winning the aerial duels García kept asking of them.

**[[Min Sung-jun]] (민성준):**
Redemption after the corner he did not reach at Seongnam. Two headers from García, one over the bar under pressure, one caught cleanly, and a first clean sheet in four matches. The broadcast made a point of praising his decisiveness on crosses, which was a fair thing to notice after last week.

**[[Baek Ji-woong]] (백지웅):**
Two weeks after being named in the press room for the Paju error, he was restored at center-back and was immaculate: a first-half tackle that stopped García at the top of the box, calm distribution, no fuss. The manager's public criticism and the player's answer to it are now a closed loop, at least for this week.

**[[Alan Carius]] (까리우스):**
The offside on 8 cost him a goal by a stride. After that he was the midfield's engine, pressing, carrying, and taking the ball into the corners late. Withdrawn on 87 without a booking. Available for Saturday.

## Fan Reaction

The loudest Seoul E-Land night online since the Busan win, and almost all of it about one player.

FM Korea's live threads on the brace went past 40,000 views each. The thread on the first goal was equal parts Euller worship and disbelief at the marking; the thread on the second, titled around the trajectory of the curler, filled with Son Heung-min comparisons that half the commenters immediately warned against, plus the observation that a 2009-born cannot even move abroad yet. The most interesting thread was the one built around his broadcast confession that he did not defend at school: it became a genuine argument about how Korean youth football develops forwards who never learn the other half of the game, with fans quoting pundits and arguing about whether the club's project approach to him is smart or reckless. His Suwon Samsung academy roots and the fact that his father is a Seoul assistant coach were repeated in every thread, usually admiringly.

The 100th-win discussion was warmer and smaller: praise for a manager who plays attacking football and trusts a teenager, and a thread of speculation about him as an Ulsan HD candidate that Seoul fans will want to stop reading. The E-Land search on FM Korea was otherwise about promotion math and the logistics of getting into Mokdong on Saturday. DC Inside's Seoul gallery managed a single laughing post about the goal; the overseas-football gallery spent three days arguing about Ahn Joo-wan's kicking power. Naver comments and X were not reachable this round.

## News and Transfers

**Kim Do-gyun's 100th win, in his 247th K League match: 100 wins, 59 draws, 88 losses.** He is the 23rd manager in the league's history to reach the number. His post-match words were about the team rather than himself: an important away match, the players did what they had prepared, the first-half goals were what they wanted, and he was grateful for the clean sheet. On the record-breaker: congratulations, and do not stop here. Before the match he had told reporters Seoul needed to win all nine remaining games. One down.

**The Geraldes trade, finally explained.** The deadline-day deal that brought Hong Seok-jun from Cheongju was a two-for-one: Francisco Geraldes went the other way permanently and Park Jin-young on loan, with the stated aim of covering the hole Byun Kyung-jun's enlistment left. Our R23 preview reported that no Geraldes exit had surfaced anywhere; it surfaced two days later and completed on the final day of the window. He made his Cheongju debut at halftime here, against the club that sold him. Hong Seok-jun, who had played every 2026 match for Cheongju before the move, came on against his old club on 86.

**Kim Oh-kyu was left out.** The captain came off unexplained on 57 at Seongnam and was not in the eleven here; Osmar and Baek Ji-woong kept a clean sheet without him. Whether it is fitness or form is the biggest selection question before Suwon.

**The manager's card.** Kim Do-gyun was booked on 54 for his dialogue with referee Park Jin-ho. In a season where his temperament has been a public asset, it was a small sign of what a title race does to everyone.

**Quinta's read.** Cheongju's manager said the difference was the two halves: an early error gave Seoul the ball and the tempo, his side defended for forty-five minutes and then "found the football we want" after the break. He blamed his team's losses of possession in dangerous areas, which is a polite way of describing the two goals.

## Tactical and Strategic Watch

**The frozen prediction called this a loss; Seoul won.** Our July call had Round 25 as an L, and it is the second time since the restart that the team has beaten the published prediction on the right side, after the win at Busan. The grading now reads five hits in eight, with the misses split evenly between pessimism and optimism.

**Scoring first fixes everything.** In the two draws before this, Seoul conceded first and spent the second half in a siege that produced one goal from 40 shots. Here they scored twice before halftime and the match changed character completely: Cheongju had to come out, Seoul had room, and the second half was a controlled, if nervy, hold. Ahn Joo-wan said it himself afterwards: they kept conceding first, and scoring first and keeping a clean sheet felt completely different. That is the whole tactical lesson of August in one sentence from a teenager.

**Osmar in defense, Carius in midfield: the strong shape is settled.** The two weeks of experiments, Osmar as the six and Baek Ji-woong as the holder, produced one goal from two matches and a set-piece concession each. The return to Osmar and Baek Ji-woong as the center-back pair, with Carius, Yoon Seok-ju and Park Chang-hwan ahead of them, produced a clean sheet and two goals. If there was any doubt about the eleven for Saturday, this match should have ended it.

**The bench was used for Saturday, not for Friday.** Euller and Park Jae-yong off together on 79, Hong Seok-jun and Cho Jun-hyun and Caio Marcelo all given minutes. A manager who was managing the next match while winning this one.

## My Honest Read

I want to resist writing a love letter to a seventeen-year-old and I am going to fail, so let me at least be precise about why. The brace is not the point. The point is that in a fortnight where this team's problem was screamingly obvious, control without a first goal, a player who was on the bench for both draws walked into the eleven, hit the bar, scored a header, scored a curler, and then explained in an interview that he had been telling himself for weeks that he had to score. Kim Do-gyun has spent two months looking for someone who attacks the space behind a defense instead of passing in front of it. He has one. He is 17. The answer to August was in the building the whole time.

Two cautions, because that is what this column is for. First, this was Cheongju, thirteenth, three wins all season, and Seoul still needed the goals to come in stoppage time to make the half look comfortable. The first forty-four minutes had the same shape as Paju and Seongnam, right down to the disallowed goal. Second, the manager was booked defending a two-goal lead against the thirteenth-placed side. That is a coach feeling the weight of the next match before this one had ended. I do not blame him. I note it.

Now the part that matters. Seoul are second, five behind, nine unbeaten, with the leaders coming to Mokdong on Saturday and the third-placed side the Saturday after. The team that turned up in Cheongju, the strong eleven, Osmar at center-back, Carius in midfield, a teenager on the wing, is the one that should start. It has now produced the only clean sheet in a month and the only comfortable win since Ansan. Kim Do-gyun has his hundredth. The hundred-and-first is the one this club has been waiting twelve years for.
