# Round 19 Digest: Seoul E-Land FC vs Cheonan City FC

> *A seventeen-year-old came off the bench and wrote his name into the K League record book, Alan Carius produced a goal and two assists, and Seoul E-Land still had to survive a 4-1 lead turning into 4-3.*

## The Round in One Paragraph

This was the best night and the most alarming night of Seoul E-Land's season, and it was the same night. [[Mokdong Stadium]] said goodbye to Byun Kyung-jun (변경준) before his military enlistment, watched Cheonan City take a thirteenth-minute lead, and then watched Alan Carius (알랑 카리우스) answer four minutes later with the best individual goal a Seoul player has scored all year. From there it became a procession: Osmar (오스마르) headed in a Carius corner, [[Ahn Joo-wan]] (안주완) came off the bench at seventeen years old and scored a goal that made him, by the broadcast's reckoning, the youngest scorer in K League history, and [[Park Jae-yong]] made it 4-1 from a third Carius delivery. Then Seoul stopped. Aboubacar Toungara (뚠가라) and a stoppage-time header dragged it back to 4-3, and the final whistle arrived with Cheonan camped in the box. Three points, 33 from 18, and a defensive habit that is now impossible to write off as a coincidence.

## Match Report

[[Kim Do-gyun]] went back to a back four for the home fixture, a 4-3-3 with [[Min Sung-jun]] (민성준) in goal, [[Bae Seo-jun]] (배서준) and [[Oh In-pyo]] (오인표) as full-backs, and Osmar (오스마르) alongside [[Baek Ji-woong]] (백지웅) in the middle. The midfield three was Alan Carius (알랑 카리우스), [[Yoon Seok-ju]] (윤석주), and [[Park Chang-hwan]] (박창환) wearing the armband, with Byun Kyung-jun (변경준), [[Kim Hyun]] (김현), and Euller (에울레르) in front. That is two shapes in two matches: the 3-4-3 away at a promotion rival, the back four at home against a bottom-half side. Cheonan City came in a 3-4-3 of their own under Park Jin-seop (박진섭).

The occasion mattered before the football did. This was Byun Kyung-jun's last match before enlisting for military service, and the home end came with banners and messages for a forward whose pace off the bench has changed several matches this season. He started, which was the right call.

Seoul began as though the evening would be simple. Euller, who the commentary team called a Cheonan killer, twice found the far post with left-footed deliveries that had the visiting defense scrambling, and [[Yoon Seok-ju]] continued his recent habit of striking cleanly from distance with a half-volley that flashed past the frame. Then, against the run of everything, Cheonan scored. Cha Seung-hyun (차승현) pressed Euller and won the ball high, Seoul failed to clear the danger, Lee Ji-hoon (이지훈) crossed, and Bruno Lamas (라마스) arrived at the far post to finish on thirteen minutes. Seventh goal of the season for Lamas, and Seoul were behind at home to the side sitting well below them.

The response took four minutes and it was extraordinary. Alan Carius collected the ball in a position where the angle had effectively closed, on his left foot, and rather than cut back he simply hit it, up and over Lee Seung-gyu (이승규) and into the top of the goal. It was the finish of a striker who spent ten months injured and knows exactly what his left foot can do; the Saudi second-division golden boot he won before arriving suddenly made sense in a way it had not in a Seoul shirt before. One apiece inside seven minutes.

The rest of the first half was scrappy and physical. Choi Kyu-baek (최규백) picked up a knock and played on through a cooling break before eventually being withdrawn, [[Bae Seo-jun]] took a heavy challenge on the ankle, and both sides traded half-chances. Euller worked Lee Seung-gyu with a low effort and Park Chang-woo (박창우) dragged a long-range attempt wide for Cheonan, who looked most dangerous when they got their wing-backs high. Half time, 1-1.

[[Kim Do-gyun]]'s substitutions decided the match, and they decided it emphatically. [[Park Jae-yong]] replaced [[Kim Hyun]] at the interval; not long after, Lee Ju-hyuk (이주혁) and [[Ahn Joo-wan]] came on for Euller and the departing Byun Kyung-jun. Every one of them affected the scoreline.

The second goal came from a corner, and it came from the player who has been the set-piece problem all season for the opposition rather than for Seoul. Carius delivered it accurately for the first time all evening from that side, and Osmar rose above everyone to head it in. Two-one, and Osmar's second goal of the season.

Then the moment the night will be remembered for. Seoul broke, [[Park Chang-hwan]] chased down a ball that looked certain to run out of play near the touchline, kept it alive, and looked up to find [[Ahn Joo-wan]] arriving on the opposite side of the box completely unattended. The finish was calm, first-time, and utterly unlike a teenager's. Born on April 14, 2009, [[Ahn Joo-wan]] was seventeen years, three months, and ten days old, and the goal is the youngest in the combined history of K League 1 and K League 2. It broke Park Seung-soo's mark of seventeen years, three months, and thirteen days, set with Suwon Samsung in 2024, by three days. Three days. Had this fixture fallen the following week, the record would have stayed where it was. It was also his first K League goal, and there is a detail that makes it better: his other record, the youngest appearance in K League 2 history at sixteen years, eleven months, and seven days, was also set against Cheonan City, back in Round 4. He has now taken two K League records off the same opponent.

Seoul made it four through the player who has been their most consistent scorer. Carius, with his third assist-quality delivery of the night, found [[Park Jae-yong]], and the finish was clinical. It carried a story of its own: [[Park Jae-yong]] had personally invited a young supporter to the match and promised a specific celebration, and he delivered both the goal and the promise. Carius finished the evening with a goal and two assists and was withdrawn to an ovation, [[Kang Hyeon-je]] (강현재) replacing him.

At 4-1 with fifteen minutes left, this should have been finished. Instead Cheonan, a side the commentary team pointed out had already scored five goals after the ninetieth minute this season, went and did it again. Aboubacar Toungara (뚠가라), on as a substitute and returning from a long injury layoff, converted a Lamas pass to make it 4-2. Then, two minutes into stoppage time, Lee Kyu-min (이규민) delivered from the left and Lee Sang-jun (이상준) headed in to make it 4-3, confirmed after a review. Cheonan had two further moments, a claim for handball waved away and another Lee Sang-jun effort that cleared the bar as he chased a stoppage-time brace, before the whistle finally went.

## Player Performances

**Alan Carius (알랑 카리우스):**
A goal and two assists, the K League 2 Player of the Round award, and the single best piece of individual quality Seoul have produced this season. The equaliser from a closed angle on his left foot was outrageous, and both set-piece and open-play deliveries for Osmar and [[Park Jae-yong]] were precise. Ten months out of football with an injury, and this is the match that announces he is properly back. If he holds this level, the promotion push has a genuinely different ceiling.

**Ahn Joo-wan (안주완):**
Seventeen years old, first K League goal on sixty-four minutes, and a record that will follow him. What stood out was not the finish, a curled right-footed effort, but the run: he read the counter, recognized the far side was empty, and arrived unmarked because he moved before anyone else did. He said afterward that the staff had told him to make exactly that run in exactly that situation, which says something about both the coaching and the player's willingness to listen. There is a family angle to that, too: his father, Ahn Seong-nam (안성남), is Seoul E-Land's assistant manager. He named Vinicius Junior as his model and thanked his parents, and his teammates have taken to calling him a victory fairy, with Seoul unbeaten in the six professional appearances he has been involved in. On this evidence, none of it is embarrassing.

**Osmar (오스마르):**
A goal from a corner, a second consecutive full appearance after the ankle scare, and the aerial presence that Seoul's set-piece attack has been missing. At thirty-seven he is not going to play every minute of the run-in, but on this form he changes what Seoul can do in both boxes.

**Park Chang-hwan (박창환):**
Wore the armband and produced the assist of the night, running down a lost cause on the touchline and picking out [[Ahn Joo-wan]] with the pass that made history. He looked exhausted by the end, which is the cost of the way he plays. The captaincy suits him.

**Park Jae-yong (박재용):**
Another goal off the bench, another decisive substitute contribution, and a promise kept to a young supporter in the stands. He is now the team's leading scorer by a distance and has scored in three of his last four appearances regardless of whether he starts. The selection question for Round 20 answers itself.

**Byun Kyung-jun (변경준):**
His farewell before military service, and a start he earned. The pace that made him Seoul's most reliable impact substitute will be missed for the rest of the run-in, and the squad is thinner in behind without him. A proper send-off from the Mokdong crowd.

**Yoon Seok-ju (윤석주):**
Struck the ball cleanly from range again, a week after hitting the woodwork at Suwon FC. The long-range threat is becoming a genuine feature of his game and it gives Seoul a way to hurt teams that defend deep.

## News and Transfers

Byun Kyung-jun's enlistment is the significant squad development of the month and it is a real loss. He has been the most effective substitute in the squad, a pace outlet in behind that no one else replicates, and the run-in now needs someone to take that role. [[Ahn Joo-wan]]'s emergence is timely and probably not coincidental.

Alan Carius returning to this level is the other headline. Signed with the number 10 and lost to injury for most of a year, he has now produced a match-winning performance from midfield in a back-four system. Whether [[Kim Do-gyun]] keeps him deeper or pushes him closer to [[Park Jae-yong]] is one of the more interesting selection questions of the second half of the season.

## Tactical and Strategic Watch

Two matches, two shapes: a 3-4-3 away at Suwon FC and a 4-3-3 at home to Cheonan. That flexibility is a strength when it is deliberate, and [[Kim Do-gyun]]'s substitutions here were as decisive as any tactical setup, with three of the four goals involving players who started on the bench.

The problem is the same one Round 18 exposed. Seoul led 4-1 with a quarter of an hour to go and conceded twice. They led 2-1 at Suwon FC and conceded in the ninety-sixth minute. Across two rounds Seoul have led by a goal or more in the final fifteen minutes twice and given up three goals in that window. Against Busan IPark that pattern does not cost two points, it costs the match.

## Looking Ahead

Round 20 is the trip nobody wants: away at league leaders Busan IPark at Gudeok Stadium on August 2. Busan won the reverse fixture 3-2 at [[Mokdong Stadium]] back in Round 3, and they have set the pace in this division all season. It is the first of the two back-to-back away tests the season preview flagged as the hardest week of Seoul's calendar.

## My Honest Read

Four goals at home, a record-breaking teenager, a Brazilian number 10 announcing his return with a goal and two assists, and a promise kept to a kid in the stands. If you only read the highlights, this was the best night of Seoul E-Land's season, and there is a version of this article that just celebrates it. [[Ahn Joo-wan]]'s goal is the kind of thing supporters remember for twenty years, and the fact that it arrived four days before the record would have become unbreakable is the sort of detail that makes football worth following.

But the last fifteen minutes have to be addressed, because they are now a pattern rather than an incident. Seoul were 4-1 up against a side in the bottom half, at home, with the match entirely under control, and they conceded twice and finished the game defending their own eighteen-yard box. A week earlier they had been 2-1 up at a promotion rival and conceded in the sixth minute of stoppage time. That is three goals given up from winning positions in the last fifteen minutes across two matches, and the excuse of individual errors does not stretch far enough to cover it.

Here is why it matters right now rather than in the abstract. The next fixture is at the club that has led this division from the start, at their ground, and Busan IPark do not need to be handed two goals in the last quarter of an hour to beat anyone. The performances are good enough for a top-two challenge. The game management is not, and Round 20 is the worst possible venue to find that out again.
