# Round 13 Digest: Seoul E-Land FC vs Seongnam FC

> *An early Seongnam red card opened the door, and Seoul E-Land walked through it. Park Jae-yong and Euller had the first half won, and Park Chang-hwan slammed the door shut after halftime.*

## The Round in One Paragraph

Seoul E-Land FC ended a two-game winless run with a 3-1 home win over [[Seongnam FC]] at [[Mokdong Stadium]], a result that nudged them into the promotion-playoff places and, briefly on the night, into second. The match was reshaped almost before it began: Seongnam's Park Soo-bin was sent off in the 7th minute for a studs-up tackle on [[Park Chang-hwan]], and E-Land controlled the first half through [[Park Jae-yong]]'s fifth goal of the season and a stunning Euller (에울레르) free kick in the 35th. The away side rallied through Kim Min-jae after the break, but [[Park Chang-hwan]] restored the two-goal cushion in the 84th. There was extra weight in the result, this was the same Seongnam side that ended E-Land's 2025 season in the promotion playoffs, and the symbolic revenge was sweetened by Euller marking his 50th Seoul E-Land appearance with a goal. The bigger worry was Osmar's early withdrawal: 19 minutes into his return from injury, he was off again.

## Match Report

[[Kim Do-gyun]] sent his team out in a 3-4-3 with [[Min Sung-jun]] (민성준) in goal. The back three drew the first headline: with captain [[Kim Oh-kyu]] (김오규) unavailable, Osmar (오스마르) returned to start alongside [[Park Jae-hwan]] (박재환) and [[Baek Ji-woong]] (백지웅). The wingback channels gave [[Kang Hyeon-je]] (강현재) the right and Oh In-pyo (오인표) the left, with [[Seo Jin-seok]] (서진석) and [[Park Chang-hwan]] (박창환) central. Up top, [[Park Jae-yong]] (박재용) returned to the central striker role after his Round 12 bench rotation, flanked by Cho Jun-hyun (조준현) on the right and Euller (에울레르) on the left. Three changes from Round 12: Osmar in for Kim Oh-kyu, Cho Jun-hyun in for Son Hyuk-chan, [[Park Jae-yong]] back in for [[Kim Hyun]].

The match's defining moment came in the 7th minute. Park Soo-bin, Seongnam's most industrious midfielder, went studs-up on [[Park Chang-hwan]]'s ankle in a scramble inside Seoul's half. Referee Park Jong-myung produced the straight red without hesitation; replay confirmed the contact was high and reckless. Seongnam, a team built on draw-prone organization, were now asked to defend that exact profile with one fewer body for 83 minutes.

Twelve minutes later, the night's second turning point. Osmar, contesting an aerial duel with Yoon Min-ho (윤민호), came down badly and could not continue. [[Kim Do-gyun]] hurried Son Hyuk-chan (손혁찬) on in the 19th; Osmar walked off favoring his ankle. For a player whose presence anchors set-piece defending and whose contract status is the swing factor for E-Land's 2026 leadership question, that withdrawal was the most worrying moment of the match, even with the scoreboard about to tilt.

The first goal arrived in the 23rd minute and was pure Euller assist line, even without an assist on the official sheet. He whipped a free kick into the Seongnam area, the delivery skipped through a crowded six-yard box, and [[Park Jae-yong]] reacted first to bundle home from close range. It was the kind of finish that does not generate highlight clips but does generate league tables: his fifth of the season, the team lead, the kind of scruffy goal a striker on form converts without thinking. The 2-0 in the 35th was less anonymous. Euller stood over a free kick from the left half-space and aimed a left-footed delivery at the far post, intending a cross. No one rose to meet it; it clipped past Lee Kwang-hyun (이광현) and into the net. Euller said afterward he expected a teammate to head it in; that no one did mattered less than the fact the ball found its way through.

The half-time scoreline read 2-0 and Seoul E-Land had played the eventful, controlled version of the match the [[2026-R13_Seongnam_FC_Preview|preview]] had urged. The second half was more uncomfortable. Seongnam, with nothing to lose and a backbone in Venício (베니시오) at center back, kept their shape and tried to win second balls in the middle third. The chance to draw blood arrived in the 62nd. Substitute Kim Min-jae, on from the bench, turned in the box and finished cleanly from the left side; 2-1, and the match suddenly had texture. Mokdong shifted from a comfortable lead into the familiar 2026 pattern of a late-game concession threatening to swallow control.

[[Kim Do-gyun]]'s response was decisive rather than reactive. Substitutions kept the legs fresh through the press; Cho Jun-hyun and Euller continued to threaten on quick transitions; [[Park Jae-yong]] stayed central as a pressing reference. The clinching goal in the 84th had the same fingerprints as the rest of the night: composure in a crowded box. [[Park Chang-hwan]] arrived at the top of the area with the ball running across him, planted his right foot, and drove a low shot that squeezed under Lee Kwang-hyun's gloves. His second of the campaign, his second meaningful contribution to a single match after drawing the red card in the 7th. 3-1, and the result was safe.

The final whistle prompted players from both sides to drop to the turf. Seongnam had played 83 minutes with ten and given honest effort; E-Land had finally turned numerical advantage into a clean professional win after recent matches where they had not.

### Player Performances

**Park Jae-yong (박재용):**
The team's leading scorer is leading now in the right way. Five goals from a striker rotated to the bench just one round ago is the kind of return that resolves a selection question for the rest of the spring. The R13 goal will not appear in compilation reels but it carried the same trait as his earlier strikes: he is consistently the first man to the second ball inside the box. In the post-match interview he leaned hard into the team-first frame ("if I score zero goals and we win, I'll be satisfied"), which is what a manager wants to hear from a forward whose numbers are climbing. He also reframed his move to E-Land as a "last chance" mentality, which lands differently when paired with five goals in 13.

**Euller (에울레르):**
The match's catalyst even before the goal. His free kicks created both opening half goals, the second by going in directly, and his movement up front pulled Seongnam's depleted shape further out of shape every time he drifted central. Was honest in his post-match assessment that Seoul "failed to capitalize" on the numerical advantage and "lacked composure", a tell that he sees the bigger room for improvement even on a 3-1 win.

> **Career milestone, Euller now sits on 15 goals + 15 assists in K League play.**
>
> All of those numbers, every single goal and every single assist, have come in a Seoul E-Land shirt. He arrived from Marítimo in January 2025 with no prior K League minutes; the R13 strike was his 15th career K League goal, paired with 15 career K League assists across 50 Seoul E-Land appearances. He marked appearance number 50 with a goal, which fits the player: "I want to become someone remembered in club history," he said afterward. A neat symmetry that the next attacking contribution will retire, but the underlying point is harder to retire: in less than 18 months, Euller has become the most productive Brazilian to wear the [[Seoul E-Land FC]] crest.

**Park Chang-hwan (박창환):**
Drew the red card in the 7th by being where he should be, and finished the match by scoring a goal that ended any reasonable Seongnam hope. The improvement in his late-game decision-making has been the quietest story of the season; the 84th-minute strike was patient under pressure rather than rushed. Second of the season; both have come at meaningful junctures.

**Osmar (오스마르):**
Nineteen minutes is not a referendum, but it is a worry. With the captain unavailable and the back three already light on natural set-piece presence, Osmar's withdrawal forces [[Kim Do-gyun]] to think hard about R14 organization. No injury timeline reported as of writing; the broadcast read it as ankle discomfort from an aerial duel.

**Baek Ji-woong (백지웅):**
Asked to run the back three in real time after Osmar's exit, and did the job without theater. He was first to scraps in the box on both Seongnam set pieces in the second half and ran clean stoppage minutes. Still only 21; still showing the season's most consistent defensive growth curve.

**Min Sung-jun (민성준):**
Mostly routine, but the one concession was a Kim Min-jae strike struck cleanly enough that there is no plausible save complaint. Distribution was tidier than in recent rounds; the back four in front of him is more settled than the late-game scrambles of Round 12.

## News and Transfers

The most quotable piece of post-match news was the milestone match for Euller, marked with a goal and an unusually candid set of comments. He framed the 50-appearance threshold in club-history terms, then immediately deflected credit to his teammates. The contract he signed in the offseason now reads even better than it did then.

The longer-running storyline is Osmar. He has been the swing veteran of the spring, returning, missing, returning again. The R13 19-minute outing is the second time he has had to come off in this period; if the next fortnight confirms a lingering ankle issue, the back-three rotation will need [[Kim Oh-kyu]] in a more permanent role and force [[Kim Do-gyun]] to lean further on Baek Ji-woong's positional flexibility.

[[Park Jae-yong]]'s post-match interview deserves its own paragraph. A striker leading the team in goals describing his preferred path to staying in the starting XI as "I'll just defend harder" is unusual, and it reads like a player who has internalized the cultural ask of this manager: that the work between the goals decides whether the goals get you on the team sheet next week.

## My Honest Read

This was a result that should have been straightforward and very nearly was. Playing 83 minutes with a numerical advantage at home against the 11th-placed side is the easiest fixture template a promotion contender will face all spring; that E-Land cashed it in for three points and a +2 margin is the right outcome, no more and no less. There is no honest version of this recap that pretends a 3-1 win over ten men erases the structural questions raised by the R11 0-3 in Asan and the R12 second-ball lapses against Yongin.

What did improve was finishing. The first goal was scrappy but the second was a delivery that found its way home, and the third was a clean strike under pressure. After three matches of seeing Euller, Park Jae-yong, and Park Chang-hwan generate chances without converting at the rate the underlying numbers suggested, the goals finally arrived together. If that recurs against Jeonnam, the table will move.

The longer concern walks off the pitch on its own. Osmar's withdrawal in the 19th minute is the second time in a short window that he has been unable to complete a match, and the depth chart behind him is not deep enough to absorb a multi-week absence without rerouting Baek Ji-woong out of a role he has been thriving in. If the next medical update reads ten days or more, the R14 trip to Gwangyang gets harder than the table makes it look.

But there is also something worth noting that the analytics will not capture. This was the same opponent that ended E-Land's 2025 season; the symbolic margin matters, even if the football was uneven. And it was Euller's 50th match in the shirt, marked with the kind of goal he has scored in this jersey and only in this jersey, all 15 of them, all 15 assists, all of it inside a single 18-month window. That is the bigger story underneath the table-position one. A player became Seoul E-Land's most prolific Brazilian today, and the club ended the night in the top three. Promotion math does not let either of those facts carry the burden alone, but together, on this night, they were enough.
