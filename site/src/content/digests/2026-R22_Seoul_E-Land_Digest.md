# Round 22 Digest: Seoul E-Land FC vs Ansan Greeners

> *Euller scored a worked corner, a free kick and a penalty, raised a crown to the man he says wears it, and dedicated the night to his wife in the stand. Seoul E-Land went behind at home and won anyway, which is the thing they could not do a month ago.*

## The Round in One Paragraph

Seoul E-Land beat Ansan Greeners 3-1 at [[Mokdong Stadium]] and every goal belonged to Euller, whose hat-trick was the first of his K League career. It did not start well. Ansan, fifteenth and fighting relegation, scored inside six minutes through Machop Chol and for a while looked exactly like the awkward low-block problem this site has spent two digests warning about. Then Seoul worked a rehearsed corner on 14 and Euller placed it in, and after the interval he beat the goalkeeper twice more, once from a direct free kick and once from the spot. The win takes Seoul to **40 points from 21**, keeps them second, and extends the unbeaten run to six. It also, finally, answers the question the last three previews kept asking: this team can break down a side that sits in, provided somebody puts the ball in the net from a set piece.

## Match Report

The night opened with a ceremony rather than a kickoff. [[Park Chang-hwan]], the club's vice-captain, was presented for his **100th appearance in a Seoul E-Land shirt**, with his mother and younger sibling on the pitch beside him and a commemorative shirt carrying his goals, assists and running numbers. He actually reached the milestone back in Round 19 against Cheonan, but the presentation had to wait, because he served a yellow-card suspension in Round 21. That also settles something this site published as an inference in the Round 22 preview: his absence at Hwaseong was accumulated bookings, confirmed here on the pitch.

Kim Do-gyun made four changes and the shape was the interesting part. [[Min Sung-jun]] started behind [[Bae Seo-jun]], [[Kim Oh-kyu]], [[Baek Ji-woong]] and [[Oh In-pyo]], with **Osmar pushed forward** into midfield alongside [[Alan Carius]] and [[Park Chang-hwan]], and [[Ahn Joo-wan]], [[Kim Hyun]] and Euller ahead of them. Osmar's position is the switch: when he drops the shape is a back three, when he steps up it is a back four with a screen. It flexed between the two all night.

The headline selection was [[Ahn Joo-wan]], handed his **first career start**. Our preview called that the single most informative team-sheet decision of the run-in, and Kim Do-gyun made it.

Ansan did not read the script. Choi Moon-sik kept the side that drew at Chungnam Asan and they came out playing, working the ball forward through the lines until Cho Ji-hun slipped a pass through and **Machop Chol cut inside and finished across [[Min Sung-jun]]**. It was a genuinely good goal, his fourth of the season and his second in consecutive matches, and it gave the visitors the one thing that makes a low block dangerous: a lead to protect.

For twenty minutes Seoul looked like the team from Hwaseong. Euller met a second ball and dragged a right-footed shot wide. [[Alan Carius]] scuffed one. Ansan broke and Lima was put clean through, and [[Min Sung-jun]] made a save that was ruled irrelevant by an offside flag but would have been the save of the match on another night.

The equaliser came from the training ground. [[Alan Carius]] took the corner short, the ball was worked back across, and **Euller placed a left-footed finish into the far corner** rather than hitting it. The commentary called it a fully prepared routine that came off at a hundred per cent, and that is what it looked like: nobody improvised.

Between then and the interval both goalkeepers earned their money. Tim Hartzell threw himself in front of a Euller effort with a tackle the broadcast called super. Lee Seung-bin, Ansan's goalkeeper, produced two saves that had no business being saves, the better of them a fingertip stop from an [[Ahn Joo-wan]] cross-shot delivered from an angle that should not have threatened. Half-time, 1-1.

Kim Do-gyun did not wait. [[Park Jae-yong]] and [[Son Hyuk-chan]] came on at the break, the leading scorer and a defender who can play wide, which is a substitution that says *we are winning this* rather than *we are managing this*.

The second goal was Euller alone. A free kick in a central position, no deflection, no scramble: he struck it to the exact spot he had picked and it went in off nobody at all. He said afterwards that this is what the set-piece work is for, aiming inside the frame so it counts even when it does not touch anybody, and that the same thing had happened in a previous home match. **He then made the crown gesture**, two hands lifted above his head. The broadcast read it as Euller crowning himself. He corrected that afterwards, and the correction is the better story: the crown is not his.

The third arrived from the penalty spot after Im Ji-min was penalised in the box, and Euller rolled it low and precisely inside the post while the goalkeeper guessed correctly and still could not reach it. Hat-trick, and the first of his K League career.

The last half hour was comfortable in a way Mokdong has not often been this season. [[Lee Ju-hyeok]] replaced [[Ahn Joo-wan]] and immediately looked lively. [[Park Chang-hwan]] was booked for arriving late on Lee Seung-bin as the goalkeeper came to claim. Euller went off on 84 to an ovation, replaced by [[Yoon Seok-ju]]. Ansan emptied their bench, five changes in all, and never seriously threatened a second.

## Player Performances

**Euller (에울레르):**
Three goals, three different ways: a rehearsed corner finished with placement rather than power, a direct free kick struck to a spot he had already chosen, and a penalty rolled inside the post. It is the first hat-trick of his K League career and it arrived in the fixture this site had flagged as the season's trap. The detail that matters is that none of the three came from open play. On a night when Ansan defended the middle competently, Euller simply took the game somewhere they could not defend.

**[[Park Chang-hwan]] (박창환):**
Played his hundredth match for the club on the night it was marked, drew the free kick that led to the second goal with a piece of technique in a crowded area, and generally ran the game from the left of the midfield three. Booked late for a challenge on the goalkeeper that the referee judged reckless. His return after suspension restored exactly what was missing at Hwaseong.

**[[Ahn Joo-wan]] (안주완):**
First career start, and he justified the argument for it inside twenty minutes. The cross-shot that forced Lee Seung-bin into the save of the match came from him, from an angle where most players cross. He did not score and it does not matter: the seventeen-year-old was picked because this team needed someone to attack the space behind and around a deep defence, and he did it from the first whistle.

**Osmar (오스마르):**
Deployed as the deepest midfielder rather than a centre-back, which let [[Kim Oh-kyu]] come in beside [[Baek Ji-woong]] and gave Seoul a body in the space Ansan wanted to play through. He also produced one of the reads of the night, taking a shorter line than the runner and getting to a through ball first. At thirty-seven, being moved into midfield to manage his legs is a sensible piece of squad management, and it worked.

**[[Min Sung-jun]] (민성준):**
Beaten by a good finish and otherwise untroubled, with one excellent stop from Lima that an offside flag made academic. Quiet nights are the goalkeeping equivalent of a clean sheet.

**Lee Seung-bin (이승빈), Ansan:**
Worth naming from the opposition. Two saves in the first half alone kept his side level, and Seoul needed a set piece, a free kick and a penalty to beat him. Ansan lost 3-1 and their goalkeeper was the reason it was not worse.

## News and Transfers

**Euller gave his post-match interview in Portuguese**, which this site is better placed than most to report, and it was the warmest thing anyone said all night.

On the win: the three points, he said, take Seoul into second place, and it was a very important match for them.

On the free kick, and this is the useful part tactically: *"a gente sempre tenta acertar na direção do gol"*, we always try to hit it towards the goal. He explained that when the squad works on set pieces they aim to put the ball inside the frame even if it touches nobody, that exactly this had happened in a previous home match, and that he was happy the ball did not hit anyone on the way in. The goal was not a fluke of a deflection that never came; it was the intended outcome.

On the crown celebration, and this is worth getting right because it was widely read the other way: the crown is **not** Euller crowning himself. *"Jesus é o meu rei"*, he said. Jesus is my king. The gesture points the crown upward, not inward. He added that his story is built on the Lord and that he gives the glory to God, because he knows he cannot do any of this alone.

And on who it was for: he dedicated the hat-trick to **his wife, who was in the ground**, and to his family watching at home.

**[[Park Chang-hwan]]'s 100th appearance** was formally marked before kickoff with his family present. The delay from Round 19 to Round 22 is explained by the Round 21 suspension, which this confirms was yellow-card accumulation.

**Nothing else moved.** No transfers, and [[Park Chang-hwan]]'s booking is the only new disciplinary note.

## Tactical and Strategic Watch

**The low-block problem has one answer, and it is set pieces.** Three goals, none from open play. Seoul have now failed to break a deep defence in open play in consecutive home-ish contexts, and won anyway because Euller is lethal from a standing ball and the corner routines are properly coached. That is a real weapon, not a fluke, but it is worth stating plainly: the open-play problem did not get solved tonight, it got outflanked.

**Ahn Joo-wan started and the shape looked different.** The preview argued that starting him was the specific solution to the specific problem rather than sentiment. He forced the best save of the game inside the first half. Whether it holds against Paju is the next question, but the selection itself is a change in how this team attacks a deep line.

**Osmar in midfield is the workload answer.** We have been asking since Round 18 how Seoul manage a thirty-seven-year-old playing everything. Moving him forward and bringing [[Kim Oh-kyu]] into the back four is more elegant than resting him, and it puts his passing in the part of the pitch where a low block has to be unpicked.

**The preview graded honestly: seven of eleven.** We called [[Min Sung-jun]], [[Bae Seo-jun]], [[Baek Ji-woong]], [[Oh In-pyo]], [[Park Chang-hwan]], [[Alan Carius]] and Euller, and we had Osmar starting although in defence rather than midfield. We missed [[Kim Oh-kyu]], [[Kim Hyun]] and [[Ahn Joo-wan]] coming in, and we had [[Yoon Seok-ju]], [[Lee Ju-hyeok]] and [[Park Jae-yong]] starting when all three began on the bench. The result call was right.

## Looking Ahead

## My Honest Read

I have written three times this month that Seoul E-Land cannot break down a team that sits in, and tonight they beat one 3-1 without scoring a single goal from open play.

Both halves of that are worth holding on to. The win was real, the margin was fair, and going behind at home to a relegation-threatened side and turning it around inside forty-five minutes is exactly the character this squad was accused of lacking in June. Six unbeaten, forty points, second place.

But look at how the goals came. A rehearsed corner, a free kick, a penalty. Ansan defended the middle of the pitch competently for most of the match and their goalkeeper made two saves that should have kept it level longer than it stayed level. Take Euller's dead-ball ability out of this team and the same fixture is a frustrating 1-1.

That is not a criticism so much as an identification. Seoul have found a way to beat the block, and it happens to be the way that depends most heavily on one player. Euller has now scored eight in the league and is a genuine contender for the division's player of the season. He is also thirty-one, on a run of form no forward sustains forever, and the club's promotion push is currently balanced on his left foot.

The encouraging part is [[Ahn Joo-wan]]. He started, he immediately created the best chance of the first half, and he is the closest thing this squad has to a second answer. Twelve matches left, seven at Mokdong. Keep picking him.
