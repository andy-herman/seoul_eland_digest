# Round 21 Digest: Seoul E-Land FC vs Hwaseong FC

> *Nobody scored, and that was the point. Two coaches spent the week working out how to switch the other one off, and both of them succeeded. [[Min Sung-jun]] made the save of Seoul E-Land's season to keep it that way.*

## The Round in One Paragraph

Seoul E-Land drew 0-0 away at Hwaseong FC in a heatwave and took a point that looked ordinary on the ticker and felt like something else entirely if you watched it. This was the back half of the hardest week on the calendar, five days after winning at second-placed Busan IPark, against a Hwaseong side on three straight wins who had put five past Daegu in their previous home outing. Seoul had sixty per cent of the ball and did almost nothing with it. Hwaseong had the better chances after the interval and found [[Min Sung-jun]] in the way, most memorably with a save from a Demetrius header at the end of the first half. Leonard Pllana struck the frame of the goal with a left-footed shot that was flagged offside anyway. [[Ahn Joo-wan]] ran in behind at the other end and was flagged as he finished. The point moves Seoul to 37 from 20 and, because Busan IPark lost at Yongin the same night, it puts Seoul back into second place on their own. It also quietly ended a run: for the first time since Round 11, across eight league matches, Seoul E-Land did not score.

## Match Report

Kim Do-gyun named ten of the eleven players our preview called. The back four was [[Bae Seo-jun]], Osmar, [[Baek Ji-woong]] and [[Oh In-pyo]] in front of [[Min Sung-jun]]. The one change from the side that won at Gudeok was in midfield, where [[Park Chang-hwan]] dropped out and [[Cho Jun-hyun]] came in alongside [[Yoon Seok-ju]], with [[Alan Carius]] the most advanced of the three and [[Lee Ju-hyeok]] and Euller either side of [[Park Jae-yong]]. The data providers logged it as a 4-3-3; the Korean broadcast described [[Alan Carius]] playing as a second-line attacking midfielder ahead of two holders, which is the 4-2-3-1 we predicted. Both readings describe the same eleven players standing in roughly the same places, and the distinction mattered less than the fact that none of them ran beyond the last defender.

Cha Du-ri set Hwaseong in the 3-4-3 that has become their signature: Kim Seung-gun in goal, Park Jun-seo, Aleksandar Vojnovic and Jang Min-jun across the back, a midfield four of Im Chan-yeol, Lee Jong-sung, Jeon Seong-jin and Kim Dae-hwan, and Pllana, Stanislav Ilyuchenko and Demetrius ahead of them. They were missing Park Kyung-min and Jegal Jae-min to yellow-card accumulation, and it says something about the depth Cha has assembled that you would not have known from watching.

It was played in serious heat, which both managers raised afterwards and which showed in the last twenty minutes.

The first half belonged to Seoul in the way that means very little. They had something like sixty per cent of the ball and spent it going sideways. Hwaseong's pressing was the reason, and the co-commentator put his finger on why it worked: pressing is not running, it is a system, and Hwaseong moved as one unit every time Seoul tried to play through the middle. When Seoul did get out, the ball tended to arrive in a crowded box with ten green shirts already back. Euller worked a free kick into Kim Seung-gun's hands. [[Alan Carius]] had a shot blocked. The best of it came when [[Bae Seo-jun]] crossed, [[Park Jae-yong]] nodded the ball down, and Euller met it on the volley and put it over the crossbar. That was Seoul's high point in forty-five minutes of having the ball.

And then, right at the end of the half, the match nearly turned. It started with Lee Jong-sung winning a challenge in midfield and ended with Kim Dae-hwan delivering a cross that Demetrius met perfectly, and it should have been a goal. [[Min Sung-jun]] threw an arm out and got enough on it. Nothing about the save was tidy and everything about it was decisive. It is the only reason this digest is about a draw.

The first change came two minutes into the second half, and it was not tactical. [[Bae Seo-jun]] had spent the first half in a series of collisions, first with Kim Dae-hwan and then with Lee Jong-sung, holding his chest after the second, and he did not last past the 47th minute. [[Kim Joo-hwan]] replaced him.

The second half was Hwaseong's. Cha pushed his line up, sent Im Chan-yeol and Kim Dae-hwan forward together instead of alternating them, brought on Lee Rae-jun to raise the pressing intensity, and for twenty minutes Seoul could not get out. Pllana, who scored four in the previous round, turned away from Osmar and hit a left-footed shot that beat [[Min Sung-jun]] and struck the frame of the goal. The flag was already up. At the other end [[Ahn Joo-wan]] ran in behind, got there, and was flagged as he finished. Twice the scoreboard flickered and twice it did not move.

Kim Do-gyun made the double change on 62, and only half of it was a choice: [[Cho Jun-hyun]] had gone down in an aerial collision with Lee Jong-sung around the 53rd minute and did not last much longer, going off alongside [[Lee Ju-hyeok]] for [[Francisco Geraldes]] and [[Ahn Joo-wan]]. Lee Jong-sung did not make it out of the collision either and was withdrawn on 58. Euller followed for [[Kang Hyeon-je]], then [[Oh In-pyo]] for [[Kim Oh-kyu]] with eight minutes left. The captain was booked a minute after coming on, which is the sort of detail that tells you what the closing stages felt like. None of it produced a clear chance. [[Alan Carius]] hit one from distance that went wide, [[Kang Hyeon-je]] got to the byline once, and that was the sum of Seoul's second half.

The closing stretch belonged to Kim Dae-hwan, who had run the right flank for ninety minutes and was still doing it at the end, and to a Hwaseong side who kept arriving in the box without ever finding the final pass. Jang Min-jun, a center-back by trade, ended up in Seoul's penalty area heading a cross over the bar. The final whistle came with the scoreboard where it started, in front of 2,392 people who had sat through it in the heat. Both benches applauded. Neither looked satisfied.

## Player Performances

**[[Min Sung-jun]] (민성준):**
Man of the match by a distance, and it is not close enough to argue about. The save from Demetrius was the moment of the game and possibly of Seoul's season, an instinctive full-extension stop from a header struck properly from close range. He punched well under pressure on two Hwaseong crosses, held everything he could hold, and never gave the hosts a second ball to attack. The commentary line late on was that there were no gaps in him tonight. On an evening when Seoul's attack contributed nothing, their goalkeeper took a point off the side sitting fourth going into the round, on his own.

**Osmar (오스마르):**
Thirty-seven years old, a third straight full ninety with the last two five days apart, and he did not visibly fade until the closing stages. He was beaten once by Pllana's turn, which is going to happen against a striker in that kind of form, and the shot hit the woodwork with the flag already up. Otherwise he was the reason Hwaseong's front three could not settle. Worth noting: at least one Korean outlet reported before kickoff that he had been ruled out with a hamstring problem. He started, played the full match, and was visibly involved throughout, so the report was either wrong or overtaken by his recovery. The workload question stays open either way.

**[[Cho Jun-hyun]] (조준현):**
Handed [[Park Chang-hwan]]'s shirt in the toughest away fixture on the schedule and did the defensive half of the job well. He broke up play, he stayed disciplined next to [[Yoon Seok-ju]], and he came off nine minutes after an aerial collision that also ended Lee Jong-sung's night. What he did not offer was the forward passing that [[Park Chang-hwan]] has made his own this season, and in a match Seoul lost by failing to progress the ball, that gap was the story.

**Euller (에울레르):**
The commentary opened by noting he is close to a double-double for the season and that this could be the night it landed. It was not. He got Seoul's best chance and put it over, took a decent free kick, and drifted for long stretches while Hwaseong doubled up on him. He was withdrawn for [[Kang Hyeon-je]] with twenty minutes left, which tells you how the night had gone. A quiet game from him is now genuinely unusual, which is its own kind of compliment.

**[[Park Jae-yong]] (박재용):**
Marked out of the match by Boinovic, who followed him everywhere and won more of the duels than he lost. Seoul's leading scorer had one meaningful touch, the header down for Euller's volley, and almost nothing else. Not every center-forward performance is a failure when the service does not arrive, and tonight the service did not arrive.

**[[Ahn Joo-wan]] (안주완):**
Nearly half an hour off the bench and the clearest evidence yet that the teenager is ready for more than cameos. He ran in behind almost immediately, which is exactly the thing nobody else on the pitch was doing, and finished it calmly before the flag went up. He got into another shooting position from the flank later. Whether the ball crossed the line is not in the written record and the offside call makes it moot. The point stands anyway: the one Seoul player who attacked the space behind Hwaseong's line is the one who did not start.

## News and Transfers

**Kim Do-gyun was unusually candid afterwards.** His read was that his players were carrying the weight of the winning run: "there was an obsession with winning three in a row, and I could see the players playing with that psychological weight." He also said he thought from around the seventy-fifth minute that Seoul needed to go and win it, and called the result "a valuable point against Hwaseong, who play good football." A manager naming the mental burden of a good run out loud is not something you hear every week.

**Cha Du-ri thought his side lacked bite early.** He said Hwaseong's willingness in duels had dropped from previous weeks, that he addressed it at half-time, and that bringing on Lee Rae-jun was specifically to raise the pressing intensity. His summary of the whole night was the sharpest thing either manager said: "for both sides, there was too much to lose if the result went either way. We were very cautious from the start."

**The Osmar question.** A pre-match report had him missing with a hamstring issue. He started and finished. Worth watching over the Round 22 build-up rather than treating as settled.

**Nothing else moved.** No transfers, no new suspensions beyond [[Kim Oh-kyu]]'s booking, and [[Park Chang-hwan]]'s absence has not been publicly explained.

## Tactical and Strategic Watch

**The preview called ten of eleven, and the shape is arguable in our favor for once.** We predicted a 4-2-3-1 with [[Alan Carius]] behind [[Park Jae-yong]]. The stats providers logged a 4-3-3; the Korean broadcast described exactly the structure we called, with [[Alan Carius]] advanced off two holders. The personnel miss was [[Park Chang-hwan]], replaced by [[Cho Jun-hyun]]. After getting the shape flatly wrong in Round 18 and Round 20, we will take it.

**Seoul do not have a plan B against a low block.** This is the third time this season a side has sat in, stayed compact and dared Seoul to break them down, and the third time Seoul have had the ball and no idea what to do with it. The commentary produced the best description of the problem anyone has managed: Seoul were making calls that nobody was picking up. The passes were there. The runs were not. [[Ahn Joo-wan]]'s twenty minutes were the exception that proved it, because the one time somebody ran in behind, Seoul created a goal.

**The bench is now genuinely deep, and it changed nothing.** Four attacking substitutions, including a returning [[Francisco Geraldes]], produced one shot. Squad depth is only an asset if the shape lets it play.

## Looking Ahead

Round 22 brings Ansan Greeners to [[Mokdong Stadium]] on August 16, and after a fortnight of Busan away and Hwaseong away, this is the fixture the schedule owed them. It is also the sort of match this Seoul side has historically found ways to complicate. We will find out which kind of team this is.

## My Honest Read

A 0-0 at Hwaseong is a good result and a bad performance, and both of those things are true at the same time.

Take the away trip in isolation and it is fine. Fourth place, three straight wins, five goals in their last home match, and Seoul went there five days after a hard win at Busan, missing their best passer from midfield, and did not concede. That is a promotion side's away point.

Watch the ninety minutes and something less comfortable shows up. Seoul had the ball for most of the first half and generated one shot worth the name. They were second best after the break and were rescued by their goalkeeper. Strip out [[Min Sung-jun]] and this is a defeat, and it is the second time this month the goalkeeper has been the difference.

The thing I keep coming back to is [[Ahn Joo-wan]]. A teenager came on, ran in behind immediately, got there, and was flagged by a margin you could measure in inches. Nobody else tried it all night. Seoul have built a squad that can pass around a block and no longer has anyone who will run past one, which is what happens when your quickest outlet enlists in the middle of a promotion race. Byun Kyung-jun is at Nonsan and the problem he leaves behind showed up eight days later, exactly on schedule.

The other thing worth sitting with is what Kim Do-gyun said. He did not blame the heat, or the pitch, or the referee. He said his players were carrying an obsession with winning three in a row and that he could see it in how they played. That is a manager describing a team that has started to feel the weight of being good, which is a problem Seoul E-Land have not had to deal with before and which the run-in is going to test properly.

Thirty-seven points from twenty games, second in the division, and they got there by not scoring. This team is going to face six or seven more sides who sit in and invite them on, and the answer cannot keep being that [[Min Sung-jun]] is excellent.
