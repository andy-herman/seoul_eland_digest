# Round 26 Digest: Seoul E-Land FC vs Suwon Samsung Bluewings

> *The biggest crowd Seoul E-Land have ever drawn to a league match watched them concede from a corner in the ninth minute and spend the next eighty-one failing to score past a reserve goalkeeper. Eighteen shots, eleven corners, a header off the post, and a 0-1 that ends the unbeaten run, ends the title talk, and drops Seoul to fourth on goals scored with the second-placed side due at Mokdong in a week, and the captain suspended for it.*

## The Round in One Paragraph

Seoul E-Land lost 0-1 to Suwon Samsung Bluewings in front of 11,077 at Mokdong, the largest attendance the club has ever recorded for a league fixture. Dubickas scored on 9 minutes, meeting Kim Min-woo's headed knockdown from a worked corner routine after [[Min Sung-jun]] had saved his first effort seconds earlier, and Suwon, missing two starters and their first-choice goalkeeper, then did what the best defense in the division does: sat in, absorbed 61 percent possession and 18 shots, and let Kim Min-jun make six saves. [[Park Jae-yong]] hit the post with a header on 56. Kang Hyun-muk had a second goal disallowed on 64 after an on-field review found Pesin offside and in the goalkeeper's line. [[Park Chang-hwan]] collected his eighth yellow card of the season and misses Saturday's match against Daegu. Kim Do-gyun's verdict afterward was blunt: the title has drifted away, and the aim is now second or third. Around the result, everything went wrong. Daegu beat Yongin 3-1 to go second, Suwon FC drew with ten-man Cheonan but still moved above Seoul on goals scored, and Hwaseong and Busan both won. Seoul are fourth on 45 with seven to play, one behind Daegu, who visit Mokdong on September 19.

## Match Report

Kim Do-gyun named the same eleven that won at Cheongju eight days earlier, a 4-3-3 with [[Min Sung-jun]]; [[Oh In-pyo]], [[Baek Ji-woong]], Osmar, [[Bae Seo-jun]]; [[Park Chang-hwan]] captaining from midfield beside [[Yoon Seok-ju]] and [[Alan Carius]]; Euller, [[Park Jae-yong]] and [[Ahn Joo-wan]] in front. Lee Jung-hyo's Suwon were without Ko Seung-beom (torn fascia), Bruno Silva (adductor) and goalkeeper Kim Jun-hong, away with Korea at the Asian Games, so Kim Min-jun started for the second week running behind a 4-4-2 of Lee Geon-hee, Ko Jong-hyun, Hong Jung-ho and Lee Sang-min, with Luis and Kang Sung-jin wide, Jung Ho-yeon and Kim Min-woo central, and Dubickas making his first league start alongside Kang Hyun-muk. The crowd was 11,077, roughly 5,000 of them in blue: the club had opened a neutral section for this match only and it sold out with the away end in under seven minutes.

The pre-match word from Kim Do-gyun had been about avenging the opening-day defeat in Suwon and about controlling the wide players, Luis and Kang Sung-jin. The goal came from neither. It came from the sequence he said afterward he had feared most: an early mistake before the team had settled. Kim Min-woo launched a ball to Luis on the left, Luis slipped Dubickas in, Min Sung-jun blocked, and the corner that followed was a rehearsed one. Jung Ho-yeon played it short to Kang Sung-jin, back to Lee Geon-hee on the edge, and his cross went behind the defensive line to where Kim Min-woo had drifted. His header came down for Dubickas, unmarked at the far post, to finish right-footed. Nine minutes. Seoul appealed for a foul on the knockdown and the referee checked; the goal stood. The broadcast's analyst said it had been executed exactly as Suwon had prepared it, and nobody in a Seoul shirt could disagree.

For twenty minutes after that Suwon threatened to make it a rout. Kang Sung-jin drove in from the right and was saved on 11 and again on 17, with Luis heading the second rebound wide. On 19 Min Sung-jun miskicked a goal kick straight to Suwon, Luis ran onto it and shot, and the goalkeeper redeemed himself with the save. Two more stops from Luis followed, one of them a full-stretch dive the commentary called a super save. Kim Do-gyun's own summary was that "the mistakes began with the goalkeeper's build-up" and that the side found its footing only after the half hour. It did. Park Jae-yong headed at Kim Min-jun on 24, Euller shot from distance on 29, and Park Chang-hwan and Carius both had efforts saved on 43. In between, on 41, Park Chang-hwan gave the ball away, grabbed Luis as he broke, and brought him down from behind. The yellow card was his eighth of the season. Under the K League rule, a fifth caution earns a one-match ban and every third one after that earns another, so it was the second suspension of his year and the one that removes him from the Daegu match.

Kim Do-gyun used both of his prepared changes at halftime. [[Ahn Joo-wan]], the 17-year-old who had spent the first half being jeered on every touch by the Suwon supporters he grew up among at Maetan, came off for [[Kim Hyun]]. [[Bae Seo-jun]] came off for [[Son Hyuk-chan]]. The manager said later that he had spent two days preparing for the possibility of chasing the game and used all of it. Suwon answered by pulling into a five-man defense, and Seoul's best moment of the afternoon came on 56: Carius delivered from the right, Park Jae-yong met it, and the header came back off the inside of the right post. Kim Min-jun, who was beaten, said afterward it had been the longest second half of his year. [[Kim Oh-kyu]] replaced [[Yoon Seok-ju]] on 59. Suwon brought on Pesin and Han Hyun-seo on 56, and on 64 Luis crossed from the left, Kang Hyun-muk headed, Min Sung-jun saved, and Kang followed up to score. The referee went to the monitor and ruled it out: Pesin, in an offside position, had interfered with the goalkeeper. The Suwon bench was baffled and so, judging by the debate afterward, were plenty of neutrals, since Pesin had not touched the ball and was arguably behind Son Hyuk-chan in Min's line of sight. It stayed 0-1.

Gabriel came on for Carius on 69, cut inside from the left and shot into the side netting when the pass was on. Baek Ji-woong went off injured on 78, [[Cho Jun-hyun]] replacing him, and the severity was not reported. Kim Hyun's header was saved on 84 and Kim Oh-kyu headed the resulting Son Hyuk-chan corner wide of the far post, a free header in a good position. Luis headed at Min Sung-jun on 89, Gabriel headed at Kim Min-jun on 90, and Kim Oh-kyu and Luis were booked in stoppage time. The final numbers, 18 shots to 9, 7 on target to Suwon's 3, 11 corners to 2, an expected-goals figure of 1.74 without a goal, read like the numbers of the winning side. Suwon have conceded 20 goals in 25 matches and none in their last two. This is what they do, and Seoul had been warned.

## Player Performances

**[[Min Sung-jun]] (민성준):**
A contradiction of an afternoon. One of the six or seven best saves of his season, the dive to keep out Luis in the first half, and the stop that forced the disallowed goal. Also the miskicked goal kick on 19 that gave Luis a free shot, and a first half his manager described, without naming him, as beginning with errors from the goalkeeper's build-up. A clean sheet at Cheongju before this, one goal conceded, and the goal was not his fault. The build-up was.

**[[Park Jae-yong]] (박재용):**
Nine goals and none since August. The header off the post on 56 was the best chance Seoul created and he did everything right with it. He also became the focal point of Sports Chosun's midweek piece on fatigue in the front line. He told FourFourTwo before the Daegu match that the game is "like a war" and that the pressure is now "a luxury we cannot afford." Saturday will be his 100th K League appearance.

**[[Park Chang-hwan]] (박창환):**
The captain's booking was the kind he has collected all year, a foul born of frustration after his own turnover, and the timing was the worst possible. His eighth yellow costs him the match that now decides whether Seoul are second or fourth. His shot on 43 was saved. His absence on Saturday is the single biggest selection problem of the run-in.

**[[Ahn Joo-wan]] (안주완):**
Substituted at halftime after a first half in which he touched the ball rarely and was whistled by the Suwon end whenever he did. He said afterward that it affected him, that he felt shrunken with the Suwon bench and their supporters on that side, and that it was his first experience of anything like it. He is seventeen. His manager said the change was about his state of mind against a top side, not his fitness. Both were honest, and the club's handling of the next few weeks matters more than the ninety minutes did.

**[[Alan Carius]] (까리우스):**
The cross for the header that hit the post, a shot saved on 43, and the midfield's most consistent carrier until he came off on 69. On a day when the team's problem was the final ball, he delivered the best one.

**Kim Min-jun (김민준, Suwon):**
The opposition goalkeeper is in this section because he decided the match: six saves, every one of Seoul's seven shots on target, and a manager who called him the best player on his team afterward. He had been out for three months before Suwon's previous match. Seoul's afternoon was a case study in how a good reserve goalkeeper on a good day beats an expected goals figure.

## Fan Reaction

The build-up produced the atmosphere the club has been chasing for years, and the result produced the argument.

The ticketing story dominated the week before: the neutral section the club opened for this fixture and the away allocation sold out together in under seven minutes, the first away sellout since March 2025, and by Saturday morning the advance sale was around 10,000. The final gate of 11,077 beat the previous club league record of 9,644, set against the same opponent in March 2025, and sits behind only the 13,205 for the promotion playoff against Jeonbuk in December 2024. Star News ran a photo piece on the Suwon end under the headline "feels like our home, doesn't it?", which Seoul supporters did not enjoy. On the pitch the Suwon fans booed Ahn Joo-wan from the first minute, a reaction some reporters tied to his breaking of records held by a Suwon academy graduate; Suwon supporters online said it was simpler than that, he left.

After the match the discussion split three ways. One strand was the Pesin decision, which Seoul fans conceded was a lucky break and which the namu wiki editors flagged as a possible refereeing error in Suwon's favor being overturned in Seoul's. A second was the goalkeeper, with the first-half build-up errors drawing the sharpest comments and the saves the most grudging praise. The third, and the loudest by Sunday evening, was the table: Seoul had gone from second to fourth in 26 hours without losing again, and the phrase in every thread was that Saturday against Daegu is now worth more than the Suwon match was. Suwon supporters, for their part, spent Sunday cheering for Cheonan.

## Tactical and Strategic Watch

**The corner routine.** Suwon's goal was a short corner played back to the edge and crossed behind the defensive line to a runner arriving late, with the striker waiting for the knockdown. It is a pattern designed against a zonal-leaning defense that steps up together, and it is exactly the shape Seoul defend corners in. The concern is not the goal but the repetition: the Seongnam equalizer in Round 24 came from a corner too, and Suwon had scored from a set piece against Seoul in February. Three of the last four goals Seoul have conceded have come from dead balls.

**Seven on target, none in.** The expected-goals number of 1.74 is Seoul's best home figure of the second half and produced nothing. Half of that is Kim Min-jun. The other half is the type of chance: five of the seven were headers or first-time shots from crosses into a crowded box, the kind a low block invites. Seoul's best chance of the day, the post, came from a cross too. What was missing was anyone taking the ball past a defender inside the box. Ahn Joo-wan is the player who does that and he was gone by the second half; Gabriel is the other, and he arrived on 69.

**The Daegu selection.** Without Park Chang-hwan, the midfield three picks itself only if Kim Do-gyun trusts Cho Jun-hyun or Osmar in the center. Osmar in midfield was the August solution and it cost the defense its best aerial presence, which against Deckers and Seraphim is a real cost. The cleanest answer is Cho Jun-hyun beside Yoon Seok-ju and Carius, with Kim Oh-kyu at center-back if Baek Ji-woong is not fit. It is a weaker midfield than the one that lost on Saturday, against a better attack.

## My Honest Read

Seoul did not play badly. They played into exactly the match Suwon wanted, and the reason that happened is the ninth minute, and the reason the ninth minute happened is the same reason the Seongnam goal happened: set-piece defending that steps up as a unit and leaves a runner behind the line. Fix that and the eighteen shots are enough on most days. Do not fix it and Deckers, who is two meters tall and scored twice last weekend, is next.

The bigger picture is uncomfortable. Seoul have gone from second to fourth without losing ground on the leaders in any meaningful sense; the title was five points away last Saturday and is eight now, and it was probably never really there. What has changed is the race for second. Daegu are a point ahead with a game each in hand for nobody, Suwon FC are level with a game in hand and 47 goals, and both of them are projected above Seoul by a model that was projecting Seoul above Daegu a week ago. Saturday is the last match Seoul can win that directly moves a rival. After it, the club is at the mercy of Suwon FC's calendar, which is six of eight at home against the bottom half. That is not a good place to be, and the way out is three points against Daegu without the captain.
