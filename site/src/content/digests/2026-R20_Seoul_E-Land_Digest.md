# Round 20 Digest: Seoul E-Land FC vs Busan IPark

> *Byun Kyung-jun came off the bench with a shaved head, one day before enlisting, and set up the winner. Park Jae-yong scored it and gave it to him. Seoul E-Land went to the second-placed side and came from behind to win.*

## The Round in One Paragraph

This was the best night of Seoul E-Land's season and it was not close. At Gudeok Stadium, against the club that has led K League 2 for most of the year, Seoul went a goal down to a Cristian Renato finish minutes after Busan IPark emptied their bench at half time, and then did the thing they have not done all season: they responded instead of collapsing. [[Baek Ji-woong]] headed in an inswinging corner to level it, Osmar (오스마르) threw a knee in front of a shot that was going in, and then Euller (에울레르) switched the play to Byun Kyung-jun (변경준), who carried it into the box and crossed for [[Park Jae-yong]] to win it. Byun Kyung-jun played that match with a shaved head because he enlists for military service tomorrow; he sleeps in Busan tonight and reports to Nonsan in the morning. [[Park Jae-yong]] dedicated the goal and the assist to him afterwards and called him the biggest legend in the club's history. Seoul move to 36 points from 19, level with Busan IPark and ahead of them on goal difference, which puts Seoul E-Land second in K League 2 and one point off the top.

## Match Report

[[Kim Do-gyun]] picked a 4-2-3-1 and picked it well. [[Min Sung-jun]] (민성준) started behind a back four of [[Bae Seo-jun]] (배서준), Osmar (오스마르), [[Baek Ji-woong]] (백지웅), and [[Oh In-pyo]] (오인표). [[Yoon Seok-ju]] (윤석주) and [[Park Chang-hwan]] (박창환) formed the double pivot, Lee Ju-hyuk (이주혁), Alan Carius (알랑 카리우스), and Euller (에울레르) played across the line behind [[Park Jae-yong]] (박재용). Two changes from Round 19: [[Park Jae-yong]] and Lee Ju-hyuk in for [[Kim Hyun]] and Byun Kyung-jun.

A note on our own preview, since it is only fair to grade it. The predicted XI named all eleven starters correctly, including the calls that Byun Kyung-jun would drop out and [[Park Jae-yong]] would be promoted. The shape was the miss: we said 4-3-3 with Carius in the midfield three, and Kim Do-gyun instead pushed Carius up as the number ten in a 4-2-3-1. We also had Busan down as a 4-4-2 side; they lined up 3-4-3, the same shape as their previous match, with three changes in the front line. Right players, wrong shapes, on both sides of the pitch.

The first half was the away performance the preview asked for and did not really expect. Seoul controlled the ball, kept the game narrow, and refused to let Gudeok become the shootout Busan have been playing all season. Osmar clipped a lovely early ball over the top for Euller that was flagged offside but showed the intent. [[Park Chang-hwan]] arrived late from midfield onto an [[Oh In-pyo]] pass and dragged his shot wide. Carius cut inside and shot wide. Lee Ju-hyuk collided with Koo Sang-min (구상민) in the box in a moment Seoul briefly wanted looked at, and the game went on.

Busan carried a threat through Kim Chan (김찬), who had a spell of four attempts in quick succession and forced the save of the half. [[Min Sung-jun]] got across to turn away his best effort, then reacted superbly again when Ahn Yeon-beom (안연범) struck first time from a cut-back. Gutemburg (구템베르그) went into the book. Half time, goalless, and the shape of the match was exactly what Seoul wanted.

Cho Sung-hwan (조성환) did not accept it. Busan made three substitutions at the interval, sending on Cristian Renato, Gabriel, and Baek Ga-on for Gutemburg, Kim Se-hun (김세훈), and Kim Chan, and inside seven minutes the changes had produced a goal. Gabriel beat his man and worked the space, Renato arrived, and on 52 minutes the finish was clean. One-nil, the crowd up, and Seoul facing the exact scenario that has ruined their season: chasing a game in the last half hour, away from home, against the best attack in the division.

What followed was the most encouraging twenty minutes Seoul have played this year. [[Oh In-pyo]] surged infield and forced a save. [[Bae Seo-jun]] made way for Kim Joo-hwan (김주환). And then, on 72 minutes, the equaliser, and it came from the routine Seoul have been threatening with all season: an inswinging corner, attacked properly, and [[Baek Ji-woong]] getting his head to it. Twenty-one years old, and the goal that started the comeback at the ground where the league leaders were beaten four days earlier.

Busan had their moments after that and Seoul survived them, which is itself new. Kim Hee-seung (김희승) struck the post with his left foot. A scramble in the six-yard box saw [[Oh In-pyo]] hack one clear, [[Min Sung-jun]] punch another away, and Jeon Seong-jin (전성진) meet the loose ball only for Osmar to fling a knee in the way and block it on the line. That was a goal, and Osmar decided it was not.

The winner was pure quality and it belonged to a man who is about to disappear for eighteen months. Euller picked the ball up and switched it left, a long, precise diagonal into space. Byun Kyung-jun took it down, drove into the box, kept his head up, and pulled it back. [[Park Jae-yong]] did what he has done all season and put it away. Two-one, and Seoul had come from behind to beat the second-placed team in their own stadium.

[[Kim Do-gyun]] closed it out properly for once, bringing on [[Ahn Joo-wan]] (안주완) and [[Kim Oh-kyu]] (김오규), the latter for the goalscorer [[Baek Ji-woong]] on 88, and shifting to a back five. Chico Geraldes (제랄데스) was booked for a late challenge, [[Min Sung-jun]] picked up a card of his own in the fifth minute of stoppage time for the kind of goalkeeping that wins away matches, Gabriel curled one just wide in the final seconds, and the whistle went with Seoul E-Land still ahead. After the last two rounds, that sentence is the whole story.

## Player Performances

**Park Jae-yong (박재용):**
The winner, and the interview that will be replayed. He has now scored in four of his last five appearances and continues to be the most reliable finisher in the division's form charts. Asked about his own season he credited the manager for giving him the chance and said he treats it desperately. Then he gave the night away: he called Byun Kyung-jun the biggest legend in Seoul E-Land's history, said he had almost never seen a player take the pitch the day before enlisting, and dedicated both the assist and his goal to him. He also thanked his parents and his agents for standing beside him when it was hard.

**Byun Kyung-jun (변경준):**
A substitute appearance with a shaved head, less than twenty-four hours before reporting for eighteen months of military service, and he produced the assist that beat the league's second-placed club. He sleeps in Busan tonight and travels to Nonsan tomorrow. Back in May, when the enlistment was confirmed, he told reporters he felt sorry to be leaving at such an important time and set himself a target of ten attacking contributions before he went. He finishes on three goals and two assists, the last of them the one that won this. He did not reach the number, and it is hard to imagine a supporter who cares.

**Baek Ji-woong (백지웅):**
The equaliser, from the corner routine that has been building all season, and another defensive performance that belied his age. Twenty-one, scoring important goals from set pieces, and now the most consistent defender in a back line that just kept the division's best attack to one.

**Osmar (오스마르):**
A block with the knee on the goal line that was worth as much as any goal on the night, plus the early passing range that set the tone. Thirty-seven years old, three full matches into a heavy stretch, and still the man who throws himself in front of it.

**Min Sung-jun (민성준):**
Two outstanding saves in the first half, from Kim Chan and then Ahn Yeon-beom, and a punch in the second-half scramble that kept the game level long enough for Seoul to win it. He was beaten by a good finish and nothing else. His best match of the season.

**Euller (에울레르):**
Quieter than usual in the final third and then decisive in one moment, the switch of play that created the winner. Busan double-marked him for long spells; the assist for the assist was his answer.

**Alan Carius (알랑 카리우스):**
Played as the ten rather than in the midfield three and looked lively without finding the finish, shooting wide twice and slipping in the through ball that Koo Sang-min blocked one-on-one. A step down from his Round 19 performance but still the man Busan had to account for.

## Fan Reaction

A note on this section, which is new to the digest from this round onward. It is supporter sentiment rather than reporting, and it is labeled that way deliberately.

This week it is thin, and honesty is better than padding. The match finished only hours before publication, and the Korean supporter platforms this section will normally draw on, the DC Inside Seoul E-Land gallery, FM Korea's football boards, and the comment threads under Naver Sports match pages, had not yet surfaced post-match discussion in any form we could retrieve. Nothing usable had been indexed. Rather than characterise a mood we have not actually observed, we are saying so.

What can be reported is what happened in the ground. Seoul E-Land's away support traveled to Gudeok on a Sunday night for a fixture that finished with a comeback win, and [[Park Jae-yong]] made a point of thanking them by name in his post-match interview, singling out the fans who had come a long way. The attendance was 2,339 in a stadium that holds more than twelve thousand, so the visiting section was a small group who will remember it for a long time.

Expect the fuller picture to be about Byun Kyung-jun. A shaved head, a farewell substitute appearance, and the assist that won the match is the kind of image that travels without needing a caption, and it lands in a supporter culture that takes military-service farewells seriously. We will fold the actual reaction into the Round 21 digest once the boards catch up.

## News and Transfers

Byun Kyung-jun is gone. He enlists tomorrow, traveling to the Nonsan training center before joining Gimcheon Sangmu for an eighteen-month term as part of the second 2026 batch of military sports specialists. That he chose to play, and shaved his head to do it, tells you as much about the player as the assist does. Seoul lose the squad's most reliable pace outlet and, on the evidence of [[Park Jae-yong]]'s interview, a genuinely important dressing-room figure.

The other squad note is a happier one. [[Ahn Joo-wan]] came on again, one round after becoming the youngest goalscorer in K League history, and is now clearly in the rotation rather than being handed ceremonial minutes. With Byun Kyung-jun gone, the pace role from the bench is his to take.

## Tactical and Strategic Watch

Three matches, three shapes: the 3-4-3 away at Suwon FC, the 4-3-3 at home to Cheonan, and now a 4-2-3-1 at Busan. [[Kim Do-gyun]] is not being indecisive, he is matching the shape to the fixture, and the version he picked here, a double pivot screening the back four with Carius given license between the lines, was the right answer to a 3-4-3 that wanted to attack down the channels.

The bigger point is the one this column has been hammering for two rounds. Seoul had conceded three goals from winning positions in the final fifteen minutes across Rounds 18 and 19. Here they went behind on the hour to a triple substitution, and instead of folding they scored twice and then saw the game out with a defensive change and a back five. The block from Osmar and the punch from [[Min Sung-jun]] in that six-yard-box scramble are the moments that separate this from the last two matches.

## My Honest Read

I have spent two digests writing that this team cannot close games, and I am glad to be made to eat it.

Everything about this match was the version of Seoul E-Land supporters have been waiting for. They went to the ground where FC Seoul were beaten four days earlier, played the low-event away game the preview asked for, survived the best forty-five minutes Busan could throw at them, and then, when the goal finally came against them, they did not do the thing they have done all year. They equalised from a set piece they had been building towards. They blocked one off the line. They took the lead through a substitute who is going into the army in the morning. And then they killed the game with a back five instead of inviting the pressure that cost them at Suwon FC.

The Byun Kyung-jun story is the one that will last. A player at the end of his time at a club, one day from eighteen months away from professional football, shaved head, comes off the bench in the biggest match of the season and produces the pass that wins it. Then the striker who scored dedicates the goal to him and calls him the club's biggest legend. You do not get many nights like that following a second-division team.

And the table now says something it has not said all year. Thirty-six points from nineteen, level with Busan IPark and ahead of them on goal difference, which is second place, one point behind Suwon Samsung Bluewings. Automatic promotion is not a stretch goal any more. It is the position Seoul are currently occupying. The caveat is real, Hwaseong away in five days is a hard fixture and this squad just lost a rotation forward. But for one night the analyst and the supporter agree completely. This was the performance the season needed.
