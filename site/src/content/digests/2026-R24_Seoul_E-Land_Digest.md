# Round 24 Digest: Seongnam FC vs Seoul E-Land FC

> *Kim Do-gyun changed five players, started two men who had been at the club for eleven days, and left Euller on the bench for a Friday trip that had not happened yet. Seongnam scored from a corner nobody cleared, Gabriel headed in his first goal of the season with seven minutes left, and Seoul E-Land woke up fourth on the goals-scored tiebreaker.*

## The Round in One Paragraph

Seoul E-Land drew 1-1 at Seongnam on a heavy August evening at Tancheon Stadium, and the honest summary is that the manager gambled on rotation and got the minimum out of it. With a Friday night at Cheongju six days away, Kim Do-gyun named a side with five changes from the Paju eleven, including debuts for center-back Caio Marcelo and winger Hong Seok-jun (홍석준), both signed in the final week of the window, and benched Euller, Gabriel, and [[Ahn Joo-wan]]. Seongnam's new manager Kim Hae-un said afterwards that six of the names surprised his staff into a Plan B. It looked like it for an hour, then a corner at the hour mark bounced off Benicio's head, through [[Min Sung-jun]]'s hands, and off Jeon Min-gyu's outstretched boot for the second goal of a career that is two league matches old. Gabriel, on for [[Lee Ju-hyeok]] on 79, headed [[Yoon Seok-ju]]'s long cross into the corner on 83, and Lee Kwang-yeon (이광연) spent the rest of the night making sure it stayed 1-1. It is a second consecutive draw against a bottom-half side, a third in four rounds, and in a weekend where Suwon Samsung, Suwon FC, and Daegu all won, it dropped Seoul from second to fourth on goals scored, level on 42 points with Daegu and five behind the leaders.

## Match Report

The team sheet was the story before a ball was kicked. Kim Do-gyun kept the 4-3-3 and kept [[Min Sung-jun]], [[Kim Oh-kyu]], [[Oh In-pyo]], [[Park Chang-hwan]], Osmar and [[Park Jae-yong]], and changed everything around them: [[Son Hyuk-chan]] at left-back for [[Bae Seo-jun]], Caio Marcelo alongside the captain in his first appearance since arriving from Vila Nova, [[Yoon Seok-ju]] in for [[Alan Carius]], [[Lee Ju-hyeok]] on the right, and Hong Seok-jun, signed from Chungbuk Cheongju on August 19, given a first start on the left. Osmar sat as the deepest of the three midfielders, the job he had been pulled out of inside a half against Ansan. Euller, Gabriel, [[Baek Ji-woong]], [[Ahn Joo-wan]], and [[Kim Hyun]] waited on the bench. Kim Hae-un, a former Seongnam goalkeeper who was the club's sporting director until they sacked Jeon Kyung-jun in early August and handed him the job, set out a 4-4-2 with Villero and Freitas as the reference points and Jeon Min-gyu, the debutant who scored a 90th-minute winner at Ansan the previous week, alongside them.

Seongnam nearly punished the changes inside two minutes. Yu Ju-han's ball in behind found Yang Tae-yang, whose pass set Jeon Min-gyu clear for a finish the assistant's flag erased for offside. It was the first of two warnings that Seoul's new-look back line was slow to step up, and it was the only one Seongnam converted. The rest of the half belonged to the visitors in the way this digest has described too often: territory, pressure, no goal. Park Jae-yong lifted a good chance over after a Seongnam mistake on 17. Hong Seok-jun tried his luck from range. Lee Ju-hyeok had a shot deflected off a defender's head. Villero, Seongnam's round MVP from the previous week, drifted wide and hit one across the face of goal. The heat forced a drinks break, and just before halftime Benicio and Park Chang-hwan clashed heads so hard that both left for treatment and both came back bandaged. Five minutes of stoppage time, no goals, and a half in which Seongnam won most of the aerial duels and Seoul most of the ball.

Euller came on for Hong Seok-jun at the break, which read like an admission. Then, on the hour, the goal Seoul have conceded a dozen versions of this season. A Seongnam corner, Benicio's header flicking the ball on, Min Sung-jun coming and not getting there, and Jeon Min-gyu stretching a leg at the far side to poke it over the line. It was ugly, it was avoidable, and the goalkeeper who has rescued this team all season was, for once, the reason it went in.

Kim Do-gyun's response was to empty the bench and change the shape of the attack: [[Baek Ji-woong]] had already replaced the captain on 57 for reasons nobody has explained, and on 64 Euller, nineteen minutes into his evening, came off with Caio Marcelo to make way for [[Ahn Joo-wan]] and [[Kim Hyun]]. Kim Hae-un said later that Lee Jae-uk had handled Euller well and that the change did not surprise him; nothing in the written record suggests an injury, and Euller started and assisted six days later, so the withdrawal was explained afterward by Kim Do-gyun as a breathing issue that has recurred occasionally and needs checking. The switch to a taller, more direct front line produced the best chance of the night on 73: Kim Hyun headed down, Osmar met it with a left-footed drive, and Lee Kwang-yeon made the save of the round from inside the penalty spot, then stood up as if nothing had happened.

The equalizer came from the plan the substitutions announced. Gabriel replaced Lee Ju-hyeok on 79 and four minutes later [[Yoon Seok-ju]] hit a long, hanging cross from deep, Gabriel rose above Hwang Seok-gi, the defender Kim Hae-un had just introduced, and headed it into the far corner. First league goal of the season for a forward who arrived from Cheongju thirteen months ago, and the second time in six days that a Seoul substitute's header has been the decisive touch. Seven minutes of stoppage time followed: Ahn Joo-wan ran a counter and shot over on 88, Seongnam's Freitas dropped deep as Yu Seon tired, and [[Oh In-pyo]]'s long-range hit in the 96th minute was Lee Kwang-yeon's last save. Referee Cho Ji-eum's whistle came with 2,639 people in the stands and Seoul still unbeaten at Tancheon in ten visits. That, and the point, were the consolations.

## Player Performances

**Gabriel (가브리엘):**
Eleven minutes plus stoppage time, one header, one point. He had not scored in the league all season and the club's own fans were joking online that he had taken a year to charge up. The goal itself was a proper center-forward's header, timed, above the defender, into the corner, and it followed the exact pattern of the Paju equalizer: a direct delivery and a substitute arriving first. Seoul's bench is producing goals at a rate the starting attack is not.

**Lee Kwang-yeon (이광연, Seongnam):**
Man of the Match for the second week running against Seoul, Paju's Ryu Won-woo having done the honors before him. The stop from Osmar's drive on 73 was the moment of the match, and his handling in the seven minutes of stoppage time was the difference between a point and none for his side. Seoul have now met two goalkeepers in a row who had the game of their season.

**Caio Marcelo (카이오 마르셀로):**
A debut sixty-four minutes at center-back eleven days after signing, in heat, in a rotated side. He was not at fault for the goal, which was a goalkeeper's error on a set piece, and he was not obviously exposed in open play either. The real verdict will come in a match where the back line is not being assembled on the night.

**Hong Seok-jun (홍석준):**
A first start ten days after arriving, a long-range effort over on 23, and a halftime withdrawal for Euller that told you what the manager thought of the first half. Not a failure so much as a player thrown into a rotated side with no rehearsal. He will get better nights.

**[[Min Sung-jun]] (민성준):**
The goal was his. He came for a corner he could not reach, the flick-on took him out of the play, and Jeon Min-gyu scored with the goal unguarded. Every column this site has written since July has credited him with points this team did not deserve, so the ledger can absorb one bad night. But it was a bad night, and it came at a set piece, which is where Seoul's season keeps leaking.

**Osmar (오스마르):**
Asked to play a full match as the single holding midfielder for the first time, at thirty-eight, in August heat, and he was the one Seoul player who nearly won it, his drive on 73 needing a wonder save. The experiment of Osmar in midfield is producing Seoul's cleanest passing and Seoul's best chances; whether it is producing a defense that clears corners is a different question.

**[[Ahn Joo-wan]] (안주완):**
Twenty-six minutes off the bench and, once again, the only Seoul player attacking the space behind the last line. His counter on 88 should have been the winner and he lifted it. Six days later he would score twice in a half at Cheongju, so treat this as the warm-up.

## Fan Reaction

Thin on the Seoul side, loud on the Seongnam side.

The Seoul E-Land gallery on DC Inside, never busy, managed a few posts on the night and none of them were kind: sarcasm about whether the team actually wants promotion, posted within minutes of Jeon Min-gyu's goal; a wisecrack that Gabriel had spent a year charging up before finally releasing a goal; and flat despair at the phrase "two straight draws." No comments, no debate, just resignation typed into an empty room.

Seongnam's gallery was busy and furious, which is the part that should interest Seoul supporters: their fans thought they should have lost. The most-recommended thread of the night argued that Kim Hae-un's substitutions, moving Yu Ju-han into the middle, pulling a center-back while defending a lead, invited Seoul's late siege, and that Seongnam only survived because "a high schooler," meaning Ahn Joo-wan, wasted the chances. Hwang Seok-gi losing the aerial duel on the equalizer was called a disaster, Kim Min-jae was criticized for his cameo, and a fan-counted "thirteen shots on target" was cited to defend Lee Kwang-yeon against anyone daring to criticize him. Opinion split on the regime change, some missing the sacked Jeon Kyung-jun, some preferring the livelier football. Nobody on either board thought Seoul E-Land had played badly. Everybody thought Seoul E-Land should have won. FM Korea, Naver comments, and X were not reachable this round.

## News and Transfers

**Two debuts, one week after the window shut.** Caio Marcelo, signed from Vila Nova on August 18, and Hong Seok-jun, signed from Chungbuk Cheongju on August 19, both started. Neither had been in the squad against Paju. Throwing two new arrivals into a rotated side away from home is a statement about how thin Kim Do-gyun considers his options for a two-match week.

**Kim Hae-un's Seongnam are a different problem now.** Seongnam sacked Jeon Kyung-jun on August 4, went through a caretaker match, and on August 11 handed the job to their sporting director, a former goalkeeper and one-club man who had never coached a senior side. He sold the captain Park Su-bin to Jeju, threw in youth players, told the press his team plays far more directly than before and turns the ball over accordingly, and has taken four points from Seoul's two nearest promotion rivals' opponents... more precisely, four points from three matches including this one. His post-match line about Seoul, that six unexpected names forced a Plan B, was delivered with obvious satisfaction.

**Jeon Min-gyu is Seongnam's new story.** A university signing Kim Hae-un says he tracked for two years as director, he scored the winner at Ansan on his debut, scored here in his second match, and would score again at Gimpo the following week. Two goals from three shots in his first 128 minutes.

**No Kim Do-gyun press conference surfaced.** Roughly forty searches across the Korean outlets produced Kim Hae-un's remarks in full and nothing from the Seoul manager. His most recent public words remain the ones from before Paju: rivals will have their draw days too, and he had asked the players not to watch other results.

**Kim Oh-kyu's early withdrawal is unexplained.** The captain came off for [[Baek Ji-woong]] on 57 with the game level. No injury report followed, and he was left out of the eleven at Cheongju six days later. Worth watching before Suwon Samsung arrive.

**The 100th win waited another week.** Kim Do-gyun has sat on 99 career K League wins since Ansan on August 16; two draws later, the milestone traveled to Cheongju.

## Tactical and Strategic Watch

**Our published prediction called this a draw, and for once being right was no comfort.** The frozen July call had Round 24 as a D, the fourth hit in seven graded rounds. There was no pre-match preview for this fixture, so there is no lineup to grade, which is just as well, because nobody would have called two debutants and Euller on the bench.

**Rotation had a logic and a cost.** Seoul had a Friday night at Cheongju six days later and a top-of-the-table match with Suwon Samsung two weeks out, and Kim Do-gyun has been managing Osmar's minutes and Euller's card count all month. Resting Euller, Carius, and Baek Ji-woong at a ground where Seoul had never lost is defensible. The cost was that a side with two debutants and a reshuffled midfield produced the same problem as the full-strength one: possession without penetration, and a set-piece concession.

**The set-piece wound reopened, and this time it was the goalkeeper.** Paju's equalizer came from a cross and a deflection; Seongnam's came from a corner, a flick-on, and a keeper who committed and lost. Different players, same category: a dead ball into the box producing the one Seoul concession of the night. Every remaining opponent has this on tape.

**The direct route worked. Again.** Both Seoul goals in the last two rounds have come from substitutes heading crosses, Kim Hyun's flick creating one, Gabriel finishing the other from a Yoon Seok-ju long ball. The patient buildup this team prefers is producing shots for opposition goalkeepers to enjoy; the crude version is producing goals. That is not an argument for abandoning the identity, but it is an argument for reaching for the alternative before the 79th minute.

## My Honest Read

The rotation was the right idea executed at the wrong ground. Six days before a Friday trip and two weeks before the leaders visit, resting Euller, Carius, and Baek Ji-woong against a tenth-placed side you have never lost to is exactly what a manager with a thin squad and a title race should do. Where I part company with it is the scale: five changes and two debutants is not rotation, it is a different team, and a different team played like one for the first half hour, slow to step up, slow to find each other, and lucky the flag went up on the second minute.

The goal conceded needs saying plainly. It was a goalkeeping error at a set piece, the first real one [[Min Sung-jun]] has made in a season where he has repeatedly been the reason Seoul kept points, and it came in exactly the phase of play, dead balls into the box, that has cost this team most of its dropped points since July. The pattern is now unmistakable and it is not about personnel, because the personnel keep changing and the concession keeps coming.

And yet the point is real, and the schedule made it feel smaller than it was. Three draws in four is the form of a team that has forgotten how to beat the sides it should beat, but it is also the form of a team that has not lost since June. Seoul fell to fourth without dropping a single point more than their prediction said they would, because Suwon FC and Daegu both won and the leaders scored four. That is the race now. Being good is not enough; every draw is a week the other three did not have. Friday at Cheongju would show which of those two truths this team believes.
