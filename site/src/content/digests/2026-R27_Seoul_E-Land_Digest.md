# Round 27 Digest: Seoul E-Land FC vs Daegu FC

> *Without the captain, in a shape they had not used in a month, Seoul E-Land beat the second-placed side 2-1 in a match with eleven minutes of stoppage time, two goals ruled out and a video review that took the celebration away from Park Jae-yong and then gave it back. His tenth of the season, in his hundredth K League appearance, sends Seoul into the three-week break level on points with Suwon FC for the second automatic promotion place, one ahead of Daegu, with every head-to-head against a rival now behind them.*

## The Round in One Paragraph

Seoul E-Land beat Daegu 2-1 at Mokdong in front of 6,105, the match the Korean press had spent the week calling a six-point game, and it played like one. Kim Do-gyun, without the suspended [[Park Chang-hwan]], switched to a 3-4-3 and gave the captain's role to [[Baek Ji-woong]], who delivered the low cross from the right that [[Cho Jun-hyun]] turned into his first professional goal on 29 minutes after Han Tae-hee saved the first attempt. Deckers headed Cesinha's corner in on 42 for his sixth of the season. Six minutes into the second half Euller found Baek Ji-woong on the right, Baek played a first-time pass inside, and [[Park Jae-yong]] took one touch and hit a right-footed strike into the top corner; the referee went to the monitor for a possible handball on the control, ruled the ball had struck his stomach, and Park celebrated twice. Euller had a goal ruled out for a foul in the build-up on 30, Osmar's clearing header hit his own post on 35, and in the second minute of stoppage time Deckers' looping volley went in off [[Min Sung-jun]]'s hands only for the whistle to have already gone for Park In-hyuk's offside. Eleven minutes were added. Seoul held. They are third on 48 points, level with Suwon FC, who had won 3-0 at Jeonnam that afternoon and sit above on goals scored, and one clear of Daegu, whose seven-match unbeaten run ended and who drop to fourth with the bye next.

## Match Report

The team sheet was the story before a ball was kicked. Kim Do-gyun had told reporters on Thursday that he had given Park Chang-hwan's role to Baek Ji-woong, "a different type of player, but one with height and goals," and that he had prepared an organized defensive block with a plan to change the left side in the second half for fitness reasons. What that meant in practice was a 3-4-3: [[Min Sung-jun]]; Caio Marcelo, Osmar and [[Baek Ji-woong]] across the back; [[Kim Joo-hwan]] and [[Oh In-pyo]] as wing-backs with [[Cho Jun-hyun]] and [[Yoon Seok-ju]] inside; [[Lee Ju-hyeok]], [[Park Jae-yong]] and Euller in front. [[Ahn Joo-wan]] started on the bench for the first time since his record brace at Cheongju. Choi Sung-yong's Daegu were the expected eleven with Kim Dae-woo and Hwang In-taek back from suspension, Han Tae-hee in goal, Cesinha and Deckers up front with Kim Ju-gong, and Seraphim, Hwang Jae-won and Park In-hyuk held on the bench. Caio, who spent a year at Daegu, had said before kickoff that his job was to make Cesinha's evening as annoying as possible.

Daegu started faster. Cesinha's pass set Deckers up for a chested drop-volley from the edge of the area inside two minutes, and a few minutes later Caio and Min Sung-jun collided as the goalkeeper came to claim, Caio's boot catching his own goalkeeper in the face. Osmar, making what the broadcast counted as his 365th K League appearance against Cesinha's 304th, blocked the Brazilian's next attempt to turn him. Seoul's first real moment came from a Daegu turnover: Lee Ju-hyeok drove into the left channel and shot, Han Tae-hee saved, and the analyst on the broadcast noted that Seoul had five players in the box and used none of them.

The goal came on 29 and it was a second-ball goal. Baek Ji-woong, stepping out of the back three onto the right, put a low cross into the six-yard area; Cho Jun-hyun met it first time, Han Tae-hee got a foot to it, and Cho followed up to poke the rebound over the line. It was his first goal as a professional, roughly two years after his debut, and it came from the position Park Chang-hwan normally fills. Sixty seconds later Seoul thought they had a second: Lee Ju-hyeok crossed from the left, the header was saved, and Euller finished the rebound, but referee Jung Hoe-su had seen a foul in the aerial contest and the goal did not stand. On 35 Cesinha swung in an early cross and Osmar's clearing header came back off his own post. On 38 Cho Jun-hyun pulled Jang Sung-won down as Daegu broke and was booked.

Deckers equalized on 42 the way everyone in the ground knew he might. Cesinha, who had been finding his range with each set piece, delivered from the right corner and Deckers rose above a crowd of bodies to head in. It was his sixth of the season and his third in two matches. The half ended 1-1 with the broadcast crew agreeing that both teams had spent 45 minutes trying to pass forward rather than hold the ball, which is the kind of football that produces 11 minutes of stoppage time.

Kim Do-gyun made the change he had promised: Ahn Joo-wan for Lee Ju-hyeok at halftime. Choi made two, Seraphim and Hwang Jae-won for Kim Ju-gong and Choi Kang-min. Within two minutes Ahn had a shot cleared off the line by Han Tae-hee's foot after Euller had laid the ball across. And on 51 Seoul scored the goal of the night. Euller carried down the right and found Baek Ji-woong, who had again pushed forward; Baek's first-time pass came from behind Park Jae-yong, who let it run through his legs onto his right foot without breaking stride, opened his body, and hit a shot that was in the top corner before Han Tae-hee moved. Park slid on his knees, fell forward, and then had to wait: the referee signaled an on-field review for a handball on the control. The replay showed the ball on his abdomen. The goal stood, Park celebrated again, and Baek Ji-woong got the hug and, per Park's post-match promise on the broadcast, will be getting drinks bought for him for the rest of the season. Tenth goal of the season, the first time in his career he has reached double figures, in his hundredth K League appearance.

The last forty minutes were a siege in slow motion. Seoul made a double change on 61, [[Alan Carius]] and [[Son Hyuk-chan]] for Cho Jun-hyun and Kim Joo-hwan; Daegu sent on Park In-hyuk and later Lee Won-woo, a center-back Choi pushed forward as a target. Kim Dae-woo's shot from a Deckers knockdown was blocked by Caio's body on 65. Son Hyuk-chan and Carius were booked in quick succession. Cesinha's deflected shot from distance forced a save on 72 and Min Sung-jun then blocked Deckers' follow-up. Lee Won-woo's turn and shot on 84 missed the right post by very little, the broadcast comparing the touch to Ibrahimovic. Ahn Joo-wan beat three defenders with one change of pace and was denied by Han Tae-hee, who made a string of saves through the second half. [[Kim Oh-kyu]] came on for Euller on 81, [[Seo Jin-seok]] for Baek Ji-woong on 86.

Eleven minutes were announced. In the second of them, Seraphim crossed from the right, Deckers threw himself at it and produced a looping, acrobatic volley that dropped over Min Sung-jun's hands and into the net. The whistle had gone: Park In-hyuk had been offside earlier in the move. "I was startled too," Kim Do-gyun said afterward, "but the whistle had already been blown." Cesinha was booked in the twelfth added minute after a tangle; the referee looked at the incident again and left it yellow. Oh In-pyo still had the legs to sprint clear and set up Ahn Joo-wan for one more save from Han Tae-hee. Then it was over, 2-1, and Kim Do-gyun's hundredth win as a K League manager, presented before kickoff, had a hundred-and-first alongside it.

## Player Performances

**[[Park Jae-yong]] (박재용):**
A hundred appearances, ten goals, and a finish that will be replayed for the rest of the season. The touch was the goal: the ball came from behind him, he let it through his legs onto his right foot without slowing down, and the shot did the rest. His manager's post-match line was affectionate and precise: worth congratulating, still inconsistent, sacrificing himself for the team, an example to the others. Park himself said on the broadcast that the goal mattered more than the milestone and that he could not promise six wins from six but would promise six fights like this one.

**[[Cho Jun-hyun]] (조준현):**
Asked to replace the captain in a match the club could not afford to lose, he scored his first professional goal in the 29th minute and was booked nine minutes later for the kind of foul Park Chang-hwan usually commits. Withdrawn on 61 with the job done. Two years after his debut, this is the night his career at Seoul starts.

**[[Baek Ji-woong]] (백지웅):**
Two weeks ago he was named in the press room for an error. On Saturday he was given Park Chang-hwan's role, stepped out of the back three to deliver the cross for the first goal, and played the first-time pass that made the second. Officially one assist; in practice two. Went off on 86 to an ovation.

**Euller (에울레르):**
A goal disallowed, the pass that started the winner, the lay-off for Ahn Joo-wan's shot off the line, and 81 minutes of carrying the right side. No goal contribution on the official sheet, and the most influential attacker on the pitch again.

**[[Min Sung-jun]] (민성준):**
The save from Cesinha's deflected shot and the block from Deckers' follow-up on 72 kept the lead. He spilled the disallowed volley into his own net in stoppage time and was rescued by the flag. His manager said he has been steadier in the second half of the season than the first, and the numbers agree: five goals conceded in the last six.

**Caio Marcelo (카이오):**
First start since his debut at Seongnam, against the club he left in the summer. Blocked Kim Dae-woo's shot with his body, put his boot in his own goalkeeper's face by accident in the first ten minutes, and kept his promise about Cesinha for most of the night. Osmar beside him hit his own post and was otherwise immaculate.

**Han Tae-hee (한태희, Daegu):**
The Daegu goalkeeper is here because without him this is 4-1. Saved Lee Ju-hyeok early, cleared Ahn Joo-wan's shot off the line with his foot, saved Ahn twice more late, and could do nothing about the two he conceded.

## Fan Reaction

The supporter forums were not reachable this round; FM Korea's board carried no Seoul E-Land threads on the night and the DC Inside gallery was down, so this section leans on the press and the ground.

The gate was 6,105, a good Saturday number for Mokdong outside the Suwon matches and roughly half of the previous week's record, with a Daegu contingent that Choi Sung-yong began his press conference by apologizing to. The pre-match ceremonies for the manager's hundredth win and the club's fifth straight Fan-Friendly Club award went ahead as scheduled. Inside the match the moments that carried were Park Jae-yong's double celebration, the collective intake of breath when Deckers' volley dropped in before anyone realized the whistle had gone, and the eleven added minutes, which the broadcast crew joked the referee had awarded because he was enjoying the match.

The media framing was unanimous and, for once, unqualified: Star News called it a six-point battle won and Seoul's return to the direct-promotion fight; MyDaily led on the seventh-match defeat for Daegu and the five-point gap to the leaders; Sports Chosun said Seoul had "rekindled the race for second"; MK Sports quoted Kim Do-gyun's own verdict, that a hurdle had been cleared. The one sour note was arithmetic rather than opinion: Suwon FC's 3-0 at Jeonnam earlier in the afternoon meant Seoul climbed only one place, not two.

## Tactical and Strategic Watch

**The 3-4-3 worked because of who was in it.** Kim Do-gyun's first big-match back three since August put Baek Ji-woong on the right of it with license to step out, and both goals came from him doing exactly that. It also put two natural wing-backs, Kim Joo-hwan and Oh In-pyo, against Daegu's Jang Sung-won and Choi Kang-min, and Seoul won that contest on both flanks. The cost was in the air, where Deckers scored anyway, and in the number of shots Daegu got from the edge of the box after turnovers. Choi Sung-yong's own diagnosis of his team, too many turnovers in transition, was Seoul's plan working.

**Set pieces, again, both ways.** Seoul conceded from a corner for the third match in a row that they have conceded at all. Kim Do-gyun had said before the match he wanted Osmar, Baek Ji-woong, Park Jae-yong and Caio scoring from set pieces; instead Osmar nearly scored an own goal from one. It is the one area of the team that has not improved since July.

**The goals-scored problem is now the manager's stated priority.** Seoul are level on points with Suwon FC and five goals behind on the tiebreaker, and Kim Do-gyun said on Saturday, unprompted, that this could decide second place and that the substitutes need to contribute more goals. He is right. In the six remaining matches, a 1-0 is worth the same three points as a 4-0 but a 4-0 is worth more in November.

**The head-to-heads are done.** Seoul have played every top-six rival twice. What remains is Gimpo, Gimhae, Yongin, Jeonnam, Chungnam Asan and Gyeongnam, all currently eighth or lower. Kim Do-gyun named the danger himself: in each of the last two seasons the club lost the matches it had to win against sides with nothing to play for. This year, he said, the team is sturdier. Six matches will tell.

## My Honest Read

This was the best result of the season measured by what it cost to get: no captain, a formation change, a second-half handball review, eleven added minutes, and an opponent that had not lost in seven. Seoul won it by being braver than Daegu in exactly the way Choi Sung-yong later admitted his team was not, playing forward from the first minute and living with the turnovers.

And it moved the projection by two percentage points. That is the frustrating truth of Saturday: Suwon FC's win at Jeonnam, without their best player, against a side that had not lost at home in four, was the more important result of the day for Seoul's promotion hopes, and it went the wrong way. Seoul are now exactly where the calendar always said they would be, in a straight race with a club that has five home matches from seven and a five-goal head start on the tiebreaker. The good news is that Seoul control every one of their own six, and the manager has correctly identified that the way to beat a goals-scored tiebreak is to score more goals. The break is three weeks. The next match is against a team Seoul have not lost to in two years. There is nothing left to do but win, by more than one.
