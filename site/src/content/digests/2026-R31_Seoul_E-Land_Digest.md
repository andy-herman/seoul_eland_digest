# Round 31 Digest: Yongin FC vs Seoul E-Land FC

> *Two-thirds of the ball, 19 shots and ten corners, and a 4-2 defeat: Seoul E-Land lost their captain to an ankle injury after 21 minutes, gifted Yongin a goal, conceded a penalty and a stoppage-time counter, and let the chance Suwon FC had handed them a week earlier slip away. "We lost with nothing to complain about," said Kim Do-gyun.*

## The Round in One Paragraph

Seoul E-Land lost 4-2 at Yongin FC on Saturday in front of 3,712 at Yongin Mireu Stadium, Seoul's first ever visit there. [[Park Chang-hwan]] went off injured on 21 minutes. Yoo Dong-gyu put Yongin ahead on 24 from Gabriel's pass, and Gabriel, Yongin's Brazilian centre-forward, made it 2-0 on 38 when a back pass from Caio to [[Min Sung-jun]] went wrong. [[Osmar]] headed in [[Euller]]'s corner in first-half stoppage time, but Gabriel scored a VAR-awarded penalty on 52. [[Cho Jun-hyun]], Park's replacement, made it 3-2 on 66, Euller hit the crossbar late on, and Vitinho scored on the counter in the tenth minute of stoppage time. It was Yongin's first back-to-back league wins in the club's history. Seoul stayed on 54 points and went to bed second on goals scored, but Suwon FC beat Ansan 1-0 the next day and are three points clear. Daegu drew 2-2 with Suwon Samsung and are one point behind Seoul.

## Match Report

Kim Do-gyun kept the eleven that beat Gimhae 6-2 but changed the shape, from a back three to a back four. [[Oh In-pyo]], Osmar, Caio and [[Bae Seo-jun]] made up the defence in front of Min Sung-jun, with [[Baek Ji-woong]] moving up into midfield beside Park Chang-hwan and [[Yoon Seok-ju]], and [[Alan Carius]], [[Park Jae-yong]] and Euller in attack. [[Son Hyuk-chan]] was suspended. Choi Yun-kyum's Yongin played Gabriel alone up front, with their usual striker Seok Hyun-jun injured, and five across midfield behind him.

Seoul had the ball from the first minute, and early on they had their best chance of the half: Euller's cross from the right forced a scramble, and goalkeeper No Hee-dong reached Park Chang-hwan's shot from close range. On 15 minutes Park was stepped on by a defender, Sports Chosun reported. He played on, could not run properly, and went off on 21 for Cho Jun-hyun.

Three minutes later Yongin scored with their first real attack. Gabriel held off a defender near halfway and slipped the ball down the right for Yoo Dong-gyu, who ran through and hit it high past Min Sung-jun; he said afterward that the goalkeeping coach had told the forwards to aim for the top of the goal in exactly that situation. On 38 the lead doubled. Caio won the ball from Gabriel and played it back to his goalkeeper, Min could not take it cleanly, and Gabriel rolled it into an empty net. Seoul had a reply before the break: No Hee-dong saved from Euller, Euller took the corner, and Osmar headed it in. Two-one at half-time.

Kim Do-gyun changed his defence at the break. Caio and Bae Seo-jun came off for [[Kim Hyun]] and [[Kim Joo-hwan]], Baek Ji-woong dropped to centre-back and Oh In-pyo moved to left-back. Within seven minutes the reshuffle cost a goal. Gabriel broke into the box, went down under Baek's tackle and appealed; referee Jeong Hoe-su went to the pitchside monitor and gave the penalty for a careless challenge, and Gabriel scored it himself.

Seoul kept coming. [[Ahn Joo-wan]] replaced Carius on 62, and on 66 Oh In-pyo's pass, Park Jae-yong's back-heel and Cho Jun-hyun's right foot made it 3-2. Yongin had a goal from Kim Hyun-jun ruled out for offside on about 79 minutes, [[Seo Jin-seok]] came on for Yoon Seok-ju on 80, and in stoppage time Euller's header from close range came back off the crossbar. Ten minutes were added, and in the last of them Lee Gyu-dong, on for four minutes, sent Vitinho away on the counter to make it 4-2. Vitinho was playing his first match since a hamstring injury in September.

The numbers were all Seoul's except the score: 68 percent of the ball, 19 shots to 8, ten corners to two, and 569 completed passes to Yongin's 162. Yongin had six shots on target and scored four of them. Seoul were not booked; Yongin collected three yellow cards.

## Player Performances

**[[Osmar]] (오스마르):**
A goal, his third of the season, and 100 of 105 passes completed. Moved to the left of the centre-backs at half-time, which Kim Do-gyun said was meant to help Seoul build attacks. One of Seoul's three best on the league's ratings.

**[[Cho Jun-hyun]] (조준현):**
On for the injured captain after 21 minutes and Seoul's most dangerous player after that: four shots, two on target, and the goal that made it 3-2, his second of the season.

**[[Euller]] (에울레르):**
The assist for Osmar's goal, his sixth of the season, three key passes, a shot saved before the corner that made Seoul's first goal, and a header against the crossbar in stoppage time. The best numbers of the afternoon, on the losing side.

**[[Park Chang-hwan]] (박창환):**
Captain for the day, the best chance of the opening minutes, and then the injury that may have decided the match as much as anything. Scans showed a torn ligament in his ankle, Sports Chosun reported, which is not season-ending: up to four weeks out. Kim Do-gyun had first feared the season was over after Park said he heard something snap.

**Caio (카이오):**
The back pass that led to the second goal, and off at half-time. Kim Do-gyun said afterward that his condition had not been good: "Mistakes aside, he looked heavy."

**[[Baek Ji-woong]] (백지웅):**
Started in midfield, dropped to centre-back at half-time, and gave away the penalty seven minutes later. Still on four yellow cards.

**[[Min Sung-jun]] (민성준):**
Could do little about three of the goals. The second came from his mix-up with Caio.

**Gabriel (가브리엘, Yongin):**
Two goals, an assist and the penalty he won himself: the league's Round 31 MVP and the first Yongin player to reach ten league goals. Not to be confused with Seoul's own Gabriel, who was not in the squad.

## Fan Reaction

The Korean press was blunt. Goal Korea called it a red light for direct promotion, and Mydaily wrote that Seoul's hold on second was in danger. Sports Chosun called Caio's back pass a fatal mistake, and Interfootball's report summed up the defeat as early chances missed and concentration lost at the back. Goal's reporter questioned the half-time decision to drop Baek Ji-woong into the defence, since the penalty followed within minutes. The club's own report blamed an early injury, defensive errors and bad luck with the woodwork.

On the other side it was a celebration. Yongin's fans, who had chanted at their own team after a 4-0 home defeat to Ansan a fortnight earlier, held a carnival at the gate after the match. Choi Yun-kyum credited them: "Our fans' wishes brought the players luck today," he said. "The woodwork helped us and we had a lot of luck."

## News and Transfers

- **Park Chang-hwan**: torn anterior talofibular ligament in the ankle, up to four weeks out (Sports Chosun, November 4). He has said he wants to play against Chungnam Asan on November 22, even on painkillers, and the club plans to manage his minutes.

- **Daegu** drew 2-2 at home to leaders Suwon Samsung in front of 12,230 on Saturday and are fourth on 53, one point behind Seoul.
- **Elsewhere:** Chungnam Asan beat Gimpo 5-1, Gyeongnam beat Hwaseong 1-0, Jeonnam and Seongnam drew 2-2, Paju won 2-1 at Chungbuk Cheongju, and Gimhae and Cheonan drew 3-3. Busan had the bye.

## Tactical and Strategic Watch

**Control without command.** The back four put Baek Ji-woong in midfield, and Seoul had the ball: 68 percent of possession and 652 passes. But Yongin needed only eight shots, two of them from Seoul's own mistakes in their half. Kim's own diagnosis afterward was not tactical at all. It was a team problem, not one or two players, he said, and about mental preparation rather than ability.

**The half-time reshuffle cost a goal.** With Caio off, Baek Ji-woong dropped into the back line he had left at kickoff, and the penalty came seven minutes later. Choi Yun-kyum had planned for exactly this sort of match: sit deep, let Seoul have the ball, and break through Gabriel, who he says suits a counter-attacking side better than his usual striker.

**Height did not tell.** Choi said he had prepared for four Seoul players close to 190 centimetres and told his goalkeeper to come for crosses. Ten Seoul corners produced one goal.

**What the defeat costs.** A win would have put Seoul second with three matches to play. Instead Suwon FC are three points clear with three to play, and Daegu are one behind Seoul.

## My Honest Read

Kim Do-gyun's verdict was the right one: Seoul lost with nothing to complain about. Dominating the ball against a side that wants you to have it is not the same as dominating the match, and Yongin were the better-prepared team. Seoul gave them a goal, a penalty and a counter-attack in stoppage time while pushing for an equaliser, and lost their captain along the way. The cost is large. A week ago Suwon FC lost at home and handed Seoul the chance to take second place; this was the afternoon to take it, and the next afternoon Suwon FC took it back. From here Seoul need Suwon FC to drop points twice in three matches, and they need to stop dropping them themselves. The playoff place is safe. The question now is whether Seoul finish third, which means sixth in the semi-final and a home final if they get that far, or fourth.
