## Why This Match Matters

Seoul are third on 57 points, three behind Suwon FC with two matches left, and level with them on goals scored, the first tiebreaker, at 57 each. The model gives Seoul an 8 percent chance of the top two. A win here lifts it to about 18 percent; a draw drops it to about 3, and a defeat ends it in all but a handful of the model's runs. By kickoff Seoul will know whether Suwon FC won at Gimpo the day before. If they did, second place is close to gone whatever happens at Mokdong.

The other race is for third, and it is live. Daegu are one point behind Seoul with one more goal scored, 58 to 57, so if the two finish level on points and the goal totals stay as they are, Daegu go above. Daegu host Gimhae two and a half hours before this kickoff. Third place matters because third plays sixth rather than fifth in the playoff semi-final on December 2 and hosts the final on December 5 if they get there; in both, the higher seed goes through on a draw. Kim Do-gyun said before the Jeonnam match that if Suwon FC win out, Seoul must at least hold third. This is the match that decides whether they can.

## Opponent Scouting Report

**Chungnam Asan** are seventh, and they are the in-form side outside the top six. They are unbeaten in five league matches, three wins and two draws, including a 1-0 win at Suwon FC in Round 30, which ended Suwon FC's 15-match unbeaten run, and a 5-1 home win over Gimpo in Round 31. They still have a mathematical chance of the top six, which the model puts at 13 percent before their match at Paju on November 8.

The coach is André, a Brazilian, the first foreign coach in the club's history and a former Daegu head coach who won the Korean FA Cup there. He was appointed at the end of April after Lim Kwan-sik left, though a visa delay kept him off the bench until Round 12 in May; Lim is now at Jeonnam. After the 5-1 against Gimpo, André said the players were in a good mood because a chance of the playoffs that had been 2 percent had gone up. Under André they have settled on three centre-backs, Lee Ho-in, Kim Hye-seong and Byeon Jun-young, with Kim Ju-seong at left wing-back, and a front three of Han Kyo-won, Kim Jong-min and Denison, with Son Jun-ho running the midfield.

**Who to watch.** Denison, the Brazilian wide forward, has nine league goals and three assists, and scored twice against Gimpo. Kim Jong-min has seven goals and scored a hat-trick against Seoul in May. Son Jun-ho has seven assists and scored twice against Gimpo. Kim Ju-seong (five assists) and Han Kyo-won (five) are the other creators, and Ngoy has five goals, mostly off the bench.

**The pattern.** Chungnam Asan are much better at home than away. At home they have won eight, drawn four and lost three; away they have won three, drawn five and lost six, with 12 goals scored in 14 away matches. Their recent away results are a 1-1 at Gimhae, a 0-0 at Gyeongnam and the 1-0 at Suwon FC: hard to beat, rarely prolific.

**Cards.** Son Jun-ho, Jang Jun-young and Park Seong-woo are each on four yellow cards, and Choi Hee-won on nine, so a booking at Paju on November 8 would rule any of them out of this match. Asan realistically need to win at Mokdong to stay in the playoff race: a defeat would cap them at 48 points, the total Busan and Hwaseong already have.

## How Seoul E-Land Match Up

Seoul are 9 wins, 2 draws and 4 defeats at Mokdong this season, with 34 goals scored. Since 2024 they have played Chungnam Asan four times at home and won three, including 5-0 and 4-1. The away meeting in May was the exception, and it was emphatic: Kim Jong-min scored a hat-trick, and Lee Ju-hyeok was sent off with Seoul already 2-0 down.

The match-up that matters is Seoul's front three against Asan's back three. Euller and Park Jae-yong have 25 league goals between them, and Park's 12 have all come from open play. Asan's wing-backs push up, which leaves space behind them for Euller on the right and whoever plays on the left. The danger is the other way: Seoul have conceded eight goals in their last three matches, and Kim Do-gyun's own diagnosis is that losing the ball in midfield is what lets opponents counter. Asan, with Denison and Kim Jong-min, are built to punish exactly that.

## Strategy That Can Work

Keep the ball in midfield and make Asan defend for long spells. They have scored 12 goals in 14 away matches; the risk is not that they dominate, it is that they score on the break after a Seoul turnover. Use the full-backs to pin Asan's wing-backs and create two-on-ones on the flanks, and let Euller's crossing and set pieces do the rest. Seoul's last three matches have all included a goal from a set-piece situation.

## Keys to a Seoul E-Land Win

### 1. Do not give the ball away in midfield

Kim Do-gyun named it after Jeonnam: when Seoul lose it in the middle, dangerous counter-attacks follow. Yoon Seok-ju and whoever partners him need to play safe until Seoul are in the final third.

### 2. Get Euller on the ball, or plan without him

The tendon is the question. If he is fit, he is Seoul's best route to goal from open play and from the spot. If he is not, the attack needs another plan, and against Jeonnam it did not have one.

### 3. Score more than one

On equal points the tiebreaker is goals scored, against Suwon FC for second and against Daegu for third, and Seoul are level with one and one behind the other. A 2-0 is worth more than a 1-0.

## Bottom Line

Seoul should be favorites at home against a side that wins rarely away, but this is not Yongin or Jeonnam. Chungnam Asan beat Seoul 3-0 in May, went to Suwon FC and won in October, and arrive unbeaten in five. The answer is what Kim Do-gyun keeps asking for: patience on the ball and more than one goal.
