# Second Round Robin Preview: The Playoff Run-In

> *Seoul E-Land go into the second round robin exactly where a promotion project wants to be and nowhere near safe. Fifth of seventeen, inside the playoff line, one bad month from outside it. Here is what the run-in demands, the strategy that gets it done, and a round-by-round call on where it ends.*

## Where We Stand

The first round robin is done and the table has settled. After sixteen games, Seoul E-Land sit fifth on 29 points, a 9-2-5 record with a plus-nine goal difference. That is a promotion-playoff position, but it is the most precarious one on the board.

## What Is Actually at Stake

Promotion out of K League 2 in 2026 runs on a wider gate than usual, and understanding it changes how you read the run-in.

- **First and second place are promoted automatically.** Two direct tickets to K League 1, no playoff required. This is an expansion from prior seasons, tied to the league growing to fourteen clubs in 2027.
- **Third through sixth enter the promotion playoff.** Third hosts sixth and fourth hosts fifth in single-leg semi-finals, with the higher seed at home. The winners meet in a final, and the final winner earns a third promotion place.
- **A conditional fourth spot exists.** If Gimcheon Sangmu finish eleventh or higher in K League 1, the K League 2 playoff runner-up gets a promotion-relegation playoff against the K League 1 side that finishes twelfth. If Gimcheon finish bottom, that door does not open.

So the honest framing is not "can Seoul E-Land reach the playoffs." They are in the playoff zone right now. The framing is **hold the top six, and climb.** Finishing third or fourth means a home semi-final. Finishing fifth or sixth means going on the road in a one-off knockout, the exact low-margin trap that ended the 2025 season against Seongnam. And finishing first or second skips the lottery entirely. The gap between those outcomes is a handful of points spread across sixteen games.

## The Math: What Seoul E-Land Need

The 2026 format is new, so precedent is a guide rather than a guarantee, but the targets are clear enough.

- **Hold a playoff place (top six):** roughly 52 to 56 points looks safe. From 29 now, that is 23 to 27 points from the sixteen remaining games, about 1.5 points per game. Very achievable at Seoul's current level.
- **Earn a home semi-final (third or fourth):** roughly 57 to 60 points. That is around 1.8 points per game the rest of the way, a real step up from the first-half pace but not a fantasy.
- **Automatic promotion (second):** roughly 62 to 64 points, which means better than two points per game AND one of the sides above stumbling. This is the stretch goal, not the base case.

The single most useful number: Seoul play eight home games and eight away games in the second round robin, a clean mirror. The playoff push will be won at Mokdong and defended on the road.

## The Run-In, Cluster by Cluster

The sixteen games split into three honest groups.

**The home base (eight games at Mokdong).** Cheonan, Ansan Greeners, Paju Frontier, Suwon Bluewings, Daegu, Gimhae FC, Jeonnam Dragons, Chungnam Asan. Six of those eight are mid-table or worse, and Seoul have already beaten Paju, Gimhae, and Jeonnam once this season. This is where the points target is met or missed. A promotion side wins its home games against the bottom half, full stop.

**The away gauntlet (the swing).** Suwon FC (R18), Busan IPark (R20), Hwaseong FC (R21), Gimpo (R28), Yongin (R31), Gyeongnam (R34). The back-to-back trip to league-leading Busan and in-form Hwaseong in early August is the hardest week of the season. Seoul do not need to win these. They need to not lose all of them, and to steal a result where the opponent is beatable.

**The draw-specialist traps.** Seongnam (R24 away), Chungbuk Cheongju (R25 away), Yongin (R31 away). These three have drawn a combined mountain of games, Cheongju alone have eleven draws in sixteen and already beat Seoul 2-1 at Mokdong in Round 15. Low-event, low-margin matches against organized blocks are exactly where Seoul have leaked points all year. Turning even one of these from a draw into a win could be the automatic-promotion margin.

## The Strategy

Six things decide which version of that projection Seoul get.

### 1. Make Mokdong non-negotiable

Eight home games, six of them against the bottom half. A promotion team does not drop points to Ansan, Paju, Gimhae, or Jeonnam at home, and it beats Cheonan and takes at least a point off Daegu and the Bluewings. The entire playoff push is built on a near-perfect home record. Everything else is a bonus on top of that foundation.

### 2. Survive the August away gauntlet, then cash the swing games

Busan away and Hwaseong away in the same week will hurt; treat a single point from those two as an acceptable outcome and refuse to let the damage spread. The games that actually move the table are the beatable away trips, Gimpo, Yongin, Gyeongnam, plus the return at Suwon FC. Two wins and a draw from that group is the difference between third and fifth.

### 3. Break the draw-specialist low blocks early

Seongnam, Cheongju, and Yongin have made a season out of dragging better teams into scoreless, nervous afternoons, and Cheongju already have Seoul's number. The Round 13 win over Seongnam is the template: score first, force the compact side to come out, and punish the space that opens. Passive, horizontal, patient football against these three is how a promotion campaign quietly bleeds to death. Turn even one of these draws into a win and the auto-promotion math changes.

### 4. Make the back four hold what it was built to hold

Kim Do-gyun (김도균) has declared the back four the identity for the second half of the season, and the run-in is its real exam. Seoul's recurring 2026 flaw is the late, soft goal off a second ball or a cross, the exact way they conceded at Gimhae and the exact way tight away games get lost. A double pivot that screens the center backs and clean rest-defense when the full-backs push are worth more points across sixteen games than any single attacking tweak.

### 5. Own both boxes on set pieces

Attacking set pieces have been a season-long Seoul strength, and Euller's (에울레르) dead-ball delivery is a repeatable source of goals, his direct free-kick against Seongnam being the latest. In low-event matches against organized opponents, and there are several coming, the dead ball is the most reliable way to break a deadlock. Defensively, the set-piece marking that let Chungnam Asan and others score early has to tighten. Set pieces are the cheapest points in the run-in, in both directions.

### 6. Keep the front three healthy and manage the depth

Park Jae-yong (박재용) leads the team on goals and has settled the striker question. Euller is the creative engine and, at fifteen goals and fifteen assists in K League play, the most productive Brazilian in the club's history, though his discipline needs watching after a needless yellow at Gimhae; Seoul cannot afford to lose him to an accumulation or a red in a decisive match. Alan Carius (알랑 카리우스) is finally back and available after ten months out. Behind them the depth is thinner, Osmar's (오스마르) fitness has been managed all season and his set-piece presence matters, and there is an unconfirmed possibility that Byun Kyung-jun could depart for military service around August, which would remove an impact option from the bench. The run-in is sixteen games in five months. Rotation and availability, not just the best eleven, will decide it.

## Bottom Line

Seoul E-Land start the second round robin fifth, inside the playoff zone, and closer to the auto-promotion places than to falling out of the top six. That is a good place to be and a nervous one. The path is not complicated: win at Mokdong like a promotion side, survive the August away gauntlet without a collapse, and stop drawing with the teams built to hold you. Do that and 60 points, a top-four finish, and a home playoff semi-final are all realistic. Fail to break the low blocks and leak the same late goals, and the same total slips to fifth and a road knockout, the exact scenario that ended last season. The margin between those two seasons is three or four results across four months. This preview's call is that Seoul find them, 9-4-3 in the second round robin, third or fourth at the line, and a home tie to play for promotion.
