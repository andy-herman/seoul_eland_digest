## Why This Match Matters

Seoul's run-in was always going to be six matches against clubs below the race, and this is the first of them. The stakes are simple and Kim Do-gyun stated them himself after the Daegu win: the head-to-heads are done, the remaining opponents are all in the bottom half, and in each of the last two seasons the club dropped the points it had to take against exactly this kind of side. Seoul are level with Suwon FC on 48 with a game more played and five goals worse on the tiebreaker. Every point Suwon FC drop from here is worth something to Seoul only if Seoul have already banked their own.

The model has this as the second-easiest of Seoul's six on paper, behind Gimhae, and the away match Seoul are most likely to win. A win lifts the top-two probability from 22 percent to about 33; a draw leaves it around 16; a defeat drops it to 10. It also comes with a scoreboard advantage: Suwon FC play Hwaseong at 14:00, so Seoul will walk out knowing whether the club above them has dropped points.

## Seoul E-Land Squad News

**[[Park Chang-hwan]] is back.** His one-match ban for the eighth yellow was served against Daegu. He returns to a midfield that coped without him, which is a nicer problem than the one Seoul had a fortnight ago.

**Nobody is reported injured.** The Daegu match produced no injury news; [[Lee Ju-hyeok]]'s halftime withdrawal was the planned fitness change Kim Do-gyun had announced beforehand. Three weeks of rest should clear anything minor.

**Cards.** Three Seoul players are one yellow from a one-match ban: [[Baek Ji-woong]] (booked against Ansan, Yongin, Cheonan and Paju), [[Kim Oh-kyu]] (Gimpo, Hwaseong, Paju and Suwon Samsung) and [[Son Hyuk-chan]], whose booking against Daegu was his fourth. A fifth yellow at Gimpo would rule any of them out of Gimhae on October 18, and the count carries into the playoffs. [[Park Chang-hwan]] is on eight, three clear of his next ban at eleven.

**No call-ups.** Seoul have nobody at the Asian Games and no senior internationals, so the break is a full three weeks of training for the whole squad. Kim Do-gyun said the priority is goals, specifically from the substitutes.

**[[Ahn Joo-wan]]** started on the bench against Daegu for the first time since his record brace, came on at halftime and was Seoul's most dangerous player for 45 minutes. The manager's choice between him and Lee Ju-hyeok on the left is the selection question of the week.

## Opponent Scouting Report

**Gimpo FC** are seventh on 35 points after beating Busan 1-0 on September 19, their first home win in six matches and only their second all season at Solteo. Ko Jeong-woon, in his seventh season in charge and the man who took the club up from K3, plays a 3-4-3 built on an immediate counter-press after losing the ball, a compact block, and wing-backs for width. It produces draws: 11 in 25 this season, the second-highest total in the division, and five of nine at home.

**The Luis-shaped hole.** Luis scored 10 of Gimpo's first 26 goals and was sold to Suwon Samsung on August 20, along with defender Kim Kyul. In the four matches since, Gimpo have scored five and conceded seven. The attack now runs through Ruan, a Brazilian with 5 goals who has been sent off twice this season, Park Dong-jin as the aerial focal point, Paulinho and Dizauma off the bench, and Lee Si-heon, who scored the winner against Busan and also scored the winner at Suwon in April.

**The Busan lineup (3-4-3):** Son Jeong-hyeon; Lee In-jae, Kim Tae-han, Kim Dong-min; Kim Min-sik, Kim Do-hyeok, Park Sang-hyun, Lee Hak-min; Kim Min-seok, Park Dong-jin, Lee Si-heon. Park Sang-hyun made his first professional start and assisted the goal. Son Jeong-hyeon, the goalkeeper sent off at Mokdong in May, started ahead of Yun Bo-sang and made the save of the match. Paulinho and Dizauma came on after the hour.

**Home form on the new pitch.** Solteo was fully relaid over the winter and Gimpo played their first thirteen matches of the season away. On the new surface they are 2 wins, 5 draws, 3 losses in ten, with 14 conceded, roughly the reverse of a normal home advantage. Their away record, 6-5-2, is the better one.

**Off the pitch.** The embezzlement case reached the point of an arrest on September 17: an accounts clerk was detained over roughly 8.3 billion won taken since 2023, most of the club's annual budget, spent on property in Japan and imported cars. The K League has a support team resident at the club and says sanctions will follow the police outcome; the city council cut the club's supplementary budget request by 90 percent on September 4; the chief executive resigned in August. The supporters' group issued a statement that the club's future must not disappear. None of this has produced a fire sale beyond Luis, but it is the backdrop to a squad that is playing for its club's survival as an institution rather than for a table position.

**Availability.** No injuries or suspensions were reported after the Busan match, and Gimpo have no cup fixture in the break. Accumulated yellow-card counts are not published in any readable source; assume a full squad.

## How Seoul E-Land Match Up

Seoul have not lost to Gimpo in four meetings: 2-1 at Mokdong in May, 1-0 at Solteo last October (Byun Kyung-jun in stoppage time), 1-1 at Mokdong in August 2025, 2-1 at Solteo in May 2025. Gimpo have had a player sent off in each of the last two, including two in stoppage time at Mokdong when Ruan and Son Jeong-hyeon both went. The last three visits to Solteo have all produced Seoul wins.

The matchup is between Gimpo's counter-press and Seoul's build-up. Ko's teams win the ball back high and go straight to the wing-backs; Seoul's goalkeeper build-up has been the identifiable weak point in both September matches, the miskick against Suwon and the early nerves against Daegu. If Seoul play through it cleanly, Gimpo's block will sit at the edge of the box for 90 minutes and Seoul will face the Suwon Samsung problem of crosses into a crowd. If they do not, Gimpo will get the two or three counters a match they need.

The thing Seoul have that Gimpo do not is a way through a low block: Ahn Joo-wan taking players on, Euller cutting inside from the right, Park Jae-yong on the move rather than as a target. That is exactly what was missing against Suwon and present against Daegu.

## Strategy That Can Work

Score first and Gimpo have to come out, which is the one thing their shape is not built for. Their two losses at home in the last month both came after conceding first. So the plan writes itself: patient build-up with the goalkeeper taking no risks, Ahn Joo-wan and Euller isolated against the wing-backs, and a real effort to win the match by two rather than one. The tiebreaker with Suwon FC is goals scored. A 1-0 at Solteo is three points; a 3-0 is three points and a fifth of the gap.

## Keys to a Seoul E-Land Win

### 1. Do not give the ball away in the first third

Both of Gimpo's home wins this season came from turnovers in the opponent's half. Seoul's build-up has been shaky in September. This is the match to be boring for the first twenty minutes.

### 2. Get the ball wide and take people on

Gimpo's back three is comfortable against crosses and uncomfortable against dribblers. Ahn Joo-wan's second half against Daegu is the template.

### 3. Keep going to the end

Gimpo score late and concede late. Nine goals after the 80th minute on their side of the ledger this season, and Seoul's own late goals at Cheongju and against Daegu were the difference in both. Whichever side is fitter in the last ten minutes after three weeks off will decide a close match.

## Bottom Line

The first of six matches Seoul are supposed to win, against a side in institutional crisis that has one win in ten at home and no Luis, on a holiday afternoon with the Suwon FC result already on the board. The model says Seoul by a clear margin and so does the recent history. The only way this goes wrong is the way it went wrong in the last two seasons: a slow start, a turnover, a goal conceded, and a low block that Seoul cannot open. Kim Do-gyun has named that risk himself, which is the best sign that the squad has heard it.

**Prediction: Gimpo 0-2 Seoul E-Land.** One from Ahn Joo-wan beating a wing-back, one late from a substitute, because the manager has asked for exactly that.
