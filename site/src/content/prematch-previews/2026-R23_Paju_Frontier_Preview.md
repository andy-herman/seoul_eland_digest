## Why This Match Matters

Seoul E-Land are one point off the top of K League 2 and it is the tightest the division has been all season. Suwon Samsung Bluewings lead on 41 after drawing the Suwon derby. Seoul are second on 40. Daegu are third on 38 and winning again. Suwon FC are fourth on 37 with a game in hand and the best goal difference in the league. Busan are fifth on 37, Hwaseong sixth on 36. Six clubs are covered by five points and two automatic promotion places.

In that context a home fixture against tenth is not a routine three points, it is the kind of match that decides whether Seoul are still in the top two in October. They have taken 40 points from 21 and are unbeaten in six. They have also lost three times at [[Mokdong Stadium]] this season, more than they have lost on the road all year, and Paju will arrive with every reason to defend.

## Seoul E-Land Squad News

**[[Park Chang-hwan]] is available and fresh on his card count.** He served the Round 21 suspension, returned for Round 22, was marked for his 100th appearance before kickoff, and was booked during it. One yellow does not put him at risk again.

**[[Cho Jun-hyun]] remains the open question.** He came off nine minutes after an aerial collision at Hwaseong in Round 21 and did not appear in Round 22. No medical update has been published, and his absence from the team sheet is consistent with an injury without confirming one.

**Euller is in the best form of his Seoul career.** Eight league goals and four assists, three of those goals in one night, and the first hat-trick of his K League career behind him.

**Osmar is being managed, not rested.** Round 22 moved him from centre-back into the deepest midfield role with [[Kim Oh-kyu]] coming into the back four. At thirty-seven that is a smarter answer to the workload question than dropping him.

**Byun Kyung-jun is gone**, and it matters specifically here: he scored twice in the reverse fixture at Paju.

## Opponent Scouting Report

Paju Frontier are in **their first ever season of professional football**, and that context is the whole scouting report.

The club was founded in 2012 as Paju Citizens FC, a social cooperative, worked its way up through the K3 and K4 pyramid, won the K4 title in 2020, and was finally approved for K League 2 in January 2026. They rebranded to Paju Frontier for the jump. They are one of three clubs that entered this season alongside Yongin and Gimhae, expanding the division to seventeen.

For a first-year cooperative club, tenth on 24 points is a decent return. They are not in the relegation fight and they are not in the promotion race, which makes them exactly the sort of opponent that can play without fear.

**Gerard Nus (제라드 누스)** is the manager, a 41-year-old Spaniard with previous Korean experience on the Jeonnam staff, and he is not a pragmatist by instinct: his sides are built to keep the ball and attack. After a rare win in Round 17 he talked about his players being rewarded for long-standing effort; after a home defeat in a poor run he called it part of the growth process. That is a coach with a project rather than a survival plan.

**Borja Baston (보르하 바스톤) is the reason to pay attention.** The Spanish striker signed in March 2026 and has seven goals in nineteen appearances, which is most of Paju's attacking output. His career is genuinely unusual for this division: Atletico Madrid academy and first team, twenty-five goals for Eibar as the Segunda top scorer in 2014-15, Premier League football with Swansea City, a spell at Stoke, and the Golden Boot at the 2009 Under-17 World Cup. He is thirty-two or thirty-three now, physical, experienced, and composed in the box.

Around him, **Yoo Jae-jun (유재준)** has two goals and three assists and is a direct dribbler. He is also the player who scored against Seoul in April. **Arias**, a new foreign signing, scored on debut in Round 17 and set up Borja in the same match. **Ryu Won-woo (류원우)** is a thirty-six-year-old goalkeeper who started that Round 17 move with a long distribution.

Form is volatile: won at Hwaseong in Round 17, then lost four in a row, then beat Seongnam 2-1 at home in Round 22. Before Round 17 they went seven without a win. This is a team that strings results together in neither direction for long.

## How Seoul E-Land Match Up

The April meeting is instructive and slightly misleading. Seoul won 3-1 at Paju Stadium having gone behind to a Yoo Jae-jun solo goal on nineteen minutes, equalised through [[Baek Ji-woong]] before half-time, and then won it late with two goals from Byun Kyung-jun, **both assisted by Euller**.

Two of those three goals came from a player who is now at Gimcheon Sangmu. What survives is the supply: Euller creating from wide areas. What has changed is that Euller is now also the one finishing.

Kim Do-gyun's own read on that match was that Seoul conceded early and that the forwards lacked sharpness in the first half. That is close to a description of the Ansan match as well, and the pattern is worth naming: this team starts slowly at home and needs a first goal to settle.

## Strategy That Can Work

**Cut off Borja Baston and Paju's attack shrinks.** Seven of their twenty-one goals are his and there is no second consistent source. [[Kim Oh-kyu]] and [[Baek Ji-woong]] are a physical pairing and this is the fixture for it.

**Do not let them keep the ball.** Nus wants possession, which is unusual for a bottom-half side at Mokdong. That is an opportunity rather than a threat: a team that tries to play out is a team that can be pressed, and it means Seoul may not face the ten-behind-the-ball problem they have complained about for a month.

**Score first.** Seoul have gone behind at home twice in recent weeks and recovered both times, which is a credit to them and not a plan. Against a side with Paju's form volatility, an early goal is likely to end the contest.

**Keep using set pieces.** Three of the last three goals came from them. Paju have conceded twenty-six.

## Keys to a Seoul E-Land Win

### 1. Start properly

Two of the last three home matches have begun with Seoul conceding or being second best for twenty minutes. Tenth-placed opposition and a promotion race one point tight is the wrong week for another slow start.

### 2. Give Ahn Joo-wan the same licence

He started against a low block and immediately produced the best chance of the half. If Paju sit deeper than Nus would like, that run is again the most valuable thing Seoul have.

### 3. Do not become a one-man team

Euller has scored eight and set up four. That is wonderful and it is also a dependency. [[Park Jae-yong]], [[Alan Carius]] and [[Baek Ji-woong]] all need to be threats, because the run-in will eventually include a match where Euller is marked out of it.

## Things to Watch

- **[[Park Jae-yong]] or [[Kim Hyun]]** at centre-forward.
