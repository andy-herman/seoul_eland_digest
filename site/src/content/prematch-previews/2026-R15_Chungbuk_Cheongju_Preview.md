## Strategy That Can Work

The plan against Cheongju is not complicated, but it has to be deliberate. This is the league's most stubborn draw side, a team that has turned the low-event stalemate into an identity, so the job is to break the game open early without handing them the transition moments that are their only real route to a point.

- Score first and score early. Cheongju's entire 2026 identity is the low-event draw, so the faster Seoul make them chase the game, the more they have to abandon the compact block that has earned all those stalemates.
- Patience over panic against a packed box. Expect a deep, narrow shell; switches of play, overlap timing, and cutbacks to pull the block apart are worth far more than hopeful early crosses.
- Win the set-piece battle at both ends. With Cheongju's attack toothless and goals scarce, a single dead ball can be the difference, and Seoul's set-piece strength is a logical edge.
- Respect the counter, not the possession. The danger is not Cheongju keeping the ball; it is Seoul over-committing and getting caught by Garcia or Lee Jong-eon, so clean rest-defense behind the wingbacks is essential.
- Manage the pre-break game state. This is the last match before the World Cup break and it is at home, so once ahead Seoul can game-manage and protect legs rather than force the issue.

## Keys to a Seoul E-Land Win

### 1. Protect the first pass after turnovers

This is the clearest tactical key. Seoul can live with sterile Chungbuk Cheongju possession, but they cannot let their own attacks become Chungbuk Cheongju counters. The center backs and holding midfielders need clean rest-defense spacing whenever the wingbacks or wide forwards push high.

Chungbuk Cheongju's transition block is easier to manage if Seoul force it to move first. Switches of play, overlap timing, and cutbacks should be more valuable than early floated balls. The goal is to make Chungbuk Cheongju defend the second and third pass, not just the first delivery.

### 3. Treat set pieces like a swing factor

Garcia's aerial threat makes defensive restarts important, but Seoul should also see dead balls as a route to control. A clean set-piece game would reduce Chungbuk Cheongju's emotional lift and give Seoul a direct way to turn pressure into chances.
