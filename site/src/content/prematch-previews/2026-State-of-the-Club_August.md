# State of the Club: Seoul E-Land After Twenty Matches

## The Short Version

Seoul E-Land FC have 37 points from 20 matches, they are unbeaten in five, and they are second in K League 2 in August. They are also a team that has lost more matches at home than away, cannot break down a side that sits in, and just spent ninety minutes at Hwaseong being rescued by their goalkeeper. They took second place, incidentally, in a match where they did not score.

Both of those paragraphs are true. This is an attempt to explain how.

## Where They Actually Are

The club has never played in K League 1. Not once. Every season since they entered the league has ended somewhere between mid-table and mildly disappointing, and the 2025 campaign ended with a promotion playoff exit to Seongnam that still gets brought up in the stands. So the number that matters is not really 37 points. It is this: with twelve matches left, Seoul E-Land are close enough to automatic promotion to talk about it without embarrassment.

## What They Are Doing Well

### They are a road team, and nobody expected that

Here is the single strangest fact about Seoul E-Land's season.

| | Played | Record | Points | Per game |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home | 9 | 5W, 1D, 3L | 16 | 1.78 |
| **Away** | **11** | **6W, 3D, 2L** | **21** | **1.91** |

They have lost three times at [[Mokdong Stadium]] and twice on the road all season. Their two biggest results of the year, the comeback win at second-placed Busan IPark and the point at Hwaseong, both came away from home in the same week. Whatever it is that makes a team travel well, this squad has it, and it is the opposite of the usual story about a club with a passionate home support.

### Euller has become the best import in the club's history

Euller arrived from Marítimo in January 2025 with no Korean football behind him. He now has 15 goals and 15 assists in K League play, every single one of them in a Seoul E-Land shirt, and he reached the 50-appearance mark by scoring in the game itself. He is closing on a double-double for the season. When he said he wanted to be remembered in the club's history, it read like the sort of thing every foreign signing says. It has stopped reading that way.

### The spine holds

[[Min Sung-jun]] has now won Seoul points on his own twice in one month, most obviously with the full-stretch save at Hwaseong that turned a defeat into a draw. Osmar is thirty-seven years old and completed three full ninety-minute performances in eleven days without anyone deciding to rest him. [[Alan Carius]] came back from injury and immediately produced a goal and two assists to win Player of the Round. [[Park Jae-yong]], who described his move here as a last chance, leads the team's scoring.

### They finally closed a game out

For most of this season the criticism wrote itself: Seoul E-Land built leads and gave them away. Then they went to Busan, went behind to the division's best attack, equalised through [[Baek Ji-woong]], won it late through [[Park Jae-yong]], and defended a one-goal lead with a back five for the last five minutes. That is a different team from the one that drew 2-2 at Suwon FC three weeks earlier. Whether it holds is the season's real question, but it happened once, and it had not happened before.

## What to Watch For

### There is no plan B against a low block

This is the flaw, and Hwaseong exposed it completely. Seoul had roughly sixty per cent of the ball in the first half and produced one shot worth remembering. Give this team space and they are one of the most dangerous sides in the division. Ask them to break down ten men behind the ball and they pass sideways until something goes wrong.

The Korean broadcast produced the best description of it anyone has managed. Seoul, the co-commentator said, were making calls that nobody was picking up. The passes were available. The runs were not being made.

### Nobody runs in behind any more

Byun Kyung-jun enlisted for military service on August 3, one day after setting up the winner at Busan. He was Seoul's most reliable source of pace off the bench, and eight days later, at Hwaseong, the hole he left was visible to anyone watching.

The exception was [[Ahn Joo-wan]]. The seventeen-year-old came on, ran in behind immediately, got there, and finished it. The flag went up for offside by inches. He was the only player on the pitch all night who tried the thing that would have won the match, and he is not yet a starter.

### Seven of the last twelve are at Mokdong

This is where the two halves of this article collide.

**Remaining home:** Ansan Greeners, Paju Frontier, Suwon Bluewings, Daegu, Gimhae FC, Jeonnam Dragons, Chungnam Asan
**Remaining away:** Seongnam FC, Chungbuk Cheongju, Gimpo Citizen, Yongin, Gyeongnam

Seven of the last twelve matches are at the ground where this team has been least convincing, and several of them are exactly the fixture Seoul struggle with most: a mid-table or bottom-half side coming to Mokdong with no intention of opening up. Ansan on August 16 is the first of them. If Seoul have found an answer to the low block, the run-in is very winnable. If they have not, the schedule is going to keep asking the same question until they do.

### Osmar is thirty-seven

He has been outstanding and he has played almost everything. The cover is better than it looks: [[Kim Oh-kyu]], the club captain, has 20 appearances and 1,681 minutes of his own, level with [[Min Sung-jun]] for the most in the squad, and has simply been the one dropping to the bench during this run. At some point in the next twelve matches, either Seoul manage that workload deliberately or it manages itself at a moment of its own choosing.

## The Fun Part

A few things about this club that have nothing to do with the table.

**The Brazilian pathway is real.** Euller, [[Alan Carius]] and [[Francisco Geraldes]] give Seoul one of the more entertaining foreign contingents in the division, which is why this site publishes a full Portuguese edition. There is a genuine Brazilian readership following a Korean second-division club, and that is a delightfully modern thing to exist.

**Mokdong is small and it matters.** Seoul E-Land play in the shadow of FC Seoul, a club with roughly a hundred times the profile, in a city that has largely decided which team it supports. The crowds are modest. The away following at Busan for the biggest win of the season was a couple of thousand in a twelve-thousand-seat ground, and [[Park Jae-yong]] made a point of thanking them.

**The Korea Cup went badly and it is worth remembering.** Seoul lost 4-2 after extra time to Ulsan Citizen, a K3 side, in July. This site predicted a comfortable win and left the prediction visible rather than quietly editing it. It remains the single most embarrassing ninety, sorry, one hundred and twenty minutes of the season, and it also cleared the calendar for the run-in.

## The Read

Seoul E-Land are good. Not great, not lucky, genuinely good: a top-two goal difference, a goalkeeper in form, the division's most productive foreign player, and an away record that would win most promotion races on its own.

They are also one tactical problem away from throwing it away, and that problem is going to be sitting in a compact 4-4-2 at Mokdong roughly every other week from now until November.

Twelve matches. Seven at home. The season is entirely in front of them, which is both the good news and the warning.
