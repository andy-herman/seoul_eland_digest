## Why This Match Matters

A week ago this was the second of two home matches that could decide the title. The first was lost, the title is eight points away with seven to play, and this one has become something narrower and, for Seoul, more urgent: the last match on the schedule in which Seoul can take points directly off a rival for second place.

The arithmetic is simple. Daegu are second on 46, Seoul fourth on 45, Suwon FC third on 45 with a game in hand and more goals scored. A Seoul win puts Seoul on 48, Daegu stay on 46, and Seoul go into the break second regardless of what Suwon FC do at Jeonnam earlier in the day. A draw leaves everyone where they are, which with Suwon FC's game in hand is a slow loss. A Daegu win puts them four clear of Seoul with six to play, and Seoul's promotion hopes would then rest on Suwon FC and Daegu both dropping points against clubs in the bottom half, which Suwon FC have done twice in a row and Daegu have not done in six matches.

The model's numbers make the same point. Seoul's chance of an automatic promotion place is 21 percent going in. Win and it is 35 percent. Lose and it is 7. The Daegu Ilbo called it the watershed for staying in the top two; Park Jae-yong, who makes his 100th K League appearance, called it a war.

## Seoul E-Land Squad News

**[[Park Chang-hwan]] is suspended.** His 41st-minute foul on Luis last Saturday was his eighth yellow card of the season, which under the five-then-every-third rule brings his second one-match ban of the year. He is the captain, the midfield's ball-winner and its most-used passer, and there is no like-for-like replacement in the squad. The Geraldes trade in August left central midfield as the thinnest position on the roster.

**[[Baek Ji-woong]] went off injured on 78 minutes** against Suwon and nothing has been published on the severity since. He and Osmar have been the first-choice center-back pairing since Cheongju. If he is unavailable, [[Kim Oh-kyu]], the club captain who has been on the bench for two matches, comes straight back in.

**Everyone else is available.** No further injuries or suspensions were reported in the week. [[Ahn Joo-wan]] was withdrawn at halftime against Suwon for his state of mind rather than his fitness, and said afterward that being booed by the Suwon end was a first experience he expects to get used to. Euller, [[Alan Carius]], Gabriel and [[Park Jae-yong]] all played on Saturday.

**Nobody else is one card from a ban.** Baek Ji-woong is on four yellows and Kim Oh-kyu, booked in stoppage time on Saturday, is on four; a booking for either on Saturday would cost them the Gimpo match on October 9, not this one.

### Why this shape

Kim Do-gyun has used the 4-3-3 with Carius, Yoon Seok-ju and Park Chang-hwan as the three for every big match since July, and the rest of the eleven has not changed in three weeks. ## Opponent Scouting Report

**Daegu FC**, relegated from K League 1 last December after nine seasons, are second on 46 points with the joint-best attack in the division (47 goals) and a defense that has tightened sharply under Choi Sung-yong, who replaced Kim Byung-soo after Round 8. His record since taking over is 10 wins, 5 draws and 2 losses. They are six unbeaten (4 wins, 2 draws), with 8 scored and 3 conceded in the last five, and they beat Yongin 3-1 last Saturday with two goals from Deckers and one from Seraphim, who came off the bench, scored, assisted and was named the round's MVP.

**Shape.** A 3-4-3 that becomes 3-5-2 in possession, with Cesinha dropping between the lines and the wing-backs pushing on. Choi described the base pattern after the Yongin match: Hwang Jae-won inverting from right wing-back into midfield and Kim Dae-woo advancing into the number eight role. Both were suspended for that match, and both are back for this one, so expect the base version rather than the improvised one.

**Expected eleven (our read, not published):** Han Tae-hee; Kim Kang-san, Hwang In-taek, Hong Jae-seok; Hwang Jae-won, Han Kook-young, Kim Dae-woo, Choi Kang-min; Deckers, Cesinha, Kim Ju-gong. Seraphim has been the impact substitute in each of the last two matches and has scored or assisted in both; the pattern says bench again, but he has 11 assists this season and a start is possible.

**Missing.** Edgar, their eight-goal striker, has been out with a calf injury since early September with no return date. Kim Hyung-jin, the center-back, has not played since a head collision on August 22. Neither was mentioned in any of the week's previews.

**The danger men.** Deckers is tall, aerial, and has scored three in his last two, including a stoppage-time winner at Gyeongnam and a stoppage-time equalizer against Busan. Seraphim (8 goals, 11 assists) is the supply from the right. Cesinha, 36, is the captain and the reason the local press call Daegu a one-man attack when he is quiet: they have not won a match this season in which he neither scored nor assisted, and he assisted the third goal last Saturday.

**The weakness.** Daegu's away record against the current top six this season reads lost, drew, drew, lost (at Busan, Suwon FC, Suwon Samsung and Hwaseong) with no wins, and the Hwaseong match was a 1-5. They have beaten a current top-six side once all season, 1-0 against Hwaseong on opening day. Choi spent this week's training on defending counters after the Yongin goal exposed the space behind his wing-backs, and told the Maeil Shinmun that "small mistakes turn into goals conceded" in matches like this.

## How Seoul E-Land Match Up

The March match at Daegu was the template and the warning. Seoul won 3-1 with Kang Hyeon-je, Aidale and Baek Ji-woong scoring, Daegu had an Edgar header ruled out by VAR and a penalty award overturned, and Cesinha was injured. Kang Hyeon-je and Aidale have since left, Daegu have since changed manager and Cesinha is fit. The lesson that survives is the structural one: Daegu's wing-backs push high in possession and the space behind them is where Seoul scored twice.

The matchup problems are in the air and in the middle. In the air, Deckers against Osmar and whoever partners him; Seoul have conceded from set pieces in two of their last three matches and Daegu's supply from Seraphim is the best in the league. In the middle, Cesinha dropping into the pocket between Seoul's midfield and defense, which on Saturday is a midfield without Park Chang-hwan. Yoon Seok-ju and Cho Jun-hyun will have to decide who follows him and who holds, and get it right for ninety minutes.

Where Seoul are better is in the transition the other way. Euller and Ahn Joo-wan against Choi Kang-min and Hwang Jae-won, both of whom Choi has told to run "more directly, higher up", is the mismatch Seoul want. Daegu conceded to exactly that at Yongin.

## Strategy That Can Work

Seoul's 18 shots against Suwon were mostly crosses into a low block. Daegu do not sit in. They press from a back three and commit wing-backs, which means the chances will be fewer but better, and the first goal will matter even more than it did last week. A Seoul side that scores first against a Daegu team that has to chase is a Seoul side that gets to play the counter-attack that won in March. A Seoul side that concedes first is playing a Daegu side that is very good at protecting a lead and at scoring late.

So: defend the first fifteen minutes properly, which means marking Deckers at corners with a body rather than a zone, and then attack the space behind the wing-backs with the two quickest players on the pitch.

## Keys to a Seoul E-Land Win

### 1. Do not concede from a dead ball

Three of the last four goals Seoul have conceded came from corners or free kicks. Daegu's striker is two meters tall and their best player delivers set pieces. This is the whole first half.

### 2. Make Cesinha's marker a decision, not an accident

Without Park Chang-hwan, somebody has to own the space Cesinha drops into. Whoever it is, it should be decided before kickoff and not discovered on 20 minutes.

### 3. Get Ahn Joo-wan and Euller running at Daegu's wing-backs

The one thing Daegu cannot defend is the ball played into the space their own shape leaves. Seoul have the two wingers to exploit it and a striker on 99 appearances who has hit the post and the bar in consecutive matches.

## Bottom Line

Seoul E-Land's season comes down to this before the break. Win and they are second going into three weeks of rest, with the run-in the calendar always promised: five of six against clubs currently ninth or lower. Lose and they are four points off second with Suwon FC's game in hand still to come, chasing two clubs whose remaining fixtures are no harder than Seoul's own. The model makes it a Seoul edge of about ten percentage points, which is another way of saying it will be decided by which side defends its first corner.

**Prediction: Seoul E-Land 2-1 Daegu.** One from a cross that Daegu's wing-backs are not there to stop, one from Deckers that Seoul should have defended better, and a second Seoul goal late, because both of these teams score late.
