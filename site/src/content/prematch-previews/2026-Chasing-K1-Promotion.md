## What Changed This Week

Seoul E-Land beat Ansan 3-1 and **cut the gap to first from three points to one**, without needing to do anything clever: Suwon Samsung drew the Suwon derby against Suwon FC, so the leaders and the fourth-placed side both dropped two.

Daegu were the other winners, beating Chungnam Asan 2-0 to move third. Busan drew 0-0 at Hwaseong, which suited neither: Busan have now taken two points from their last three and have slipped to fifth.

## The Fixture Lists Are Not Equal, and It Is Not Close

This is the part the league table cannot show you. Every club above has ten to twelve matches left, but they are wildly different matches.

**Hwaseong FC have one home game left. One.** Ten of their remaining eleven are away from home. That is not a quirk of a bad week, it is the shape of their entire run-in, and it is why the model has them finishing sixth despite sitting six points off the top today. Cha Du-ri's side have been the story of the division for two months. The calendar is about to make them earn it on the road.

**Suwon FC have eight home games from twelve, plus a match in hand and the best goal difference in the league.** On paper they have the friendliest run-in of anyone in this table by a distance. They are fourth today. The model has them finishing level with Seoul on 60. If there is a club to be nervous about, it is this one, not the two above them.

**Seoul E-Land have six home and five away**, which is roughly neutral, and only two fixtures against the other five contenders.

## What Seoul Actually Need

The model projects Seoul to finish on **60 points**, which is second, and also projects Suwon FC to finish on 60. It has Suwon Samsung on 61. The whole thing sits inside the margin of a single result.

Turned into something you can hold on to: Seoul are averaging 1.90 points per game. Maintaining exactly that over eleven matches gives them 61. **Roughly two points a game keeps them in the top two, and anything below 1.7 probably does not.**

## The Case For and Against Each Rival

**Suwon Samsung Bluewings (41).** Best defence in the division, 19 conceded. Three of their remaining eleven are against this group, including a trip to Mokdong. The worry for them: they have drawn five and their attack, on 31 goals, is the weakest of the top four.

**Suwon FC (37, one game in hand).** The genuine threat. Eight home matches left, 41 goals scored, +15 goal difference, unbeaten in seven going into the derby. Everything about their profile says they finish higher than fourth.

**Daegu FC (38).** Won again this week and have the joint-best attack on 41. But they play three of the top six, including trips that Seoul do not have to make, and they concede at a rate nobody else in this table does: 30 against.

**Busan IPark (37).** Fading. Two points from three matches, five losses in eleven since Seoul beat them, and a goal difference of +8 that is the weakest here. Still close enough that a run puts them right back in it.

**Hwaseong FC (36).** See above. The football has been excellent and the fixture list is brutal. One home match in eleven is close to a disqualifier unless they are extraordinary travellers.

## Method

Positions and records are the completed Round 22 table. Remaining fixtures and venues are validated against the K League's own published schedule, so the home and away splits above are the real ones rather than an assumption. Projections come from the same fixture-by-fixture model that drives the [Tracker](/tracker): each club is rated on points per game, home advantage is a fixed adjustment, and every remaining match is simulated to an expected-points value.

The model does not know about injuries, suspensions, form streaks or the weather. It is a baseline to argue with, not a forecast to trust.
