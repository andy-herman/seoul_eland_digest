## Why This Match Matters

Seoul go into the bye level on 54 points with Suwon FC, who are second because they lead on goals scored, 53 to 52, and who play Chungnam Asan in Round 30 while Seoul rest. Round 29 cut Seoul's deficit on that first tiebreaker from five goals to one, and the model now gives Seoul a 23 percent chance of the top two. A win at Yongin lifts that to about 35 percent. A draw drops it to about 16, a defeat to about 10. Kim Do-gyun's verdict after Gimhae was that Seoul have to win every remaining match for there to be hope. This is the first of the four, and the only one of them away from Mokdong before the last day.

## Seoul E-Land Squad News

**[[Son Hyuk-chan]] is suspended.** His booking against Gimhae was his fifth caution of the season, and under the league's 2026 rules that brings a one-match ban. He came on at halftime against Gimhae and scored the first goal of his career, so Seoul lose a player in form.

**Cards.** [[Baek Ji-woong]] and [[Kim Oh-kyu]] are still on four cautions, one from a ban. [[Oh In-pyo]] is on three and [[Osmar]] on one. [[Park Chang-hwan]], on eight after serving bans at five and eight, is two from his next.

**[[Kim Oh-kyu]]** has not played in either of the last two matches, and the club has not said why.

**[[Bae Seo-jun]]** came off at halftime against Gimhae. No injury has been reported, and the change looked tactical: Son Hyuk-chan went on at left wing-back and scored.

**The bye.** Seoul have a fortnight between matches. Their last bye, in Round 17, was followed by a 2-2 draw at Suwon FC.

## Opponent Scouting Report

**Yongin FC** are in their first season, one of three new clubs alongside Gimhae and Paju that made this a 17-team division. The club held its founding ceremony on January 4, set itself the goal of reaching K League 1 by 2030, and hired a famous face as technical director, Lee Dong-gook, with Kim Jin-hyung as general manager. The manager is Choi Yun-kyum, appointed by the city in July 2025, a veteran of Bucheon, Daejeon, Gangwon, Busan, Jeju and Chungbuk Cheongju. A top-ten finish was described as the realistic target for this first season. They are 13th, and since 42 points is the most they can finish on, against Hwaseong's 44 in sixth, they can no longer reach the playoffs; the race for tenth, with Cheongju, Seongnam and Paju just above them, is what is left.

**Who to watch.** The scoring is shared: Seok Hyun-jun and the Brazilian Gabriel have six league goals each, Choi Chi-woong four, Yoo Dong-gyu and Lee Seung-jun three. Kim Bo-seop is the creator, with six assists, and he has played all 27 matches. Choi Young-jun has worn the captain's armband in every match since September 5. Goalkeeper Hwang Sung-min was hurt in a collision with Suwon FC's Frizzo on September 5, was in and out of the side after that, and started again against Ansan.

**The pattern.** Yongin draw. Twelve of their 27 matches have finished level, second only to Chungbuk Cheongju's 14, and at Mireu Stadium they have won only twice, 4-1 against Gimhae in April and 2-0 against Busan in August, with seven draws and five defeats. Their recent problem is goals: three in their last five matches, and none in two of their last three at home.

**The Ansan collapse.** Yongin were level 0-0 at halftime at home to Ansan on October 17 and lost 4-0. Kim Bo-seop's back-pass gifted Lima the first on 49, Yongin pushed up to chase it, and Ansan scored three more on the counter, Lima finishing with a hat-trick in stoppage time. A Gabriel header was ruled out for offside. Yongin had 55 percent of the ball and three shots on target to Ansan's ten. Choi Yun-kyum apologised to the home supporters, said the fault was entirely his, and said that the team could not carry on as it was and that change was needed. Expect a different starting eleven from the one Ansan beat.

**Availability.** Lee Gyu-dong, sent off against Gyeongnam in September, is back in the squad. Lim Chae-min has six cautions and a red card this season and Gabriel five cautions; Round 30 at Cheongju could change the disciplinary picture before Seoul arrive.

## How Seoul E-Land Match Up

The only previous meeting is a warning. At Mokdong on May 16, Yongin led through Choi Chi-woong's finish from a corner on 12 minutes, Euller's deflected shot levelled it on 67, Park Jae-yong had a goal ruled out by VAR for handball on 88, Seok Hyun-jun headed Yongin back in front in stoppage time from Kim Bo-seop's cross, and Baek Ji-woong saved a point with a header from Euller's knock-down at a corner in the eighth minute of added time. Seoul needed every minute of it.

Seoul are a different team now. They have won three in a row, scored nine in the last three, and are 8 wins, 4 draws and 2 defeats away from home this season. Yongin conceded on the counter when they had to chase the Ansan match, and from a corner in the last minute against Seoul in May, and those are exactly the routes by which Seoul scored most of their six against Gimhae: an equaliser from a corner, a free kick, and goals once the match had opened up. With Caio (192 centimetres), Osmar (191) and Baek Ji-woong (188) in the back three and Park Jae-yong (191) up front, Seoul have four tall targets for every corner, and Kim Hyun (192) on the bench as a fifth.

The risk is the familiar one. Seoul conceded first against Gimhae, both Gimhae goals came from crosses, and Yongin's creator, Kim Bo-seop, crossed for Seok Hyun-jun's stoppage-time header at Mokdong in May. A side that draws twelve times a season is comfortable keeping a match level, and if it is still 0-0 at the hour, Seoul will have to break down a team with nothing to lose.

## Strategy That Can Work

Start fast and make Yongin chase. Their worst performance of the season came when they had to open up against Ansan, and their manager has promised changes, which usually means a team still finding its shape. Seoul's front three thrive when there is space behind; the first goal creates it. Then keep scoring: every goal is a tiebreaker goal now, and the gap to Suwon FC is one.

## Keys to a Seoul E-Land Win

### 1. Score first

In the reverse fixture Yongin led within 12 minutes, Seoul did not equalise until the 67th, and the point needed a header eight minutes into added time. Against Gimhae they did it again on 20 minutes. Do not do it a third time.

### 2. Defend the crosses

Kim Bo-seop has six assists and set up Yongin's second goal at Mokdong in May with a cross, and both of Gimhae's goals at Mokdong came from crosses. Bae Seo-jun, or whoever plays at left wing-back in Son Hyuk-chan's absence, has the hardest defensive job on the pitch.

### 3. Use the set pieces

Euller's corners and free kicks and the four tall men in the box have become one of Seoul's most reliable sources of goals, and Seoul's stoppage-time equaliser against Yongin in May came from a corner. Against a deep block, dead balls are the shortcut.
