# What Is the Korean Cup? The 2026-27 Bracket, Explained

> The Korean Cup is the one competition where Seoul E-Land can draw a K League 1 giant out of a hat. Here is what it is, how the new two-season format works, and the full 2026-27 bracket, round by round.

## The Basics

The Korean Cup is South Korea's national knockout tournament, open to clubs from the top flight all the way down to the amateur fifth tier. It is run by the Korea Football Association (KFA), and its official 2026 name is the Hana Bank Korea Cup 2026-27. If you still call it the "Korean FA Cup," you are in good company, but that name was retired in 2024; the KFA now brands it simply the Korea Cup.

It is one of the country's oldest competitions in spirit, with a lineage the KFA traces back through the Korean National Football Championship to tournaments first played in the 1920s. This 2026-27 edition is the 31st staging of the modern Cup. Jeonbuk Hyundai Motors and Pohang Steelers share the record with six titles each, and Jeonbuk are the defending champions.

## Why It Matters: A Ticket to Asia

Domestically the Cup sits a rung below the K League in prestige. But it carries a prize the league cannot offer a club outside the very top: the winner qualifies for the AFC Champions League Two, the second tier of Asian club football. For a K League 2 side, that is the gap between a good season and a historic one. A second-division club lifting the Cup would earn continental football, something that has almost never happened in Korean football.

The KFA also invested in the competition for this cycle. The total prize pool rose to roughly 1.1 billion won, video review (VAR) was extended deeper into the knockout rounds, and the entire calendar was rebuilt.

## The Big Change: A Two-Year Cup

The headline structural change for 2026-27 is that the Korean Cup is no longer a single-summer event. It now runs on an autumn-spring calendar spanning two years. The early rounds are played in July and August 2026, then the competition pauses over the winter and resumes with the quarterfinals in May 2027, finishing with the final in June 2027.

The logic is to align with the AFC's continental calendar and to let clubs field stronger sides without the fixture pile-up that used to force heavy rotation. For Seoul E-Land, it means the Cup's opening rounds land squarely in the middle of the K League 2 promotion run-in, a scheduling reality that shapes how hard they can chase it. ## Who Enters, and When

Sixty-three clubs enter the 2026-27 Cup, spanning K League 1 down to the amateur K5. They do not all start together: higher-ranked clubs are seeded into later rounds, so the amateurs and lower divisions fight through the early stages before the professionals arrive.

| Round | Date | Who enters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-Round | Jun 20, 2026 | 8 K5 clubs |
| Round 1 | Jul 4-5, 2026 | 26 K3 and K4 clubs, plus 4 Pre-Round winners |
| Round 2 | Jul 15, 2026 | All 17 K League 2 clubs, plus 15 Round 1 winners |
| Round 3 | Jul 29, 2026 | 8 non-continental K League 1 clubs, plus 16 Round 2 winners |
| Round of 16 | Aug 19, 2026 | 4 AFC-qualified K League 1 clubs, plus 12 Round 3 winners |
| Quarterfinals | May 19, 2027 | 8 clubs |
| Semifinals | May 26, 2027 | 4 clubs |
| Final | Jun 5, 2027 | 2 clubs |

K League 2 clubs, Seoul E-Land included, enter at Round 2 on July 15. The K League 1 clubs come in later still: the eight sides outside Asian competition enter at Round 3, and the four continental heavyweights are held back all the way to the Round of 16. Every tie is a single-leg knockout, with extra time and penalties if level after 90 minutes and five substitutions allowed.

## The 2026-27 Bracket, Round by Round

### Round 1 (July 4-5): the amateur qualifiers, done

Fifteen K3 and K4 clubs came through Round 1 to reach the stage where the K League 2 clubs enter. The one result that matters for Seoul E-Land: Ulsan Citizen FC, a K3 side, beat Chuncheon Citizen 2-1 to earn a home tie against E-Land. (Note that Ulsan Citizen are a semi-professional K3 club and have nothing to do with Ulsan HD, the K League 1 power that appears later in this bracket.)

### Round 2 (July 15): the K League 2 clubs arrive

All 17 K League 2 clubs enter here, most drawn against a Round 1 survivor from the lower divisions, with a couple of all-K2 ties where the numbers do not divide evenly. A selection of the confirmed pairings:

The lower-division side hosts, which is why Seoul E-Land travel to Ulsan Citizen rather than the other way around. The remaining ties, including league leaders Busan IPark's, complete the 16-match round.

### Round 3 (July 29): the top flight joins

The eight K League 1 clubs outside Asian competition enter here and are drawn against Round 2 winners. The marquee tie of the round, if both come through, is FC Seoul against Busan IPark, the current leaders of K League 1 and K League 2 respectively. Seoul E-Land's reward for beating Ulsan Citizen would be a meeting with Ulsan HD, one of Korea's traditional powers and a genuine step up in class.

### Round of 16 (August 19): the giants drop in

The four clubs that qualified for the AFC Champions League Elite, Jeonbuk Hyundai Motors, Daejeon Hana Citizen, Pohang Steelers, and Gangwon, are seeded straight into the last 16. This is the stage where a lower-tier run usually meets its end, and where the Cup's most famous upsets are made.

### Quarterfinals to Final (May-June 2027)

The Cup pauses over the winter and resumes in spring. From the quarterfinals onward there is an open re-draw, so the path is no longer fixed once eight clubs remain. The quarterfinals are on May 19, the semifinals on May 26, and the final, a single match, on June 5, 2027.

## Where This Leaves Seoul E-Land

Seoul E-Land begin at Round 2 against K3 Ulsan Citizen on July 15, a tie a full-time professional club is expected to win. Beyond that, the climb steepens quickly: Ulsan HD in Round 3, then potentially one of the continental giants in the Round of 16.
