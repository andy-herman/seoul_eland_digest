Seoul E-Land home matches are one of the most accessible football experiences in Seoul: a compact ground ten minutes from a subway station, tickets that rarely sell out, and a club that has spent 2026 actively making the place nicer. Here is everything you need for a first visit to [[Mokdong Stadium]], which the club brands as Leoul Park.

## The ground

Mokdong Stadium's main stadium sits inside the Mokdong sports complex in Yangcheon-gu, southwest Seoul, alongside a baseball stadium and an ice rink, on the bank of the Anyangcheon stream. It opened in 1989 and holds 15,511 seats in its full configuration; for football the club concentrates fans into a smaller setup with movable seating brought closer to the pitch, since a running track circles the field. Expect an open-air experience: roof coverage is minimal, so dress for the weather, and the main (west) stand is the best-protected side.

The club has invested in the ground for 2026: brighter floodlights upgraded to continental standard, turf work, a new entry arch reading "THIS IS LEOUL PARK," photo zones, a garden rest area outside the east gate, and a rebuilt north exit to ease post-match traffic. Fun footnote: the ground is good enough that FC Seoul borrowed it for AFC Champions League matches in early 2026 when their own pitch froze.

## Getting there

Take **Line 5 to Omokgyo Station**, whose official secondary name is literally "Mokdong Stadium." From exits 3 or 4 it is about a ten minute walk. That is the whole story for most visitors, and it is the best story: the Omokgyo intersection is congested on matchdays and parking at the complex is limited, so the subway wins. If you are the scenic type, the stadium connects to the Anyangcheon riverside bike paths, and cycling in along the stream is genuinely pleasant in spring and autumn.

## Tickets

Two honest facts about K League 2 tickets: they are cheap, and they are almost never sold out.

- **Prices** in recent seasons have started around 10,000 KRW for general seating (roughly 7 to 8 US dollars), with youth prices about half, premium main-stand seats slightly more, and specialty options like table seats and 4-person picnic seating at higher bundles. Treat those as ballpark figures and check the club's current price sheet.
- **Where to buy:** the club has moved its primary booking to its own website and app, with Ticketlink also listed in league guides. Booking channels have shifted more than once, so check [seoulelandfc.com](https://www.seoulelandfc.com/) for the current link.
- **Visiting from abroad?** Korean ticketing platforms generally require a Korean mobile number for online purchase, which makes advance booking effectively impossible for most tourists. The solution is the league norm: **buy at the stadium box office on matchday.** Every K League club sells at the ground, no ID needed, small processing fee, and E-Land matches have plenty of availability. The booth is near the main southwest entrance.

## Where to sit

- **E (east) stand:** the general-admission home end and the best atmosphere-per-won value. This is where most home fans go, and section E3 is the supporters' zone, where the drum-and-flags group Guncheong (named for the club's ultramarine navy) leads the singing. Sit nearby if you want noise, a little further if you want to watch quietly.
- **W (west/main) stand:** the premium side, with regular reserved seats plus table seats, picnic seating, and VIP options. Best shelter, best halftime amenities.
- **N section:** the away end.
- Behind the south goal you will find the food truck row and the main entrance plaza with the club shop, which does shirt printing.

Inside, there are CU convenience stores in both main stands, beer is sold and allowed at your seat, and the food trucks behind the south goal cover the snack food groups. The club runs family programming at most home matches, from kids' zones to halftime events, and the picnic seats are built for exactly that crowd.

## Make a day of it

The Omokgyo station area is a dense dining district with hundreds of restaurants within a short walk: Korean barbecue, gopchang alleys, pasta, izakaya, and plenty of places for a pre-match beer. Eat near the station, walk over along the stream, and get to the ground early enough to see the leopards warm up. If the crowd feels modest, know that the ceiling is real: the club's record home crowd of 9,527 came against Suwon in 2024, and every promotion-race home match this autumn is a chance to beat it.

## Practical notes

- Gate opening times vary by match; check the club's app or social channels rather than assuming.
- Weekend kickoffs are typically 14:00, 16:30, or 19:30 KST depending on the season; midsummer matches are evening kickoffs.
- The stadium is fully walkable from Omokgyo with no hills, and the complex has step-free routes.

Come once for curiosity, and the promotion race will bring you back.
