New to Seoul E-Land? Here is everything worth knowing about Seoul's other football club, the navy-and-gold Leopards of Mokdong, in one sitting.

## A club born from a department store empire

Seoul E-Land FC was founded on April 14, 2014, when E-Land Group announced it was bringing professional football back to its brand. The K League approved the club that August, and the Leopards kicked off in the second division in 2015.

E-Land itself is one of Korea's more unusual football owners. The group started in 1980 as a tiny clothing shop near Ewha Womans University in Seoul, founded by Park Sung-su, and grew into a conglomerate spanning fashion brands like SPAO and Teenie Weenie, the New Balance Korea license, NC department stores, Ashley restaurants, and Kensington hotels. The football connection is older than the club: E-Land ran a semi-professional team, E-Land Puma, from 1992 until early 1998, when the Asian financial crisis forced the group to fold it. Seoul E-Land is that team's spiritual successor, and when it launched it was the first new corporate-backed K League club in nearly two decades.

Even the name was crowdsourced. Around 3,400 fans voted, and "Seoul E-Land FC" beat out alternatives including "Eastern Seoul FC" and "Seoul Gangnam FC." The first manager was a Scot, Martin Rennie, hired from MLS's Vancouver Whitecaps.

## The identity: leopards, navy, and gold

The crest carries the Han River, Namsan, five stars, and three leopards representing Speed, Stamina, and Skill, three S words to echo the S in Seoul. The colors are deep navy with gold accents, and leopard print has a way of sneaking onto the kits. The mascots are Leoul (Leopard plus Seoul) and Lenyang, and the club brands its Mokdong home as Leoul Park.

Then there is the rivalry that mostly exists in waiting. Seoul has two professional clubs, but FC Seoul play in K League 1 and E-Land have never been promoted, so the two sides have met exactly once: a 2021 Korean FA Cup tie played, fittingly, on E-Land's founding anniversary. E-Land won it 1-0 at Seoul World Cup Stadium. The only Seoul derby ever played belongs to the Leopards, and a league version of it is one of the biggest prizes promotion would bring.

## From Jamsil to Mokdong

E-Land spent their first seven seasons at the cavernous Seoul Olympic Stadium in Jamsil. When the Jamsil area entered its long redevelopment as a sports and convention complex, the club agreed with the city to move west, and since 2022 home has been [[Mokdong Stadium]] in Yangcheon-gu, with the arrangement running through 2031. It is a smaller, tighter home than the Olympic bowl ever was, and the club has steadily invested in it, including brighter floodlights and new fan spaces added for 2026. ## A decade of nearly

The honest version of the sporting history is a lot of pain with the good part maybe arriving now.

- **2015:** A brilliant debut season ends in the cruelest way available. E-Land finish 4th, draw their playoff 3-3 against Suwon FC, and go out because the higher-placed team advances on a draw. Striker Joo Min-kyu scores 25 goals that season and later becomes a South Korea international, the club's most famous alumnus.
- **2016 to 2023:** The wilderness. Two last-place finishes in 2018 and 2019, an 11th place as late as 2023, and a rotating cast of managers.
- **2024:** The breakthrough that was not. A best-ever 3rd place under new manager Kim Do-gyun, a playoff win over Jeonnam Dragons, and then a 2-4 aggregate loss to K League 1 giants Jeonbuk Hyundai Motors in the promotion-relegation playoff.
- **2025:** 4th place and 65 points, the club's best points total, undone by a 0-1 home playoff loss to Seongnam. Brazilian forward [[Euller]] is the team MVP with 12 goals and 11 assists.

Kim Do-gyun, appointed in December 2023, is the central figure of the current era. He has done this before, taking Suwon FC into K League 1 in 2020, and the squad around him has stayed remarkably intact: Euller signed a three-year deal, captain [[Kim Oh-kyu]] and Osmar extended, and Alan Cariús returned.

## Why 2026 might actually be the year

Three reasons this season feels different.

First, the form. As of mid-August 2026 E-Land sit 2nd in K League 2 with 37 points from 20 matches, inside the automatic promotion places in a ferociously tight race. Second, the format. K League 1 expands to 14 teams in 2027, so for 2026 only, both first AND second place go up automatically, and the playoff pool widens to four teams. There have never been more routes up; our guide to the promotion race explains the whole system. Third, the tactical reset. After the World Cup break, Kim Do-gyun publicly retired the three-back system that carried the first half of the season and switched to a back four, a move our [[2026-R16_Seoul_E-Land_Digest|Round 16 digest]] covered in detail, and the team has stayed in the hunt since.

There is also a story for the romantics: An Ju-wan, born on the club's founding date of April 14, debuted in March 2026 as the youngest player in K League 2 history at 16, then in July became the youngest goalscorer in the history of the entire K League. A club still waiting for its first promotion is simultaneously growing its own history.
